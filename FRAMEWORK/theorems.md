## **Theorem 1 — Relational Structure Theorem**

**Statement**

A system’s behavior is fully determined by its relational structure.

---

**Formal Idea**

Let:

- \( S \) = system  
- \( R \) = set of relations  

Then:

\[
S \equiv (E, R)
\]

and all dynamics emerge from \( R \).

---

**Meaning**

- structure precedes dynamics  
- relations define possible motion  
- geometry emerges from relations

## **Theorem 2 — Regime Transition Theorem**

**Statement**

All regime transitions occur through structured regions in state space.

---

**Formal Idea**

\[
A \rightarrow B

\[
A \rightarrow M_{\text{transition}} \rightarrow B
\]

where:

\[
M_{\text{transition}} = \text{transition manifold}
\]

---

**Meaning**

- transitions are geometric processes  
- intermediate states are structured  
- branching occurs within manifolds

# 🧠 NEW — Coherence-Based Theorems

## **Theorem 4 — Coherence Stability Theorem**

**Statement**

A system remains stable if and only if its motion is aligned with the field.

---

**Formal Definition**

Let:

- \( \dot{x} \) = system velocity  
- \( F(x) \) = field direction  

\[
C(x) = \frac{\dot{x} \cdot F(x)}{|\dot{x}| \, |F(x)|}
\]

Then:

\[
C(x) \approx 1 \Rightarrow \text{stable trajectory}
\]

---

**Meaning**

- stability is alignment, not equilibrium  
- motion can be dynamic and still stable  
- coherence replaces static stability criteria  

---

Collapse occurs when coherence drops below a critical threshold.

---

**Formal Condition**

\]

---

**Meaning**

- coherence loss precedes failure

## **Theorem 6 — Directional Flow Theorem**

**Statement**

System dynamics split into opposing directional flows within the field.

---

**Formal Structure**

\[
F = F^{+} \cup F^{0} \cup F^{-}
\]

- \( F^{+} \) = forward field (C > 0)  
- \( F^{-} \) = backward field (C < 0)  
- \( F^{0} \) = interface (C ≈ 0)  

---

**Meaning**

- the field is intrinsically directional  
- motion is partitioned into regimes  
- transitions occur at interface layers
