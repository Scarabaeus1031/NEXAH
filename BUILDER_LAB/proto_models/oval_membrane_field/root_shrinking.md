# Root Shrinking

---

## 🧠 Overview

ROOT_SHRINKING describes a contraction process inside the OVAL_MEMBRANE_FIELD model.

It is not interpreted as simple collapse or disappearance.

Instead, it is understood as a **structured redistribution process** across layered space.

The root region changes because exchange occurs through the membrane field between inner and outer structure.

---

## 🔬 Core Hypothesis

The inner root region does not shrink in isolation.

It contracts because:

- pressure builds across layers
- transfer occurs through the membrane field
- the outer structure acts as counter-space
- the intermediate zone redistributes difference

Thus:

> root shrinking is not pure loss  
> but membrane-mediated reconfiguration

---

## 🧩 Minimal Structure

The shrinking process involves at least three components:

1. **inner root region**  
2. **membrane exchange zone**  
3. **outer shell / field response**

This means the process is not local to the core alone.

It is distributed across nested layers.

---

## 🥚 Layered Analogy

A useful analogy is an egg-like layered structure:

- core / yolk-like region  
- white / transfer medium  
- shell / containment layer  

In this analogy, shrinking occurs because the relation between core and shell is mediated through the middle layer.

The core does not simply “collapse”.

It is altered through exchange.

---

## 🔁 Process Logic

Root shrinking may be described structurally as:

1. the inner region enters a tension state  
2. the membrane field becomes active  
3. transfer channels intensify  
4. pressure redistributes across layers  
5. the root contracts or condenses  
6. the outer structure responds  
7. a new equilibrium, split, or collapse path emerges  

---

## ⚡ Shrinking as Transfer

Shrinking should therefore not be understood as:

- pure reduction
- passive decay
- inward-only motion

Instead, it is:

- transfer-driven
- field-mediated
- asymmetry-producing
- dependent on membrane dynamics

Thus:

> shrinking is a visible consequence of invisible exchange

## 🔻 Why the Root Shrinks

The root shrinks when it can no longer maintain its prior relation to the surrounding structure.

This may happen because:

- membrane pressure increases
- coherence weakens
- transfer becomes asymmetric
- one side dominates the exchange
- the internal structure condenses to remain viable

Thus:

> shrinking is a structural response to imbalance

- interface instability → transfer intensifies
- sustained asymmetry → root contracts

This means:

> shrinking is one possible morphological outcome of coherence loss
