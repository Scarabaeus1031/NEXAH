# NEXAH — Multi-Layer Interaction (Exploratory)

This document captures an **emerging hypothesis** within NEXAH:

> dynamical systems may exhibit behavior consistent with  
> **multiple interacting dynamical layers**

---

## ⚠️ Scope

This is:

- an exploratory hypothesis  
- not a formal model  
- not validated across systems  

It is:

> an attempt to interpret observed structure  
> in terms of layered interaction

---

## 🧭 Context

Current NEXAH models systems as:

- trajectories  
- reconstructed fields  
- stability regions  

However, observations suggest:

> some behaviors cannot be fully explained  
> by a single-layer field representation

---

## 🧩 Core Hypothesis

Instead of:

```text
system → single dynamical layer
```

we consider:

```text
system → multiple interacting dynamical components
```

These components may:

- operate on different scales  
- exhibit distinct dynamics  
- interact through shared constraints  

---

## 🔁 Interaction Hypothesis

> Observed system dynamics may result from  
> **coupling between multiple interacting components**

This could explain:

- delayed responses  
- phase-shifted behavior  
- complex transition structures  

---

## 🧠 Interpretation

The system may behave as if:

> dynamics are generated by the interaction  
> of multiple partially overlapping structures

---

## 🔗 Relation to Field Model

The field model describes:

- geometry  
- density  
- flow  

This hypothesis suggests:

> the observed field may be a **projection of multiple interacting components**

---

## 🔬 Conceptual Parallels

Loosely related to:

- multi-scale systems  
- hierarchical dynamics  
- coupled oscillatory systems  

No direct equivalence is assumed.

---

## ⚠️ Limitations

- no formal definition of “layer”  
- no measurement method  
- not implemented in current pipeline  
- unclear separation between components  

---

## 💡 Working Intuition

> The system may not evolve within a single structure,  
> but within a **superposition of interacting dynamics**

---

## 🚀 Next Questions

- can distinct components be identified?  
- can interaction be quantified?  
- does this improve modeling or control?  

---

## Status

Exploratory  
Conceptual only  
Not yet validated
