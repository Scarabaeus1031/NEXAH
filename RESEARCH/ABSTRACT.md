## 🔑 Central Research Perspective

NEXAH investigates whether transitions in nonlinear systems
are governed not only by instability magnitude,
but by breakdown of coherent structural organization.

More specifically:

```text
transition probability appears to increase when

local phase evolution,
directional transport,
and structural coherence

lose alignment relative to expected motion
inside reconstructed dynamical geometry.
```

The framework further proposes that stabilization
depends not only on phase alignment,
but also on the directional relationship
between control and intrinsic transport structure.

# 🔬 Central Empirical Observation

A core operational result
of the current framework
is that transition activation appears to correlate more strongly
with phase mismatch
than with instability magnitude alone.

Mismatch is operationally defined as:

$$M(t) = |\omega(t)-\hat{\omega}(t)|$$

where:

- $\omega(t)$ = observed phase velocity
- $\hat{\omega}(t)$ = locally expected phase evolution

Empirically observed relation:

```text
high mismatch
⇒ increased transition probability
```

across multiple investigated systems.

# 🌊 Directional Coherence Extension

Recent JANUS experiments suggest
that phase mismatch alone
may not fully explain transition organization.

Directional overlap between local transport structures
appears increasingly relevant.

Directional coherence is reconstructed through:

$$\mathcal{J}(x) = F_{\mathrm{forward}}(x) \odot F_{\mathrm{backward}}(x)$$

with normalized coherence:

$$J(x) = \frac{ \| \mathcal{J}(x) \| }{ \| F_{\mathrm{forward}}(x) \| \, \| F_{\mathrm{backward}}(x) \| +\varepsilon }$$

Exploratory evidence suggests:

```text
transition probability increases
as directional coherence weakens
```

particularly near:

- apertures
- shell crossings
- transport bottlenecks
- recursive orientation boundaries
