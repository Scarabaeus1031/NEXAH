# 🧠 NEXAH — Abstract

NEXAH is a framework for analyzing and navigating transitions  
in complex dynamical systems.

Traditional approaches model systems as sequences of states  
and define stability in terms of thresholds or equilibrium behavior.

NEXAH instead reconstructs systems as **structured dynamical fields**,  
in which motion is constrained by geometry, flow, and transition pathways.

Across multiple systems — including chaotic, discrete, and collective dynamics —  
we observe that transitions are not random events,  
but occur within **spatially organized regions** of the reconstructed field.

A key empirical result is that transition activation is not governed  
by instability magnitude alone, but by **phase mismatch**:

$$
M(t) = |\omega(t) - \hat{\omega}(t)|
$$

where phase velocity deviates from its expected evolution.

We find that:

```text
high mismatch ⇒ high transition probability
```

independent of system type.

This leads to a new control principle:

> control should not act on state magnitude,  
> but on alignment with the underlying phase structure.

NEXAH therefore shifts the perspective from:

```text
state prediction → structure-aware navigation
```

enabling systems to be understood and steered  
through their intrinsic transition geometry.

**Keywords:** dynamical systems, phase dynamics, transitions, control, field structure
