# Candidate Paper Structure

## Working title

**A Typed, Provenance-Preserving Method for Auditing Representation Change**

The NEXAH name can appear as the implementation/corpus name, not as the source of the scientific claim.

## Conventional structure

1. **Problem and scope**  
   Representation-dependent work often collapses source, view, transformation, result, trace, and interpretation. State the bounded methodological problem.

2. **Related methods and novelty boundary**  
   Compare against provenance, workflow, transition-system, type/schema, model-transformation, and human-in-the-loop approaches. State what is ordinary and what combination may be distinctive.

3. **Typed audit model**  
   Define identity/revision, state, representation/view, transformation, comparison criterion, outcomes, uncertainty, trace, provenance, interpretation, knowledge, and Human Authority.

4. **Procedure and stopping rules**  
   Show required records, fail-closed conditions, and how unresolved or undefined outcomes are preserved.

5. **Formal case: WNI-01**  
   Demonstrate criterion-bound invariance and destructive controls without claiming new topology.

6. **Applied case: IEEE Geometry**  
   Demonstrate frozen method/evaluation separation, reproducibility, preserved failures, and the boundary between equivalence and validation.

7. **Negative and boundary cases**  
   Use EXP-00 mixed/non-citable outcomes, NOS metaphor removal, and a brief TITAN external-source control.

8. **Implementation status**  
   Describe RID schema, OLS specification, and narrow ORION baseline; state missing validator/runtime/integration capabilities.

9. **Limitations**  
   No established technical novelty, external usefulness, independent scientific validation, general predictive value, or universal mechanism.

10. **Conclusion**  
    Present the framework as an auditable synthesis and identify exactly what further validation would be needed.

## Venue-family fit

| venue family | fit | mismatch | likely reviewer demand |
|---|---|---|---|
| Research software | MODERATE | Integrated executable machine and external adoption are absent | Installable artifact, conformance execution, software comparison, reuse evidence |
| Scientific workflows | MODERATE–STRONG | No workflow-engine integration or user evaluation | Comparison to workflow and provenance systems |
| Reproducibility / open science | STRONGEST | Novelty over established practice is untested | Prior-art comparison, interoperable record, external use case |
| Knowledge representation | MODERATE | No new logic, ontology evaluation, or reasoning result | Formal semantics, competency questions, comparison to existing models |
| Human-computer interaction | LOW–MODERATE | No user study or demonstrated decision benefit | User task, usability evidence, and Human Authority evaluation |
| Systems / methods | STRONG | No comparative method baseline | Clear procedure, scope, baseline, and bounded evaluation |
| Philosophy of science / methodology | MODERATE–STRONG | Technical artifact detail may dominate; philosophical novelty is untested | Precise epistemic argument and relation to existing methodology |
| Complex systems | LOW | No new dynamics, law, or cross-system mechanism | Stronger scientific model and empirical generalization |
| Domain application | LOW | IEEE and other cases lack operational validation | Domain baselines, real outcomes, calibrated uncertainty, external reproduction |

## Recommended venue direction

First assess reproducibility/provenance, scientific-workflow, and interdisciplinary-methodology venues after the literature review. Do not target complex-systems or formal-methods venues on current evidence.
