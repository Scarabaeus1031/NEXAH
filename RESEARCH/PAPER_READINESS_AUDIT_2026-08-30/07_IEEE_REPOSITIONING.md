# IEEE Geometry Repositioning

`IEEE_ROLE=SECONDARY_CASE_STUDY`

## What exists

- A frozen IEEE-9 method-development package and locked IEEE-14 evaluation.
- Six defined operators and exact manifests.
- IEEE-9: 19 frames, 17 converged, 2 preserved failures at 2.3 and 2.4.
- IEEE-14: all 19 sampled frames converged; no sampled boundary was observed.
- A standard-library replay that matched the frozen artifacts.

## What this supports

IEEE Geometry can show how the audit method:

- preserves a frozen method/evaluation split;
- binds results to exact artifacts and manifests;
- records convergence failures instead of removing them;
- distinguishes method equivalence from scientific validity;
- keeps a representation-derived readout separate from operational interpretation.

## What it does not support

- a validated early-warning or control system;
- operational usefulness;
- calibrated uncertainty;
- external scientific reproduction;
- superiority to classical power-system methods;
- a physical transition law;
- a universal geometry.

The replay establishes internal specification equivalence, not independent scientific validation. IEEE-14 convergence does not locate or prove a physical boundary. The package therefore belongs after the central method, as a bounded applied illustration or transfer challenge, not as the central contribution.

## Paper use

Use one compact figure or table showing:

1. frozen input and operator definitions;
2. locked evaluation;
3. preserved failures;
4. exact limitation language.

Do not lead the paper with IEEE Geometry and do not use it to imply engineering readiness.
