# NEXAH — Multi-Layer Interaction (Exploratory)

This document captures an emerging idea within NEXAH:

> dynamical systems may consist of **multiple interacting regimes**,  
> rather than a single unified layer.

---

## ⚠️ Scope

This is an **exploratory conceptual note**.

It is:

- not a formal theory  
- not validated across systems  
- not a claim of new physics  

It is:

> an attempt to describe observed structure in terms of **layered interaction**

---

## 🧭 Context

NEXAH currently models systems as:

- trajectories  
- reconstructed fields  
- stability landscapes  

During exploration, a recurring pattern appears:

> structure does not seem to emerge from a single level alone

---

## 🧩 Core Idea

Systems may be composed of:

- **multiple regimes / layers**
- each with its own dynamics
- interacting through shared constraints

---

## 🔁 Interaction Hypothesis

> Dynamics emerges from the **interaction between layers**,  
> not from isolated evolution within a single layer.

This suggests:

- coupling between regimes  
- delayed or phase-shifted responses  
- cross-layer influence  

---

## 🧠 Interpretation

Instead of:

- one system → one dynamic  

we consider:

> one system → **multiple coupled dynamical layers**

---

## 🔗 Relation to Field Model

The existing field model describes:

- geometry  
- density  
- flow  
- local structure  

This document extends that view:

> the field itself may encode **multi-layer interactions**

---

## 🔬 Conceptual Parallels

This idea is loosely related to:

- hierarchical systems (biology, computing)  
- top-down / bottom-up interaction  
- regime switching in nonlinear dynamics  

No direct equivalence is claimed.

---

## ⚠️ Limitations

- conceptual only  
- not yet implemented  
- not yet measurable in current pipeline  
- unclear how to separate layers formally  

---

## 💡 Working Intuition

> The system does not move freely in one space.  
> It moves within a **coupled multi-layer constraint structure**.

---

- can coupling be measured?  
- does this improve prediction or control?  
- how does this relate to field reconstruction?
