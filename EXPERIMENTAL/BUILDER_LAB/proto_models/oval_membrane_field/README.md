# OVAL MEMBRANE FIELD

Prototype model for layered transition geometry in NEXAH.

---

## 🧠 Overview

OVAL_MEMBRANE_FIELD explores a possible structural extension of the NEXAH framework:

> Transition space is not empty, flat, or infinitely thin.  
>  
> It is a **layered oval field** with an active membrane region between inner and outer structure.

This module investigates whether transition, exchange, shrinking, and pulsation emerge from a nested shell geometry rather than from a simple boundary or line.

---

## 🔬 Core Hypothesis

The working assumption of this prototype is:

> The transition space is not a single oval.  
> It is an **oval-in-oval structure** with a membrane field in between.

This means:

- there is an **inner oval**
- there is an **outer oval**
- between both lies a **membrane space**
- the membrane is itself internally structured

Thus, transition does not occur on a line, but in a **volumetric exchange zone**.

---

## 🥚 Intuitive Image

A useful analogy is the structure of an egg:

- **inner core** → yolk-like root region
- **middle transfer region** → white / membrane exchange zone
- **outer shell** → boundary / containment structure

The important insight is:

> Exchange does not happen directly between inside and outside.  
> It happens through an intermediate membrane field.

---

## 🧩 Structural Assumption

The membrane is not infinitely thin.

It has internal structure:

- an **inner membrane face**
- a **middle transition layer**
- an **outer membrane face**

This implies that the interface is not a point, but a structured zone in which:

- pressure builds
- exchange occurs
- direction can change
- asymmetry can emerge

---

## 🔁 Oval-in-Oval Geometry

The field consists of nested transition shells:

```text
inner oval   → active path / root trajectory
membrane     → exchange and comparison zone
outer oval   → containment / counter-structure
```

These structures are not perfectly symmetric.

Their relative displacement produces:

- pulsation
- contraction / expansion
- pressure zones
- local cuts
- directional asymmetry

---

## ⚡ Pulsation

Pulsation emerges because the inner and outer oval are not identical.

They are:

- offset
- stretched
- locally coupled
- dynamically mismatched

Thus:

> pulsation is generated by the relative displacement of nested shells

This makes the transition field active rather than static.

---

## 🔬 Root Shrinking

Within this model, root shrinking is interpreted as a membrane-mediated exchange process.

The inner region does not shrink in isolation.

It contracts through transfer across the membrane field, similar to exchange through an intermediate layer.

This means:

- the core gives off or receives structure
- the membrane distributes pressure and transfer
- the outer shell reacts as counter-structure

In this interpretation:

> shrinking is not simple reduction  
> but structured redistribution across layers

---

## 🌧️ Exchange Channels

The fine “rain-like” structures seen in some graphs are interpreted here as:

- micro-transfer paths
- fine membrane channels
- exchange veins between layers

They are not merely noise.

They may indicate:

- directional transfer
- pressure release
- information redistribution
- local coupling between root and shell

---

## 🔋 Potential Difference

The membrane exchange can also be understood through a polarity model:

- inner region ↔ one pole
- outer region ↔ counter-pole
- membrane ↔ transfer medium

This is structurally similar to:

- anode / cathode systems
- charge separation
- pressure equalization across a field

Thus:

> exchange is driven by difference in potential, not by direct identity
