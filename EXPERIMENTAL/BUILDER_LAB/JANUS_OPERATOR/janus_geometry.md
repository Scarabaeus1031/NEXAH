# 🔷 Three Geometric Regimes

The Janus framework currently distinguishes three conceptual regimes.

---

## 1. Coherent Bidirectional Regions

In highly coherent regions:

```math
F_{\mathrm{forward}}(x)
\approx
F_{\mathrm{backward}}(x)
```

Directional organization remains locally aligned.

Geometric characteristics:

- smooth flow
- stable local structure
- persistent coherence
- low mismatch
- low transition activation

Visual interpretation:

```text
dense coherent flow corridors
```

---

## 2. Janus Transition Regions

Near transition gates:

```math
F_{\mathrm{forward}}(x)
\not\approx
F_{\mathrm{backward}}(x)
```

Local directional compatibility weakens.

Possible characteristics:

- trajectory splitting
- local divergence
- instability accumulation
- phase drift
- coherence fragmentation
- transition concentration

These regions may form:

```text
Janus corridors
```

or:

```text
directional bifurcation zones
```

---

## 3. Directionally Broken Regions

In strongly unstable or fragmented regions:

```text
forward and backward structure
lose coherent overlap
```

Possible behavior:

- chaotic scattering
- incoherent drift
- structural fragmentation
- irreversible local geometry

Interpretation remains exploratory.
