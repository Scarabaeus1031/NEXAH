# 🔷 Forward Field

Classical forward evolution is defined as:

$begin:math:display$
F\_\{\\mathrm\{forward\}\}\(x\)\=\\lim\_\{\\Delta t \\to 0\^\+\}\\frac\{\\phi\(x\,t\+\\Delta t\)\-\\phi\(x\,t\)\}\{\\Delta t\}
$end:math:display$

Interpretation:

```text

```

# 🔷 Backward Field

$begin:math:display$
F\_\{\\mathrm\{backward\}\}\(x\)\=\\lim\_\{\\Delta t \\to 0\^\+\}\\frac\{\\phi\(x\,t\)\-\\phi\(x\,t\-\\Delta t\)\}\{\\Delta t\}
$end:math:display$

Interpretation:

```text
local reconstructed backward flow
```

# 🪞 Janus Operator

The Janus Operator combines both directional fields:

$begin:math:display$
\\mathcal\{J\}\(x\)\=F\_\{\\mathrm\{forward\}\}\(x\)\\odot F\_\{\\mathrm\{backward\}\}\(x\)
$end:math:display$

where:

```text
⊙ = local overlap operator
```

Possible overlap implementations include:

- element-wise (Hadamard) product
- cosine similarity
- normalized projection
- directional alignment kernels
- local vector overlap metrics

depending on the experiment.

---

# 🌊 Directional Symmetry

An idealized symmetric region satisfies:

$begin:math:display$
F\_\{\\mathrm\{forward\}\}\(x\)\\approx F\_\{\\mathrm\{backward\}\}\(x\)
$end:math:display$

leading to:

$begin:math:display$
J\(x\) \\rightarrow 1
$end:math:display$

Regions with strong drift or transition activation may satisfy:

$begin:math:display$
F\_\{\\mathrm\{forward\}\}\(x\)\\not\\approx F\_\{\\mathrm\{backward\}\}\(x\)
$end:math:display$

leading to:

$begin:math:display$

$end:math:display$
