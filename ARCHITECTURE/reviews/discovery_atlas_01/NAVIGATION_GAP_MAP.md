## Reader questions

**Can a new reader find the strongest results from the root?** Partly. The root
finds subsystems and current state, not a curated cross-subsystem result set.

**Can a reader distinguish architecture, research, evidence, applications, and
visual material?** Usually yes at the top level. The distinction weakens inside
historical findings and visual narratives.

**Can a reader find negative results?** Only when already inside the relevant
modern validation bundle or review.

**Can a claim be traced to specification, implementation, evidence, and
review?** Modern prime and IEEE bundles mostly permit this. Historical findings,
semi-formal structural propositions, and visual families often do not.

**Do existing portals solve most of the problem?** They solve local routing.
They do not solve repository-wide claim classification or cross-generation
status reconciliation.

## Gap diagnosis

The primary gap is **synthesis and classification**, with a secondary
repository-level navigation gap. Source evidence organization is sufficiently
strong to build a small atlas; it does not first require moving or rewriting
the repository.
