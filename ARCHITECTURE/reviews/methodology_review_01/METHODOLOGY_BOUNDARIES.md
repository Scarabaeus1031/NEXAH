# Methodology Boundaries

## Core scope

If “Orientation Methodology” is retained as an informative description, its
smallest supported scope is:

> A practice for situating source material in declared contexts, perspectives,
> and representations; applying scoped operations; preserving evidence,
> provenance, uncertainty, limitations, and disagreement; and preventing
> results from silently acquiring stronger meaning, authority, action, or
> outcome status.

This definition does not require a particular domain, carrier format,
visualization, runtime, or scientific model.

## Methodology versus supporting infrastructure

| Repository component | Classification | Reason |
|---|---|---|
| Architecture | Governing support | assigns responsibilities and boundaries; it is not the empirical method |
| OLS | Semantic support and formal expression | defines released meanings and contracts; it does not validate a domain or prescribe all research practice |
| Orientation Layer and reports | Method realization | operationalize evidence-preserving orientation in a scoped software path |
| Validation protocols and frozen bundles | Method practice | predeclare scope, tests, nulls, limits, negative outcomes, and reproducible evidence |
| Evidence Atlas / claim register | Navigational support | makes evidence discoverable without becoming claim authority |
| Library and Editorial Operating System | Publication and human-governance support | preserve identity, explanation, audience, and authority; they are not experimental evidence |
| Applications | Instantiations and tests | apply the practice to bounded domains without defining it universally |

## Individual research programs

| Program | Relationship to the methodology | Boundary |
|---|---|---|
| Prime Modular Resonance | experimental program using explicit modular representations and transition tests | its residue findings and hypotheses are not methodology principles |
| Wheel / Product Spaces | exact and empirical reference-space case with strong separation of construction, test, and representation | CRT facts and wheel outcomes are domain results |
| IEEE Geometry | mature bounded application of manifests, frozen transfer, provenance, failure-aware operators, and prohibited implications | benchmark success is not general domain validity |
| JANUS | exploratory transition/coherence research and a separately governed architectural principle/operator family | no universal mechanism or automatic bridge is established |
| Memory | implementation and research on admitted, provenance-preserving episodes | storage or retrieval is not methodology unless its admission boundaries apply |
| Control | experimental research with explicit limits | no generalized control law; action remains outside orientation |
| Lorenz and Kuramoto | computational research cases | recurrence in these systems does not prove cross-domain theory |
| Orientation Translation | methodological and editorial research that exposes recurring inspectability practices | its reader effect and cross-domain grammar remain unvalidated |
| Atlas | representation and navigation perspective | visual or geometric recurrence is not semantic or mathematical authority |
| Library | publication, identity, explanation, and human authority infrastructure | publication does not validate a claim or define the scientific method |

## What belongs inside

- declaration of scope and relevant conditions;
- separation of source, observation, representation, finding, interpretation,
  and outcome;
- scoped operator or procedure contracts;
- traceability to evidence and method;
- preservation of provenance, uncertainty, missing information, limitations,
  and disagreement;
- explicit tests, failure conditions, non-claims, and incomplete status;
- human-visible review and separate authority for decisions or publication;
- bounded replay or equivalent repeatable evidence where a claim requires it.

## What remains outside

- universal physics or a theory of reality;
- domain-specific equations, discoveries, and empirical results;
- mathematical proofs, except as inputs with their own authority;
- a claim that all reference spaces share one structure;
- implementation algorithms, package layouts, transport, APIs, or visual
  styles;
- autonomous recommendation, authorization, execution, or policy;
- scientific validity inferred from OLS conformance;
- truth inferred from publication, validation labels, or repository placement;
- philosophical claims about meaning or consciousness;
- claims of novelty or superiority over other methodologies.

## Risks

### 1. Relabeling good research hygiene as a novel science

Provenance, reproducibility, uncertainty, negative results, and scope control
are established scientific virtues. NEXAH demonstrates an unusually explicit
integration of them, but the repository contains no comparative methodology
study establishing novelty. Calling the integration a new scientific
methodology without that work would overclaim.

### 2. Methodology becoming philosophy

“Orientation” can expand until it explains everything and tests nothing. The
term remains useful only while each claim identifies its material,
representation, operation, evidence, and boundary.

### 3. Methodology replacing theory

A disciplined way to study different systems does not establish that those
systems share a mechanism. The methodology must not launder visual or verbal
recurrence into scientific unification.

### 4. Methodology replacing OLS

OLS already owns released semantics. A repository-level methodology must not
add primitives, operators, profiles, or conformance meanings indirectly.

### 5. Circular validation

The repository cannot establish the method's effectiveness merely by showing
that its own artifacts follow its own boundaries. Independent use, comparative
assessment, and outcome measures are missing.

### 6. Historical selection bias

The strongest recent bundles are unusually well bounded, while older
experiments are less reproducible and rhetorically stronger. A methodology
defined only from the best surviving examples could misdescribe actual
repository practice.

### 7. Bureaucratic overhead

More manifests, statuses, and reviews can improve traceability but also impede
exploration. The repository supports proportional rigor: exploratory work may
remain exploratory if its status and claims are explicit.

### 8. Category capture

Absorbing Research, OLS, Applications, Library, or Editorial OS into a
“Methodology” layer would erase the very authority separation the proposed
method is meant to preserve.

## Boundary test

A future artifact instantiates the methodological core only if a reviewer can
answer, without relying on promotional interpretation:

1. What source material and question are in scope?
2. Under what context, perspective, representation, and relevant conditions?
3. What operation or procedure was actually applied?
4. What evidence and provenance support the result?
5. What uncertainty, missing information, limitations, and disagreement remain?
6. Which implications are explicitly prohibited?
7. Who or what has authority to interpret, publish, decide, or act?

This is a review test, not a new OLS conformance class.
