# Amendment Principle

**Status:** proposed supporting rule for Candidate Revision 01; non-canonical.

## Minimal rule

A proposed constitutional revision should:

1. identify the evidence and integrity problem motivating revision;
2. state the exact current text, proposed text, reasons, and affected scope;
3. distinguish constitutional change from OLS, architecture, method, policy,
   editorial, Registry, or implementation change;
4. preserve the earlier version, its status, and its decision context;
5. record contradictions, unresolved questions, and affected authorities;
6. receive explicit human authorization for the exact reviewed content; and
7. declare the new status and effective relation to prior versions.

No proposal, commit, publication, repetition, implementation, or visual
prominence changes constitutional status by implication. No constitutional
revision may silently amend OLS, the Kernel, Registry, Operator contracts,
Library Architecture, Editorial OS authority, or canonical architecture.

## Deliberate omissions

This principle does not create a council, voting rule, quorum, role hierarchy,
release cadence, or amendment numbering system. The repository does not yet
contain evidence or authorization for that machinery.
