# NEXAH CONSTITUTION
## Candidate Revision 01

**PROVISIONAL CANDIDATE**  
**NOT CANONICAL**  
**PENDING HUMAN REVIEW**

## Preamble

This candidate protects the integrity of NEXAH while its representations,
methods, systems, and publications continue to change. It does not define all
of NEXAH, replace subsystem authority, or convert research practice into canon.

## Article I — Bound before extending

NEXAH declares the relevant source, scope, question, representation, and
authority boundary before consequential interpretation is preserved or
extended.

Operationally, this prevents silent expansion beyond the evidence or the actor's
authority. It does not require every informal exploration to begin with a formal
study package.

Evidence: `MANIFESTO.md`; root subsystem authority map; source-bounded
Orientation Translation studies; OLS-0 ownership and architecture boundaries;
Library proposal isolation.

## Article II — Preserve provenance and history

Consequential claims, relations, transformations, decisions, and status changes
remain traceable to their sources and decision context, and earlier governed
states remain recoverable.

Traceability enables inspection; it does not prove correctness. This article
does not prescribe one registry, file format, or technical mechanism.

Evidence: OLS-0 traceability; OLS-6 change and preservation clauses; release
manifests; Registry provenance; source snapshots, diffs, and validation records.

## Article III — Keep statuses distinct

Evidence, interpretation, editorial translation, implementation, publication,
and authority may relate, but none silently substitutes for another.

This protects against treating implementation as semantics, presentation as
evidence, recurrence as legitimacy, or a proposal as canon. It does not require
all subsystems to use one status vocabulary.

Evidence: OLS normative/informative and ownership distinctions; Root README;
System State; Editorial OS status boundaries; Orientation Translation support
classes.

## Article IV — Preserve plurality without collapse

NEXAH may hold multiple representations together without implying that they are
identical, equivalent, complete, or naturally ranked.

This enables comparison without merger. It does not prohibit explicit editorial
selection, reasoned preference, or canonicalization performed by the proper
authority within a declared scope.

Evidence: current Manifesto; comparison framework; representation reviews;
Library reader journeys; Orientation Cartography.

## Article V — Keep negative knowledge visible

Material limits, failures, rejections, non-relations, unknowns, and stopping
decisions remain visible when they affect interpretation, validation, or change.

Absence of a relation is not support for one. This article does not require the
permanent recording of every abandoned thought or private draft.

Evidence: validation failures; rejected and unsupported-edge registers; open
questions; comparison stopping rules; OLS rejected/deferred proposal history.

## Article VI — Do not equalize material asymmetry

Differences in evidence, direction, authority, scale, completeness, and
comparability remain inspectable and are not erased for visual, narrative, or
systemic coherence.

This does not prohibit comparison across difference. It prohibits presenting
unequal cases as equal without an explicit, supported transformation.

Evidence: comparison and reciprocity reviews; evidence maps; Orientation
Cartography; asymmetric source support; repository authority boundaries.

## Article VII — Let nothing become canonical silently

Changes to canonical identity, semantics, architecture, or constitutional
status require declared scope, preserved history, explicit human authorization,
and an inspectable decision.

Use, repetition, implementation, authorship, visual prominence, or repository
placement alone does not confer authority. This article creates no new approval
body and does not override existing OLS, Library, Registry, Kernel, or Editorial
Operating System governance.

Evidence: OLS-0/6 change control; Library Architecture v1.0; Editorial Operating
System human authority boundary; guarded Editorial Writer; proposal overlays.

## Boundaries

This candidate is not a theory, ontology, method, technical specification,
workflow template, marketing statement, or grant of operational authority. It
does not validate reader effects, scientific claims, or system correctness. It
does not amend any canonical artifact.

## Amendment principle

A constitutional revision requires relevant evidence, documented reasons and
scope, preservation of earlier versions, explicit human authorization, and an
unambiguous status change. No revision may silently replace history or acquire
authority by publication alone.

## Status statement

This document remains a provisional candidate until a separate, explicit human
decision establishes or rejects repository-wide constitutional authority. This
review itself cannot make that decision.
