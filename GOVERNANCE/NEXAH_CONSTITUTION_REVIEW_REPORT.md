## Executive result

The repository contains no explicitly canonical, repository-wide NEXAH
Constitution. It does contain a substantial lineage of manifestos, experimental
principles, scoped governance, canonical specification rules, editorial
boundaries, and repeated source-bounded research practices.

OLS-0's phrase “constitutional entry point” applies only to the Orientation
Language Specification suite. The review found no authority conflict requiring
termination, but it preserves that boundary explicitly.

## Stable principles found

The strongest cross-system evidence supports seven candidate articles:

1. Boundedness.
2. Traceability and preserved history.
3. Separation of epistemic, operational, publication, and authority statuses.
4. Plurality without collapse.
5. Visibility of material negative knowledge.
6. Preservation of material asymmetry.
7. Explicit, inspectable, human-authorized canonical change.

## Candidates not admitted as separate articles

- Representation humility: supporting principle underlying separation,
  plurality, and asymmetry.
- Provisionality: supporting principle protected by status separation and change
  discipline.
- Reader honesty: retained as research-ethical/local policy because reader
  effect remains unvalidated.
- Open questions: incorporated under negative knowledge.
- Relational primacy and universal transition claims: rejected as too broad.
- Current editorial operations, functions, templates, and directory structures:
  kept method-local or provisional.

## Contradictions and productive polarities

The review preserves productive polarities between openness/boundedness,
plurality/canonical specification, exploration/stopping, living evolution/frozen
artifacts, accessibility/precision, autonomy/governance, experimentation/
stability, continuity/correction, and invitation/authority. Visual coherence
versus evidence asymmetry is a specific risk: presentation must not manufacture
equal support.
