# Cartographic Principles

1. **Evidence asymmetry must survive translation.** Unequal support may produce unequal density, prominence, or connection.
2. **Visual proximity is not evidence.** Nearby placement may support comparison, not causality or identity.
3. **Relational distance is qualitative unless explicitly measured.** Do not add physical scales to conceptual landscapes.
4. **Empty space may encode a boundary.** Absence of a route can preserve a non-admitted relation.
5. **A route is not automatically a causal chain.** Reader movement, explanatory order, and mechanism are different.
6. **A region is not automatically one mechanism.** Coherence may be comparative or editorial.
7. **Open territory must remain open.** Unknowns may not be completed for visual balance.
8. **Publication editions may vary in atmosphere, not in admitted relations.** A public derivative cannot promote, remove, or strengthen evidence silently.
9. **Every final map must remain traceable to source material.** Relations, boundaries, transformations, and corrections need inspectable records.
10. **Non-edges are part of the map.** Prohibited and unsupported relations must not reappear through bridges, icons, or landscape continuity.
11. **Reader agency must not erase evidence discipline.** Multiple paths are allowed; all retain the same source boundaries.
12. **Historical artifacts keep their original names and purposes.** Later vocabulary must not overwrite their provenance.
