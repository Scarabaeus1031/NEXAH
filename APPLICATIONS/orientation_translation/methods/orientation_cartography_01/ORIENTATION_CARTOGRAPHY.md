# Orientation Cartography 01

> **Status:** PROVISIONAL · HISTORICALLY RECONSTRUCTED · NOT YET CANONICAL

Orientation Cartography is the practice of translating relations, distinctions, boundaries, scales, reader paths, and open questions into a navigable spatial representation.

Within NEXAH, this practice appears to predate its explicit naming. The Solar–Lunar–Carbon reconstruction is not treated as its invention, but as the first documented case in which a completed bounded review was translated into an auditable Evidence Map and a derivative Museum Edition.

## Scope

This folder records a provisional workflow derived from one worked example and a bounded historical reconstruction. It is not part of OLS, does not modify the Kernel, and creates no Registry or Operator identity.

## Relationship to existing terms

| Term | Current apparent role | Overlap | Distinction from Orientation Cartography | Uncertainty / standardization |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Orientation Language | Published semantic specification | can describe declarations and orientation structures | Cartography is a visual/editorial practice, not the language specification | No vocabulary change proposed. |
| Orientation Space | Bounded relational space reconstructed by a study | supplies the space to be mapped | Space is the reviewed relational object; cartography is its spatial translation and audit | More cases are needed to test the boundary. |
| Orientation Map | A particular navigational representation | direct output form | A map is an artifact; cartography is the practice/workflow producing it | Current usage is broader than the audited prototype. |
| Atlas | Curated collection or structured set of related views | may collect maps and plates | Atlas is a publication architecture, not necessarily an audit method | Atlas admission criteria remain separate. |
| Field | Continuous or relational context in which positions matter | may become a region or placement surface | Field describes context; cartography decides how to represent it | “Field” has several historical meanings in NEXAH. |
| Library | Curated Works, Series, identities, and journeys | can behave as an atlas or orientation environment | Library owns Works and human exploration; cartography is one possible practice within it | Whole-Library cartographic function remains partly supported. |
| Reader Path | Curated route for a reader purpose | routing is a cartographic function | A path is one route, not the whole map or source relation | Editorial status must remain visible. |
| Translation | Movement between representations with declared choices | central operation | Cartography is one spatial form of translation | Translation is broader than visual mapping. |
| Navigation | Reader or system movement among alternatives | purpose served by maps | Navigation is use/activity; cartography constructs navigable representation | Reader effects remain untested. |
| Representation | A bounded view, model, plate, record, or map | source and output are representations | Cartography relates representations without claiming they are reality | No universal representation ontology is implied. |

Further standardization is premature. Reuse the terms in their current contexts and record ambiguity rather than merging them.
