# Program Assessment

## What has emerged

Orientation Translation now functions as a **source-bounded editorial research program**. It repeatedly takes a fixed public representation, separates several local views of it, constructs inspectable routes and relations, records boundaries and negative decisions, and publishes artifacts that allow the work to be reviewed.

That description is supported by three completed pilots, three reflections, three neighborhoods, one governed cross-neighborhood comparison, methodological reviews, comparative planetary studies, and one audited cartographic publication pair. It does not imply a validated general method.

## Recurring capabilities

The repository repeatedly demonstrates the ability to:

1. **Bound a source.** A revision or corpus is fixed and its identity and limitations are preserved.
2. **Separate representations.** Multiple views, scales, roles, or questions are made explicit without calling one complete.
3. **Compare locally.** Differences, partial correspondences, and non-equivalences remain inspectable.
4. **Construct reader routes.** Multiple non-ranked paths are offered with purpose, source basis, and limits.
5. **Qualify relations.** Direction, support, scope, and unsupported implications are recorded.
6. **Preserve negative decisions.** Rejections, non-transitivity, failed identity, absent reciprocity, and stopping are published.
7. **Reflect after construction.** Completed translations generate a separate record of emergent questions and candidate bridges without rewriting the source.
8. **Build bounded neighborhoods.** Local relations grow under admission and stopping controls rather than graph completion.
9. **Compare independent artifacts without merging them.** Identity, role, edge, reciprocity, asymmetry, and incomparability are examined separately.
10. **Audit editorial translation.** The Cartography prototype checks whether evidence status and non-edges survive visual and public translation.

The first nine recur across more than one artifact or study. The tenth is demonstrated once.

## Recurring workflows

The strongest common sequence is not one fixed pipeline. It is a dependency pattern:

```text
bounded source or corpus
        ↓
observations and limitations
        ↓
plural representations
        ↓
local comparison and reader questions
        ↓
routes / explanations
        ↓
evaluation and reflection
        ↓
admission, rejection, direction, stopping
        ↓
frozen reviewable artifact
```

Comparison and cartographic publication begin only after their inputs have been completed and frozen. Loops exist between reflection and relation admission, and between visual audit and correction. The repository supports publication order more strongly than a universal cognitive or analytical order.

## Recurring publication forms

Repeated across three cases:

- Source Record;
- Observation Record;
- Orientation Model;
- Comparison Matrix;
- Reader Mode;
- Explain Mode;
- Orientation Map as a textual reader-route artifact;
- Evaluation and Pilot Report;

Repeated across multiple later studies:

- bounded Study Report;
- matrices and registers;
- limitations and explicit disposition;

- negative and uncertainty registers.

Evidence Map, Museum Edition, and the audited two-layer cartographic package are each one worked production. Atlas language appears in several artifacts, but an audited multi-map Atlas publication is not established.

## Recurring review disciplines

- source and version fixation;
- evidence/editorial/unsupported separation;
- direction and reverse-edge review;
- identity before edge correspondence;
- reciprocity not assumed;
- asymmetry and incomparability preserved;
- scale and role kept distinct;
- rejection and stopping recorded;
- template recurrence separated from functional recurrence;
- review dispositions bounded by explicit non-claims.

## Common structure, ignoring terminology

The repeated work is:

```text
limit what may be used
        ↓
extract what is present
        ↓
separate views that should not collapse
        ↓
compare and relate them locally
        ↓
construct navigable routes
        ↓
explain reasons and limits
        ↓
record questions, exclusions, and stopping
        ↓
publish an inspectable result
```

Some later cases add comparison between frozen results or visual translation into an Evidence Map. Those additions are not yet universal program stages.

## More than visualization?

Yes, descriptively. Most core artifacts are Markdown records, matrices, audits, and traceability documents rather than images. The program consistently distinguishes:

- **evidence** — source-supported observations and relations;
- **interpretation** — analyst classifications, comparison findings, and bounded synthesis;
- **translation** — reader routes, explanatory ordering, or spatial reconstruction;
- **publication** — frozen reports, neighborhoods, comparisons, maps, and derivatives.

The distinction is structural and documented. Its effectiveness for readers and its reproducibility by independent analysts remain unknown.

## Natural program architecture observed

The corpus supports four recurring zones rather than one mandatory layer stack:

1. **Evidence boundary** — source/corpus identity, scope, observations, limitations, traceability.
2. **Orientation construction** — representations, comparisons, questions, routes, explanations.
3. **Review and relational growth** — evaluation, reflection, admissions, rejections, neighborhoods, stopping.
4. **Publication and comparison** — frozen artifacts, cross-artifact comparison, and in one case evidence/public maps.

The Living Library is a destination and context for selected published artifacts, not a demonstrated required final stage of every Orientation Translation execution.
