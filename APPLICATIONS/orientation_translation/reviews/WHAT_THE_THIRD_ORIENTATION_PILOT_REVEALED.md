## Distinctions that survived Reflection

- phase landmark versus biological event;
- chromosome referent versus changing state label;
- temporal precedence versus causal relation;
- spatial position versus process stage;
- condition/cue versus guarantee;
- nuclear division versus cell division;
- normal result versus all possible outcomes;
- source-supported relation versus editorial navigation route.

## Initially plausible distinctions that collapsed or weakened

- A single “before/during/after” model was too coarse because spatial and state changes overlap.
- “Normal versus abnormal” was too coarse because the source distinguishes organismal variation, incomplete division, and errors with different statuses.
- “One cell becomes two” collapsed daughter nuclei, Cytokinesis, multinucleate outcomes, and errors.
- “Checkpoint controls transition” was too smooth; the source does not provide the complete mechanism or a guarantee.

## Unresolved questions

1. What exact identity relation connects replicated chromosomes, sister chromatids, and daughter chromosomes?
2. How should the broad and narrow uses of “mitosis” be reconciled editorially?
3. Which checkpoint claims remain supported after independent source-quality review?
4. How should open, closed, and semi-open forms be represented without building an ontology?
5. Which operations overlap in time rather than merely follow one another?
6. What intermediate representations would support the jump from local process to growth or regeneration?
7. Do real readers benefit from separating state, space, sequence, and condition?

## Rejected interpretations

- a universal linear phase law;
- checkpoints guarantee error-free daughters;
- every Mitosis opens the nuclear envelope;
- Cytokinesis is always the final mitotic phase;
- chromosome labels prove a formal identity ontology;
- errors directly establish cancer causality;
- the source supports social, computational, historical, planetary, or psychological analogies;
- this case supports an Orientation Hypothesis or Atlas entry.
