## What representations were present?

The source itself contained several representational forms: an overall reaction, an ordered pathway, a cycle, a membrane-gradient mechanism, spatial compartments, alternative branches, and ATP accounting. The pilot's six representations did not create those forms, but separated them by question and explanatory purpose.

## Hidden questions

The fixed source and translation make the following questions visible but do not answer them:

1. How is pathway flux regulated under changing cellular demand?
2. How are ATP yields measured rather than calculated?
3. How do amino acids and fatty acids enter the glucose-centered account?
4. How does prokaryotic spatial organization change the represented sequence?
5. Which conditions distinguish use of anaerobic respiration from fermentation across organisms?
6. How should the article's different ATP totals be reconciled?
7. How does cellular respiration scale into organismal or ecosystem flux without collapsing levels?
8. Which source claims would change after independent scientific review?
9. What reader prerequisites are actually necessary for each path?
10. Does the separation into six representations improve orientation for a real reader?

## Did Reflection remain distinct?

Yes, provisionally. The frozen translation organized source content; Reflection identified relations among those representations, consolidated evidence gradients, exposed hidden questions, and classified bridges without changing the source or pilot. That is a distinct analytical operation in this case. Repeated use still does not establish a universal layer.

## Status vocabulary audit

- **Source-supported observation/relation:** usable when tied to the fixed revision and explicit scope.
- **Process observation:** usable for properties visible only after organization.
- **Editorial procedure/construction:** usable for representations, paths, classifications, and stopping.
- **Candidate growth rule:** usable only in the later process audit, never as evidence.
- **Unsupported interpretation:** essential for cross-scale, cross-domain, and scientific overreach.

The vocabulary remained usable. The only revision needed was to distinguish a source-supported *relation* from a source-supported isolated observation inside this Reflection inventory; authority did not change.

## Procedures requiring revision or restraint

- A fixed number of representations or paths was not useful.
- A uniform depth or symmetric branch structure would misrepresent the source.
- Direct support did not compel neighborhood admission; local orientation relevance still had to be assessed.
- Cross-scale source statements required stronger boundary language than molecular transitions.
- “Freeze completed versions” required a staged interpretation: the translation core was frozen before Reflection, while the sequence-level report must be added later without rewriting the core.

## What must not be generalized

- six representations;
- four reader paths;
- a glucose-centered pathway as a universal respiration model;
- eukaryotic mitochondrial organization across all cells;
- one ATP figure;
- oxygen absence as one branch;
- the source's limited ecosystem relation;
- the usefulness of Reader Mode without reader evidence;
- any cross-pilot identity, role, reciprocity, or Atlas claim.
