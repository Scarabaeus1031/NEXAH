## Answer to the research question

The whiteboard does more than reorganize chemistry observations. Its chemistry
core remains inside Planetary Chemistry Atlas 01, but the image introduces
three additional questions:

1. whether unlike systems may be compared through geometric form;
2. whether boundaries and interfaces may be compared by relational role;
3. whether field-driven, case-specific changes may be compared without
   treating their mechanisms as identical.

These questions cannot be contained by a planetary-atmosphere composition
study without changing that study's object and evidence requirements.

The image does **not** demonstrate one unified morphology or transition space.
It combines:

- an evidenced planetary chemistry comparison;
- an editorial geometric inventory;
- a proposed boundary/interface comparison;
- one proposed field–deformation case;
- an editorial transition grammar.

## Principal findings

### Demonstrated by the review

- The whiteboard contains multiple comparison-object types and scales.
- Its non-chemical sections require evidence not owned by Atlas 01.
- Geometric similarity, boundary role, mechanism and function are separate
  comparison dimensions.
- The visual itself supports an editorial synthesis, not a shared natural law.
- `same form ≠ same process` and `same molecule ≠ same world` correctly express
  the necessary comparison boundaries.

### Plausible but not demonstrated

- Geometric forms could support a controlled comparison space.
- Boundary/interface roles could recur across independently evidenced cases.
- Field–interaction–deformation could support comparison when each mechanism
  is declared and verified separately.

### Unknown

- Whether the proposed forms recur under a stable comparison protocol.
- Whether inside/boundary/outside is more than an editorial reading grammar.
- Whether multiple physical cases share a useful transition architecture.
- Whether cross-scale comparison produces orientation gain rather than analogy.

### Not admissible from this review

- one universal morphology-to-function rule;
- one mechanism shared by planets, atmospheres, magnetospheres, cells, eggs and
  drops;
- recurrence inferred from icons or visual resemblance;
- a causal chain from composition alone to planetary properties;
- a new research field or Atlas inferred from one whiteboard.

## Comparison architecture

The best description is:

**a combination of multiple bounded comparison spaces presented as an
editorial synthesis.**

It is not adequately described by Planetary Chemistry extension, Morphology
comparison, Boundary comparison, or Transition comparison alone.

## Disposition

# Separate bounded study

This disposition recognizes that the comparison question is independent of
Planetary Chemistry Atlas 01 while refusing to promote the whiteboard into a
future Atlas or unified domain. The present review is the boundary: it records
what emerged and what remains unsupported.
