# Candidate Comparison Spaces

## Test

A comparison space is admitted here only when its objects, comparison basis,
scale rules, and evidence boundary can be stated independently. Admission as a
candidate space does not validate its scientific claims or create an Atlas.

## CS-01 — Planetary composition and process

- **Objects:** elements, species, atmospheric states, process families and
  planetary contrasts.
- **Basis:** occurrence, abundance under declared reference conditions, and
  separately evidenced process families.
- **Scale:** molecular to planetary, with explicit scale changes.
- **Evidence:** strongest of the proposed spaces; already reviewed in Atlas 01.
- **Assessment:** coherent, but not new. This is the Planetary Chemistry core.

## CS-02 — Geometric morphology

- **Objects:** sphere, ellipsoid, torus, drop and shell-like form.
- **Basis:** geometric descriptors such as enclosure, curvature, symmetry and
  deformation would be required, but are not supplied by the visual.
- **Scale:** abstract geometry with instances at different physical scales.
- **Evidence:** editorial inventory only.
- **Assessment:** a distinguishable comparison space, but presently
  under-specified and unsupported as a research finding.

## CS-03 — Boundary and interface roles

- **Objects:** inside, boundary/interface, outside/environment and exchange.
- **Basis:** positional role, permeability, coupling, containment or exchange
  would have to be declared per system.
- **Scale:** cellular through planetary.
- **Evidence:** partial for atmospheric/exospheric cases; otherwise editorial.
- **Assessment:** distinct from geometric morphology. Two objects can share a
  boundary role without sharing form or mechanism.

## CS-04 — Field–interaction–deformation cases

- **Objects:** a declared field, affected system, interaction, and observed or
  modeled change.
- **Basis:** case-specific physical mechanism and compatible measurements or
  models.
- **Scale:** system-dependent.
- **Evidence:** the whiteboard proposes a tidal case; no dedicated evidence set
  is present in this review.
- **Assessment:** potentially coherent as a case-comparison discipline, but the
  visual contains only one explicit example.

## CS-05 — Editorial transition grammar

- **Objects:** representations occupying the roles inside, boundary, exchange,
  field, environment, and transition.
- **Basis:** curated relational position in a visual explanation.
- **Scale:** representational, not automatically physical.
- **Evidence:** directly supported as an editorial construction by the visual.
- **Assessment:** coherent as a reading grammar. It does not establish a
  scientific cross-scale mechanism.

## Architecture options

| Option | Fit | Finding |
|---|---|---|
| A. Planetary Chemistry extension | Partial | Contains CS-01 and selected planetary examples, but cannot contain biological instances or generic geometry without changing its question and evidence base. |
| B. Morphology comparison | Partial | Describes CS-02 but omits process, boundary role and evidentiary status. |
| C. Boundary comparison | Partial | Describes CS-03 but not chemical composition or geometric form as such. |
| D. Transition comparison | Partial | Describes CS-04/CS-05 only if physical and editorial transitions remain distinct. |
| E. Editorial synthesis | Strong | Correctly describes what the visual presently demonstrates: a curated juxtaposition of several spaces. |
| F. Combination of multiple bounded comparison spaces | Strongest | Preserves the distinct objects, scales, mechanisms and evidence requirements visible in the whiteboard. |

## Finding

The actual comparison architecture is **F: a combination of multiple bounded
comparison spaces**, presented through **E: editorial synthesis**. No single
scientific comparison space currently contains the whole image.
