# Evidence and Boundary Grammar

## Finding

Evidence and boundary handling is the most stable part of the observed
operation family. It is expressed through recurring textual actions rather
than one visual convention.

| State or function | Recurring expression | Sources | Stability | Boundary |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| observation | source-visible statement, measured occurrence, direct support | C-03–C-08, C-14 | repeated, terminology varies | observation is not automatically validation or explanation |
| measurement | values with basis/reference conditions and source anchor | C-04–C-05 | strong inside Atlas 01, sparse elsewhere | values at incompatible scales/reference levels are not directly comparable |
| model | explicit modeled or mechanism-supported status; implementation diagrams labelled as such | C-01, C-03–C-05, C-20–C-23 | repeated but not uniform | model is not observed reality |
| inference | inferred/editorial/interpretive status and non-implication | C-01–C-05, C-10–C-16 | repeated and explicit | inference does not become evidence through repetition |
| uncertainty | unknown, unresolved, weak, conditional, deferred, limitation | C-03–C-16, C-18, C-23–C-25 | strongly recurring vocabulary family | labels are not one canonical cross-repository taxonomy |
| editorial synthesis | proposal, reader route, comparison path, review formulation | C-01–C-08, C-17–C-23 | repeated across areas | arrangement is evidence of editorial intent, not natural relation |
| exclusion | `does not establish`, prohibited implication, not admitted, rejected relation | C-02–C-18, C-20–C-25 | strongest recurring safeguard | rejection of an implication does not prove its opposite |
| stopping | stop point, scope boundary, disposition, reopening condition | C-06–C-09, C-13–C-18 | recurring in studies/pilots | closure is local, not subject completion |
| evidence boundary | citation, source code, hash, status legend, authority statement | C-03–C-05, C-10–C-16, C-20–C-26 | strongly recurring | internal provenance is not external scientific validation |

## Stable recurrence

The following recur in multiple artifact classes:

1. name the status of a representation or claim;
2. state the source or authority boundary;
3. preserve uncertainty and unsupported implications;
4. distinguish an editorial route from a scientific relation;
5. state where the artifact stops;
6. keep rejected or unresolved relations visible.

## Local formatting conventions

These do not form a stable grammar:

- specific colors for evidence classes;
- solid/dashed/dotted line semantics;
- panel border color;
- icon shape;
- vertical versus horizontal flow;
- badges and check marks;
- capitalization of status labels.

The Planetary Whiteboard Proposal (C-05) proposes a local line legend, while
the Architecture and Explanation visuals (C-21, C-23) use different color and
line meanings. No shared visual semantics can be admitted.

## OLS boundary

OLS 1.0 supplies normative semantics, declaration rules, operator contracts,
and conformance authority. This review does not derive editorial operations
from OLS and does not use visual recurrence to reinterpret OLS operators.
Words such as `compare`, `orient`, or `explain` may occur in both contexts;
their occurrence does not give the review's descriptive actions normative
status.
