# Candidate Grammar Assessment

## Conservative test

| Option | Required evidence | Assessment |
|---|---|---|
| A. no recurring editorial grammar | no traceable operation beyond isolated artifacts | Rejected: thirteen operations recur across multiple artifact classes. |
| B. isolated local conventions | recurrence explained only by one visual style, series or template | Rejected as complete account: color and arrows are local/ambiguous, but evidence boundaries, alternative routes and non-implications recur more broadly. |
| C. a family of recurring editorial operations | traceable actions recur while variation, ambiguity and counterexamples remain visible | Supported. |
| D. a provisional cross-domain editorial grammar | stable relation semantics, compositional order and reproducible use across independent domains | Not supported: operations have no mandatory syntax or shared visual semantics; independence is weak. |
| E. a candidate formal system | evidenced syntax, semantics, constraints, failures and reproducibility | Rejected: no formal syntax, parser, conformance target, independent reproduction or measured reader behavior exists. |

## Why option C is supported

The corpus repeatedly performs distinguishable editorial actions:

- it separates representations before comparing them;
- it states relation and evidence boundaries;
- it offers more than one reader route;
- it preserves asymmetry, uncertainty and rejected implications;
- it marks local stopping conditions and open continuations;
- it links spaces without automatically merging their identity or authority.

These operations recur in biology pilots, planetary comparison, Library
navigation, Living Concepts reviews, architecture communication, and method
audits. Their recurrence is functional, not merely decorative.

## Why option D is not supported

- shared prompts and authorship weaken domain independence;
- colors, arrows, boxes and line styles have inconsistent semantics;
- operation order varies by artifact purpose;
- not every domain uses the same movements;
- book recurrence is mediated through existing reviews;
- no independent editor has reproduced the operation family;
- no reader test demonstrates that the operations cause orientation gain.

## Why OLS is not the missing formal layer

OLS 1.0 is already a canonical semantic specification with its own authority,
contracts and conformance model. This review neither extends nor interprets it.
Observed editorial operations that share ordinary-language labels with OLS
operators remain descriptive review findings.

## Final disposition

# RECURRING EDITORIAL OPERATION FAMILY

This disposition admits recurrence without claiming a standard, theory,
universal grammar, natural mechanism, canonical vocabulary, or executable
system.
