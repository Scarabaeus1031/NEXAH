# Relation Type Matrix

## Purpose

Editorial operations are safe only when the relation they propose remains
inspectable. Proximity, color, symmetry and arrows are insufficient by
themselves.

| Relation type | How represented in the sample | Source examples | Ambiguity | Readers must not infer |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Identity | exact source/Work/node/clause identity; scoped identity tests | C-10–C-16, C-18, C-24–C-25 | same label may denote different role or scale | same word, icon, form or position means same object |
| Similarity | shared constituent, form, role or recurring action with qualifiers | C-01–C-04, C-10, C-17 | visual resemblance is especially strong pressure | shared origin, mechanism, function or evidence strength |
| Contrast | side-by-side cases, difference matrices, `≠`, paired status columns | C-01–C-05, C-09–C-12, C-21, C-23 | paired layout may imply exhaustive binaries | one case is preferred, opposite, or causally related |
| Sequence | numbered steps, arrows, ordered lists, reader routes | C-01, C-04, C-06–C-08, C-22–C-23 | temporal, physical, procedural and editorial order share notation | adjacency or order establishes causality or necessity |
| Causality | usually expressed cautiously in scientific prose and often explicitly blocked | C-03–C-05, C-08, C-11 | arrows and left-to-right layouts can overstate causal support | editorial sequence is a physical mechanism; correlation is cause |
| Containment | nested panels, subsystem boxes, inside/outside language, scope declarations | C-01–C-03, C-21–C-23 | geometry may represent ownership, scale or convenience | contained objects are equivalent or governed by one mechanism |
| Analogy | condensable cycles, morphology examples, representation parallels | C-01–C-04, C-17–C-18 | analogy can silently become identity | similar explanatory role establishes scientific sameness |
| Translation | source → representation → explanation; work → reviewed contract | C-14, C-18, C-22–C-23 | what is preserved/lost is not always stated | translation is lossless, neutral, or authoritative |
| Navigation | alternative paths, entry points, cross-links, next questions | C-06–C-08, C-19, C-21–C-23 | a route can look like recommendation or scientific pathway | offered order is optimal, mandatory or empirically validated |
| Evidence support | citations, hashes, evidence classes, trace tables and status legends | C-03–C-05, C-10–C-16, C-24–C-26 | a link can be mistaken for validation | provenance alone proves correctness or sufficiency |
| Open question | unresolved lists, path alternatives, reopening conditions | C-06, C-08, C-13–C-19 | open questions can look like roadmap commitments | the repository has accepted a hypothesis or future implementation |

## Matrix finding

The corpus repeatedly distinguishes relations in accompanying text and tables,
but it does not provide a stable visual syntax that identifies relation type
from line, arrow, color, border, or spatial position alone. Relation meaning is
therefore carried mainly by labels, captions, scope statements and provenance.
