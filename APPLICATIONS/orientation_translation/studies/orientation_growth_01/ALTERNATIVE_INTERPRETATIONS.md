# Alternative Interpretations

The same completed sequence can support several descriptions. This review does not select a category merely because one is more ambitious.

| Interpretation | What it explains well | What it fails to explain | Present evidence | Additional evidence needed |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| A. Editorial workflow | The ordered production of a translation, reflection, and bounded neighborhood; human selection and stopping. | Whether the sequence has stable, transferable behavior beyond a publishing workflow. | Strong: P → R → N are distinct editorial artifacts with explicit constraints. | Independent editors following the same procedure on the same and different sources. |
| B. Evidence-bounded graph construction | Node and edge admission, support classes, rejection, direction, non-transitivity, and finite stopping. | Reader purpose, explanatory lenses, and why certain representations matter editorially. | Strong: N is a small connected graph with inspected transitions. | Formal comparison with other bounded graph methods and adversarial edge audits. |
| C. Research workflow | Observation, question formation, hypothesis restraint, traceability, and follow-up study design. | It does not establish scientific findings or test a scientific hypothesis. | Moderate: the sequence produces research questions about its own process. | Preregistered replication with explicit analyst and source controls. |
| D. Learning-design workflow | Progressive orientation, neighboring concepts, and possible improvement in question quality. | No learners, learning outcomes, or comparative educational measures were tested. | Limited: reader paths and orientation gain are editorially plausible. | Reader study with tasks, comprehension, transfer, and question-quality measures. |
| E. Knowledge-architecture method | Separation of representations, relationships, provenance, authority, and navigation. | Whether the local design scales or interoperates with other knowledge systems. | Strong locally, weak beyond the pilot. | Multiple neighborhoods, versioned comparison, and governance under growth. |
| F. Orientation Growth Model | The staged movement from fixed source through representation, reflection, admission, rejection, and stopping. | Whether these stages are necessary, sufficient, independent, or reproducible. | Candidate only: one reconstructable sequence. | A second independent pilot, analyst comparison, and failure tests. |

## Counter-analysis

- The graph interpretation could explain the neighborhood without invoking a distinct orientation theory.
- The editorial-workflow interpretation could explain the entire sequence as disciplined curation.
- The research-workflow interpretation is limited because no external scientific claim was tested.
- The learning-design interpretation remains unsupported by learner evidence.
- The knowledge-architecture interpretation fits the artifacts but not yet their effectiveness at scale.
- The candidate growth-model interpretation organizes the sequence, but organization is not validation.

## Editorial recommendation

Use a narrow candidate model as a reproducibility target, while retaining all six interpretations. At present the evidence most securely supports a disciplined editorial procedure that produces an evidence-bounded local relationship structure. Whether that procedure is best understood as a transferable growth model remains unresolved.
