# Orientation Growth Model

This is a **candidate local process model** reconstructed from one completed sequence. It is not a universal model, an OLS extension, Kernel behavior, or canonical architecture.

## Narrow model

> An orientation space may grow by fixing its evidence, producing a bounded representation, reflecting on what that representation reveals and leaves open, admitting or rejecting neighboring representations through inspectable transitions, and stopping before support is inherited or scope becomes indefinite.

Growth includes additions, exclusions, and explicit closure. More nodes alone do not constitute orientation growth.

## What the model does not claim

- that every subject produces a neighborhood;
- that every neighborhood has two rings;
- that 31 nodes or 30 edges are ideal;
- that all growth proceeds linearly;
- that orientation gain has been measured;
- that the process scales or can be automated;
- that Atlas or hypothesis layers currently exist;
- that the model is independent of editorial judgment.

## Rejection and stopping

The model fails if it cannot preserve evidence boundaries while admitting useful relations. A valid outcome may be a smaller neighborhood, an asymmetric neighborhood, or no expansion. Refusal to grow is therefore an admissible result when evidence is insufficient.
