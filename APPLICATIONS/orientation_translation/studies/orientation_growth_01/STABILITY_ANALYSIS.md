# Stability Analysis

This analysis distinguishes a finite artifact from a stable method. The first is demonstrated locally; the second is not.

| Stability dimension | Current evidence | Local assessment | Remaining fragility |
|---|---|---|---|
| Structural | N contains 31 nodes, 30 admitted edges, finite rings, no extra crosslinks. | The resulting neighborhood is finite, connected, and inspectable. | A different source or analyst may produce another shape; scalability is unknown. |
| Evidential | Each edge has a support class, reason, source location, and unsupported boundary. | Evidence classes remain visible and unsupported expansions are excluded. | Classifications have not been independently audited; source granularity varies. |
| Semantic | Neighboring representations remain distinct and transitivity is prohibited. | The artifact resists silent synonymy and inherited meaning. | Natural-language labels can still conceal different senses or scales. |
| Procedural | P was frozen before R; R was frozen before N; each artifact has a distinct task. | The sequence is reconstructable and earlier artifacts remain unchanged. | It is a single execution by one editorial process; order effects are untested. |
| Interpretive | Source observations, process observations, editorial procedures, candidate rules, and unsupported interpretations are separated in this study. | Claims can be read at different authority levels. | Readers may still treat visual centrality or polished prose as authority. |

## What contributed to stability

- fixed evidence bodies;
- explicit support labels;
- edge-level reasons and boundaries;
- recorded rejections;
- non-transitivity;
- finite scope and explicit stopping;
- separation of reflection from neighborhood admission;
- no mutation of OLS, Registry, Kernel, or architecture.

## What remains fragile

1. **Single-case dependence.** No second topic has reproduced the sequence.
2. **Analyst dependence.** No independent analyst comparison exists.
3. **Source dependence.** A public source can constrain or bias the neighborhood.
4. **Classification dependence.** Direct, indirect, editorial, and unsupported remain human judgments.
5. **Visual influence.** A clean graph may appear more certain than its evidence warrants.
6. **Scale handling.** Cross-scale transitions are visible, but no general scale discipline is established.
7. **Reader value.** Better navigation and better questions are argued editorially, not empirically measured.
8. **Growth pressure.** Future additions could weaken the present boundaries if versioning and rejection are not preserved.

## Conclusion

The completed neighborhood is locally stable as a frozen editorial artifact. The method that produced it is only provisionally stable. Reproducibility, analyst independence, reader benefit, and controlled scaling remain untested.
