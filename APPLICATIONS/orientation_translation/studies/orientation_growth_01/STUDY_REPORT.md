# HOW ORIENTATION GROWS
## Study 01 — From Pilot to Neighborhood

## Scope

This study reconstructs one completed application sequence:

> Source Representation → Orientation Translation → Reflection → Neighborhood Construction

It treats the Photosynthesis pilot, its reflection, and its neighborhood as frozen evidence. It evaluates the process that connected them, not the science of Photosynthesis. No OLS, Registry, Kernel, architecture, or deferred identity is changed.

## 1. What grew?

The scientific source did not grow. The orientation space grew through bounded representations, explicit reader questions, inspectable nodes and directional transitions, exclusions, and a stopping record. The move from P to R added process observations and unanswered questions. The move from R to N added a finite local relationship structure. Rejections and boundaries also changed what the space could responsibly claim.

## 2. Which layers are actually visible?

- **Source** is a distinct evidential input.
- **Representation** and **Orientation** are distinguishable functions within the translation.
- **Reflection** is provisionally distinct because it identifies emergent relations and questions without rewriting the source.
- **Neighborhood** is provisionally distinct because it admits and rejects transitions rather than merely drawing them.
- **Atlas** is not present.
- **Hypothesis** remains outside this application sequence and belongs to an explicitly governed research context if pursued.

Reflection and Neighborhood can also be described as stages in an editorial or graph-construction workflow. One case cannot prove that they are universal architectural layers.

## 3. What counted as a growth event?

Positive additions included new bounded representations, questions, nodes, and admitted edges. Negative or limiting events included rejected bridges, unsupported expansions, non-transitivity, directional boundaries, and closure. The study therefore treats growth as a change in navigable and inspectable relations—not as node accumulation.

## 4. Which admission rules were used?

The strongest observed controls were:

- no admission by link or shared vocabulary alone;
- direct support permits review but does not compel admission;
- every edge states its reason, support, and unsupported boundary;
- indirect support remains explicitly indirect;
- editorial and unsupported bridges are excluded;
- second-ring transitions receive their own audit;
- support is not inherited transitively;
- visual proximity has no evidential authority.

Fixed evidence before every expansion, reflection-before-admission, and growth-event logging are useful candidate generalizations, not established rules.

## 5. What prevented graph explosion?

The neighborhood used a fixed evidence body, relevance review, one reviewed secondary continuation per first-ring branch, no extra crosslinks, one labeled indirect edge, explicit exclusions, and a declared stop. These controls produced a finite structure. Some are epistemic; others are editorial constraints chosen for the pilot.

## 6. Why did the process stop?

It stopped at 31 nodes and 30 admitted edges because the selected local construction was complete under its own declared limits. That endpoint is not an optimum, a completeness claim, or a reusable numeric rule. Another analyst could reasonably stop earlier or later if every difference remained explicit and supported. Reopening requires new fixed evidence or a new orientation question, version preservation, and a fresh audit.

## 7. How stable is the result?

The frozen neighborhood is structurally finite, evidentially labeled, semantically differentiated, procedurally reconstructable, and interpretively bounded. It is locally stable as an artifact. The producing method remains fragile because it has one topic, one editorial history, no independent analyst, no reader evaluation, and no test at larger scale.

## 8. What are the principal failure modes?

The principal risks are graph explosion, semantic collapse, transitive overreach, evidence laundering, boundary drift, source drift, scale collapse, reciprocity assumption, aesthetic authority, and infinite deferral. Current safeguards are mostly human editorial controls. They are inspectable, but their consistency and transferability are untested.

## 9. What kind of process is this?

The evidence supports several live interpretations:

- an editorial workflow;
- evidence-bounded graph construction;
- a self-reflective research workflow;
- a possible learning-design workflow;
- a knowledge-architecture method;
- a candidate Orientation Growth Model.

The most conservative description is a disciplined editorial procedure that produces an evidence-bounded local relationship structure. The candidate growth model is useful as a replication target, but the present case does not establish that it is necessary, sufficient, or categorically distinct from the alternatives.

## 10. What must Pilot 02 reproduce?

Pilot 02 should independently demonstrate:

1. a fixed and preserved source;
2. a bounded Orientation Translation that distinguishes evidence from editorial construction;
3. a Reflection that exposes questions without answering or promoting them;
4. a Neighborhood with edge-level reasons, support classes, unsupported boundaries, admissions, and rejections;
5. directional and non-transitive treatment;
6. a justified stop that is not copied from the first pilot;
7. frozen artifacts suitable for comparison.

It need not reproduce the same lenses, rings, counts, symmetry, or content. Cellular Respiration is a strong adjacency-based candidate, but its reverse relation to Photosynthesis must be evaluated independently.

## 11. Where does OH-001 belong?

OH-001 does not belong inside the completed local growth sequence and remains deferred. Its earliest admissible consideration would follow an independent reproduction and a separate cross-neighborhood comparison. That checkpoint would only determine whether a broader hypothesis question is warranted; it would not create an identity, change architecture, or authorize implementation.

## 12. What can be concluded now?

The completed case demonstrates that a source-bounded translation can generate a reflection record and a finite, inspectable neighborhood without silently promoting weak relations. It supports a narrow candidate model built around fixity, translation, reflection, admission/rejection, and stopping. It does not demonstrate reproducibility, analyst independence, reader benefit, scalability, automation, Atlas formation, or universality.

## Provisional disposition

**MULTIPLE INTERPRETATIONS REMAIN OPEN**
