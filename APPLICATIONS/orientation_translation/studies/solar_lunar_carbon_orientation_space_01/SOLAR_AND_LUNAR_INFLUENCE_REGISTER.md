# Solar and Lunar Influence Register

## Direct solar influence

| Relation | Evidence | Boundary |
|---|---|---|
| Light energy → photosynthetic process entry | Supported in the fixed Photosynthesis source and pilot | Not a full solar-radiation or planetary energy model. |
| Light-dependent reactions → electron transfer and carrier production | Supported | Does not cover every photosynthetic variant. |
| Radiation → planetary photochemistry | Supported in Planetary Chemistry Atlas 01 as a process family | Same process-family label does not imply identical chemistry or rates. |
| Solar forcing → planetary atmospheric conditions | Moderate, world-specific | Must be analyzed with pressure, composition, clouds, internal heat, and history. |
| Solar wind → lunar exosphere supply and variability | Supported in the registered lunar evidence | Does not imply a terrestrial biological effect. |

## Direct lunar influence

| Relation | Evidence | Boundary |
|---|---|---|
| Moon as a variable surface-bound exosphere case | Supported | This is a lunar environmental state, not influence on Earth biology. |
| Moon → terrestrial tides | Weak in this corpus | The case appears in an editorial whiteboard; no dedicated physical evidence set was audited. |

## Shared environmental influence

| Comparison | Evidence | Boundary |
|---|---|---|
| Solar and lunar contributions considered together in a terrestrial tide representation | Weak | Co-presence in a diagram does not establish quantitative equivalence, sufficiency, or one forcing mechanism. |
| Solar energy, water availability, CO₂, and temperature as photosynthetic conditions | Supported with biological boundaries | The Moon is not part of this admitted relation. |
| Solar forcing and internal energy in planetary atmosphere comparison | Supported as a required distinction | Internal energy is not lunar forcing and must remain separate. |

## Comparative finding

Solar and lunar influence do not form a balanced pair in the evidence. Solar relations enter both the biological and planetary regions directly. The lunar record is confined to a supported exosphere case and a weak tidal case. The orientation space must preserve this asymmetry.
