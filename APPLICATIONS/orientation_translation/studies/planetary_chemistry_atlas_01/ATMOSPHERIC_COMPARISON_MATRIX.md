# Atmospheric Comparison Matrix

Values are approximate orientation values, not a substitute for mission data.
“Temperature” refers to a declared surface or atmospheric reference; it is not
a single comparable global constant.

| World | Atmospheric state | Major constituents | Pressure/reference | Temperature/reference | Clouds / condensates | Observed variability and processes |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Mercury | Surface-bound exosphere | O, Na, H, He, K | Effectively collisionless; no weather-producing atmosphere | Surface about −180 to 430 °C | None as atmospheric clouds; polar water ice occurs in permanent shadow | Solar-wind and impact sourcing, loss to space, strong day/night surface contrast |
| Venus | Dense atmosphere | ~96.5% CO₂, ~3.5% N₂; trace SO₂, H₂O, CO and others | ~92–93 bar at mean surface | ~467 °C surface | H₂SO₄/H₂O cloud and haze system | Superrotation, waves, cloud variability, photochemistry, volcanism-atmosphere questions |
| Earth | Dense atmosphere | Dry air ~78% N₂, ~21% O₂, ~0.93% Ar, ~0.04% CO₂; H₂O variable | ~1.013 bar mean sea level | Strong spatial/temporal range; global mean is not used as a world identifier here | H₂O liquid/ice clouds | Weather, hydrological and biogeochemical cycles, volcanism, biospheric exchange |
| Moon | Surface-bound exosphere | Mainly He, Ne, Ar; trace H₂ and Na among others | About 10¹⁵ times fewer molecules per cm³ than Earth’s atmosphere; non-collisional | Surface extremes; no atmospheric temperature comparable to Earth | No weather clouds | Solar-wind supply, impacts, surface migration, radioactive outgassing, spacecraft contamination |
| Mars | Thin atmosphere | ~95.3% CO₂, ~2.7% N₂, ~1.6% Ar; traces H₂O, CO, O₂ | ~0.006 bar mean, seasonally and topographically variable | Roughly −153 to 20 °C surface extremes | H₂O-ice and CO₂-ice clouds | Dust storms, polar cycles, weather, atmospheric escape, surface-atmosphere exchange |
| Titan | Dense atmosphere | About 95% N₂, about 5% CH₄ near the lower-atmosphere summary level; minor organics/H₂ | ~1.5–1.6 bar surface | ~−179 °C surface | CH₄/C₂H₆ clouds and liquids; organic haze | Methane weather, lakes/seas, photochemistry, haze production, seasonal change |
| Jupiter | Deep giant-planet atmosphere; no solid surface | ~90% H₂, ~10% He; traces CH₄, NH₃, H₂O, H₂S, PH₃ | Declare level for every comparison; 1 bar is a conventional atmospheric reference, not a surface | ~−110 °C near 1-bar level, approximate | NH₃ ice, likely NH₄SH and H₂O layers | Jets, belts/zones, long-lived vortices, lightning, internal heat, aurorae |
| Saturn | Deep giant-planet atmosphere; no solid surface | H₂ dominant, He secondary; traces CH₄, NH₃ and others; exact ratios depend on retrieval/model | Declared pressure level required; no surface pressure | ~−140 °C near 1-bar level, approximate | NH₃ and deeper condensate/cloud layers | Jets, storms, north-polar hexagon, internal heat, aurorae |
| Uranus | Ice-giant atmosphere; no solid surface | H₂ and He dominant; ~2% CH₄, traces H₂O/NH₃ in accessible/deeper contexts | Declared pressure level required | Minimum atmospheric temperature about 49 K (−224 °C); not a surface value | CH₄ ice and deeper cloud candidates | Seasonal/cloud change, high winds, tilted/off-center magnetosphere, weak internal heat flux |
| Neptune | Ice-giant atmosphere; no solid surface | H₂ and He dominant; ~1–2% CH₄, other species at trace/deeper levels | Declared pressure level required | ~−200 °C near 1-bar level, approximate | CH₄ ice clouds and deeper cloud candidates | Very high winds, transient dark vortices, internal heat, tilted magnetosphere |

## Comparability warnings

- **Surface pressure** is meaningful for Venus, Earth, Mars and Titan, but not
  as the same object for Jupiter, Saturn, Uranus or Neptune.
- **Exosphere composition** is not directly comparable to a well-mixed lower
  atmosphere.
- **Cloud composition** is not bulk atmospheric composition.
- **Trace abundance** may change with altitude, latitude, season and retrieval
  model.
- **Titan’s methane ratio** varies by altitude and convention; the atlas uses
  NASA’s public lower-atmosphere summary (~95% N₂, ~5% CH₄), not the sketch’s
  unexplained 98/1.5–2 split.
- **Temperatures** refer to different physical locations. Every future visual
  must label the reference plane.
