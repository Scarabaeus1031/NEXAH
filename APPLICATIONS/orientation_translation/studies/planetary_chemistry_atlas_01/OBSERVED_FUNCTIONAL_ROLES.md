# Observed Functional Roles

“Role” is classified by evidential status. A molecule can be observed while its
planet-wide function remains inferred or unknown.

## Observed

| Observation | Worlds | Evidence boundary |
|---|---|---|
| CO₂ is the dominant atmospheric constituent | Venus, Mars | Dominance is compositional, not a complete climate explanation |
| N₂ is the dominant atmospheric constituent | Earth, Titan | Shared dominance does not establish shared origin |
| H₂ and He dominate giant atmospheres | Jupiter, Saturn, Uranus, Neptune | Accessible atmosphere is not bulk interior |
| H₂O changes phase and participates in weather | Earth, Mars; clouds/deeper layers on giants | Phase and reservoir differ by world |
| Methane condenses and participates in surface-atmosphere weather | Titan | Methane source remains unresolved |
| Methane absorbs red wavelengths | Uranus, Neptune | Color is also affected by aerosols and image processing |
| Sulfuric-acid clouds occur | Venus | Cloud composition does not imply surface liquid acid |
| Dust is transported by atmospheric circulation | Mars | Dust activity does not establish long-term climate causation by itself |
| Exospheric species move between surface and space | Mercury, Moon | Collisionless motion is not weather |

## Likely / mechanism-supported

| Functional interpretation | Support | Remaining limitation |
|---|---|---|
| Dense CO₂ strongly contributes to Venus’s greenhouse state | Radiative physics plus observations | Full evolution and feedback history remain active research |
| Low pressure limits stable surface liquid water on present Mars | Phase behavior plus observed pressure/temperature | Local transient brines and subsurface reservoirs require separate treatment |
| Gravity and irradiation strongly influence atmospheric retention | Comparative physics and escape observations | Impact history, supply, chemistry and non-thermal escape prevent a single predictor |
| Internal heat contributes to giant-planet circulation | Measured energy budgets and dynamics | Relative contributions differ by world and depth |
| Titan haze is produced by N₂/CH₄ photochemistry | Cassini-Huygens observations and laboratory/photochemical models | Detailed pathways and methane resupply remain incomplete |
| Condensable species help organize cloud layers | Spectroscopy, thermochemistry and models | Deep abundances and cloud microphysics remain uncertain on giants |

## Unknown or unresolved

- What replenishes Titan’s atmospheric methane over geological time?
- What produces the unidentified ultraviolet absorbers in Venus’s clouds?
- What are the precise deep water, ammonia and rock fractions inside Uranus and
  Neptune?
- How do cloud microphysics and deep composition couple on each giant planet?
- What fraction of each rocky world’s atmospheric divergence comes from
  initial inventory, impacts, escape, volcanism, surface reactions and—in
  Earth’s case—life?
- How should magnetic topology be weighted relative to gravity, irradiation
  and atmospheric chemistry in long-term escape?

## Prohibited role shortcuts

The atlas does not label a species “for life,” “for climate,” or “for magnetic
protection” without a world-specific relation and an evidence class. Functional
language is always scoped to a process and a world.
