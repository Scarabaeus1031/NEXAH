# Future Research Questions

Only questions that arise from the admitted comparison are recorded. No answer
or new theory is proposed.

## Comparison measurement

1. Which pressure or altitude levels permit the least misleading comparison of
   giant-planet atmospheric composition?
2. How should uncertainty and vertical variability be displayed without
   overwhelming a first-time reader?
3. Which atmospheric ratios are robust across missions and which are retrieval-
   or model-dependent?
4. Can exospheres and collisional atmospheres share a visual frame without
   encouraging false equivalence?

## Atmospheric divergence

5. Which measured variables best explain the present contrast between Venus
   and Mars beyond their similar CO₂ mixing fractions?
6. Which evidence most strongly constrains the different origins and histories
   of N₂ on Earth and Titan?
7. How much of present atmospheric composition is inherited, supplied later,
   chemically transformed, sequestered, or lost?
8. Which observations can separate gravity, irradiation, chemistry, impact
   history and magnetic topology in atmospheric retention?

## Condensables and cycles

9. At what abstraction level is the Earth water-cycle / Titan methane-cycle
   analogy useful, and where does it fail?
10. How do trace condensables organize cloud structure in H₂/He-dominated
    atmospheres?
11. Which cloud species and vertical layers remain directly observed versus
    thermochemically inferred on each giant planet?
12. How does seasonal forcing alter the inventories accessible to remote
    sensing on Mars, Titan and Uranus?

## Energy and circulation

13. How should absorbed sunlight and internal heat be represented separately
    in comparisons of Jupiter, Saturn, Uranus and Neptune?
14. Why does Neptune sustain such vigorous winds under weak solar forcing?
15. Which circulation patterns recur across rocky, gas-giant, ice-giant and
    moon atmospheres without assuming identical mechanisms?

## Reader orientation

16. Does the layered atlas help readers distinguish composition from function
    better than isolated planet descriptions?
17. Which comparison path most reliably reveals a false inference?
18. Can readers identify why “same molecule” does not mean “same world”?
19. Do declared blocked comparisons improve understanding or merely add
    cognitive load?
20. Which questions do readers ask only after using the atlas?

## Candidate bounded continuations

No continuation is authorized here. If a follow-up is approved, the strongest
bounded candidates are:

- CO₂ Worlds: Venus ↔ Mars;
- N₂ Worlds: Earth ↔ Titan;
