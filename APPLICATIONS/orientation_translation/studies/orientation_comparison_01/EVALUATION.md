# Evaluation

This evaluates only the internal design of the comparison framework. It does not evaluate Photosynthesis, a future second pilot, or an Atlas.

| Dimension | Demonstrated | Plausible | Unknown |
|---|---|---|---|
| Independence preservation | The protocol freezes and references inputs rather than merging them. | Independent neighborhoods can remain intact during comparison. | Whether future users will follow the boundary consistently. |
| Traceability | Required records connect results to both local objects, evidence, versions, and reviewers. | A future result can be reproduced and audited. | Whether source granularity will be sufficient in practice. |
| Identity discipline | Identity tests distinguish label, role, function, referent, scope, scale, and version. | False matches can be reduced. | Inter-reviewer agreement and difficult identity cases. |
| Direction | Forward and reverse edges are separate comparison objects. | False reciprocity can be detected. | Performance on complex or cyclic relations. |
| Evidence preservation | Support remains attached to each neighborhood and cannot be transferred. | Support laundering can be prevented. | Consistency of support classifications across domains. |
| Role preservation | Role is compared independently from identity. | Same-node/different-role and different-node/same-role cases remain legible. | Whether a shared role vocabulary is useful or harmful. |
| Scale discipline | Scale alignment is required and incomparability is permitted. | Cross-scale confusion can be exposed. | A sufficient cross-domain scale description. |
| Asymmetry | The framework records differences as first-class outputs. | Asymmetry can provide greater orientation value than superficial matches. | Reader and research value of specific asymmetries. |
| Stopping and rejection | Both are mandatory comparison dimensions. | Negative decisions can survive Atlas preparation. | Comparability of different stopping procedures. |
| Failure containment | Ten required failure modes have explicit safeguards and tests. | Destructive comparison patterns can be identified before use. | Robustness under real comparison pressure. |
| Atlas restraint | Minimum eligibility and explicit insufficiencies are defined. | Atlas inflation can be resisted. | Whether two, several, or no compared neighborhoods justify an Atlas. |
| Automation | Machine-readable fields are specified. | Tools could assist inventory, validation, and diffing. | Safe automated candidate nomination; automatic decisions are not supported. |
| Reproducibility | The protocol is stepwise and version-aware. | Independent teams could execute comparable reviews. | Actual reproducibility, analyst independence, time cost, and disagreement rate. |

## Framework strengths

- dimension-specific comparison rather than global similarity;
- immutable inputs and external correspondence records;
- identity firewall;
- separate role and edge analysis;
- explicit reciprocity test;
- asymmetry, rejection, and stopping preserved;
- failure-closed outcomes including `incomparable` and `unresolved`;
- Atlas eligibility separated from Atlas construction.

## Framework limits

- it has not been executed on any pair;
- it has no measured reviewer agreement;
- it does not define a universal semantic or role vocabulary;
- it cannot validate scientific evidence automatically;
- it may be editorially expensive;
- its machine-readable schema is illustrative, not canonical;
- it does not establish that an Atlas is desirable.

## Assessment

The framework is internally coherent and ready to serve as a controlled comparison proposal. Its effectiveness, reproducibility, scalability, and reader value remain unknown until independently constructed neighborhoods exist and a separately authorized comparison is performed.
