## Cross-domain findings

### Stable functions

Boundary marking, representation separation, qualification, evidence/status
labeling, scope declaration, and traceability retain closely related functions
across the greatest number of domains.

### Purpose-dependent functions

- Alternative routes and return are strongest in reader maps and Library
  navigation.
- Comparison by contrast and asymmetry preservation are strongest in scientific
  and method-comparison studies.
- Status labeling is strongest in evidence studies, architecture, and governed
  editorial artifacts.
- Stopping conditions are strongest in bounded research studies, not general
  repository entry points.

### Non-equivalence

Cross-domain recurrence does not mean identical implementation. A scientific
scope boundary, repository authority boundary, chapter boundary, and reader
stop all help delimit orientation, but they remain different objects with
different consequences.
