# Limitations

## Evidence limitations

1. No reader behavior was observed.
2. “What becomes easier” is reconstructed from artifact purpose and explicit
   claims, not measured performance.
3. Absence pressures are documented editorial risks, not deterministic reader
   failures.
4. The input operations were preselected because they already recurred; this
   review cannot estimate the frequency of non-recurring alternatives.
5. Corpus sources share one repository, editorial lineage, and substantial
   prompt/template influence.
6. Cross-domain means several repository subject areas, not independent
   institutions, cultures, languages, or author communities.
7. Scientific domains are represented mainly by three biological pilots and
   one planetary study.
8. Book evidence is mediated through prior bounded reviews rather than a new
   page-level survey.
9. Architecture and documentation artifacts often describe intended reader
   function; intention is not demonstrated effect.
10. Internal traceability verifies lineage, not factual accuracy or external
    reproducibility.

## Analytical limitations

11. Functions may overlap even though the prompt prohibits merging operations.
12. “Boundary,” “scope,” “stopping,” and “qualification” are distinguishable
    here but may co-occur in one sentence or panel.
13. Helpful/optional assessments are comparative judgments, not experimental
    necessity tests.
14. No operation is demonstrated universally necessary.
15. Domain tables compress substantial within-domain variation.
16. Language-independence is hypothetical; no translation study was performed.
17. Style independence is supported by representation substitutions, but not
    tested through controlled redesign.
18. The review cannot determine whether users notice captions, legends, or
    non-implication text.
19. Return/open-question behavior has narrower evidence than boundary and
    traceability functions.
20. Dependency relations are enabling or strengthening observations, not a
    causal or computational graph.

## Authority limitations

21. The findings do not define or revise OLS.
22. The operation labels are descriptive and non-canonical.
23. The review does not define NEXAH, a visual language, or a method standard.
24. No Kernel, Registry, Operator, Architecture, Library, or Editorial OS
    authority is created.
25. No psychological, biological, perceptual, or cognitive mechanism is
    claimed.
26. `RECURRING ORIENTATION FUNCTIONS` is a bounded study disposition, not a
    conformance class or architecture status.

## Strongest unresolved question

Whether independent readers actually use these operations to distinguish
relations and evidence boundaries remains unknown.
