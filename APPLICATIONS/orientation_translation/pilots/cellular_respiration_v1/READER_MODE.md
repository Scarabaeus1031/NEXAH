# Reader Mode

## Cellular respiration: choose the question you actually have

The fixed source presents cellular respiration as a way cells transfer chemical energy from fuels into ATP through reactions involving electron acceptors and waste products. That one sentence contains several different maps.

### If you want to know what moves

Follow **Transfer**:

`fuel → reduced carriers → electron transport → gradient → ATP synthase → ATP`

This route helps separate electron movement from ATP formation. It is a simplified orientation path, not a complete chain for every organism.

### If you want to know where it happens

Follow **Space**:

`cytosol → mitochondrial matrix → inner membrane`

That route fits the source's detailed eukaryotic aerobic account. The same layout must not be assumed for prokaryotes.

### If “without oxygen” is confusing

Follow **Branches**:

- aerobic respiration uses oxygen as the terminal acceptor in the source account;
- anaerobic respiration uses other inorganic acceptors;
- fermentation is distinguished by the absence of an external electron acceptor.

These are related representations, not interchangeable names.

### If the ATP numbers do not agree

Follow **Yield**:

The source presents several totals and also names transport costs, proton leak, shuttle differences, and other assumptions. The pilot preserves that variation. It does not choose the scientifically correct number.

## Pause points

- Are you following a molecule, a pathway stage, a location, or an accounting convention?
- Has the scale changed?
- Is a transition directly described or only suggested by adjacency?
- Does the source mark a citation limitation?
- Which claim remains outside the current map?

Reader Mode is direct navigation. It hides most procedural bookkeeping but preserves the source's uncertainty and the pilot's limits.
