## Recurring source representations

### SR-01 — Transfer representation

- **Source-supported observation:** electrons move from biological fuels through carriers toward an electron acceptor.
- **Boundary:** the source does not provide one universal carrier sequence for every respiratory form.

### SR-02 — Ordered transformation representation

- **Source-supported observation:** the aerobic account is organized through glycolysis, pyruvate oxidation, citric acid cycle, and oxidative phosphorylation.
- **Boundary:** linear ordering simplifies branching, cycling, transport, and organism differences.

### SR-03 — Compartment representation

- **Source-supported observation:** locations differ across cytosol, mitochondrial matrix, inner membrane/cristae, and prokaryotic cytoplasm.
- **Boundary:** the source does not offer a complete comparative cell-biological map.

### SR-04 — Coupling representation

- **Source-supported observation:** source sections distinguish substrate-level phosphorylation from proton-gradient-driven ATP synthesis.
- **Boundary:** the pilot does not adjudicate biochemical efficiency.

### SR-05 — Conditional branch representation

- **Source-supported observation:** the source separates aerobic respiration, anaerobic respiration, and fermentation by acceptor and pathway conditions.
- **Boundary:** “without oxygen” does not make fermentation equivalent to anaerobic respiration.

### SR-06 — Yield and loss representation

- **Source-supported observation:** theoretical accounting is reduced by transport costs, proton leak, shuttle differences, and other factors named by the source.
- **Boundary:** the source's varying numbers are evidence of representational variation, not a resolved quantitative result.

## Visible omissions

- **Process observation:** regulation appears only incidentally despite detailed pathway description.
- **Process observation:** measurement appears mainly through yield accounting; experimental method is not developed.
- **Process observation:** organismal and ecosystem contexts are examples rather than a coherent higher-scale account.
- **Process observation:** the source names many linked representations that it does not itself explain.

No omission is repaired with external knowledge.
