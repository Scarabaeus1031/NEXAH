# Reader Mode

## Where are you?

You are looking at one large topic from three different distances. Photosynthesis can be studied inside a chloroplast, across living systems, or through the conditions affecting crops. Each view is useful; none is the whole subject.

## The shared core

Photosynthetic organisms capture light and convert some of its energy into chemical forms. In the familiar oxygen-producing process, light-dependent reactions use pigments and membrane systems to drive electron transfer, make ATP and NADPH, and split water, releasing oxygen. The Calvin cycle then uses ATP and NADPH to help incorporate carbon dioxide into organic compounds.

That description is a map, not the complete reality. Different organisms use different pigments, structures, electron donors, and carbon-fixation strategies.

## Three ways to continue

### A. Follow the mechanism

`light → electron transfer → ATP/NADPH → Calvin cycle → fixed carbon`

Use this path if your question is: **How does the process work?** Notice that the light reactions and Calvin cycle do different jobs but are connected through ATP and NADPH.

### B. Follow living systems

`photosynthetic organisms → biomass and food webs → ecosystems → oxygen and carbon cycles`

Use this path if your question is: **Why does this cellular process matter to life on Earth?** Keep cyanobacteria in the picture: photosynthesis is not only a plant process.

### C. Follow human context

`crop photosynthesis → productivity → light/water/CO₂/temperature constraints → food systems`

Use this path if your question is: **Why does photosynthesis matter in agriculture?** The article supports the links through crops and environmental limits. The final food-systems step is only an orientation bridge; farming outcomes also depend on many factors the article does not cover.

## Compare before concluding

- A mechanism view explains internal relations but can hide ecological scale.
- A living-systems view shows significance but can skip biochemical detail.
- A human-context view shows relevance but can overstate what photosynthesis alone determines.

No path is labeled best. You can move between them depending on the question you are trying to locate.
