# Pilot Report

## Result

The pilot produces a complete five-stage OLS-guided construction over one fixed public article. It preserves the source/observation, representation/reality, comparison/causality, orientation/recommendation, path/optimality, and explanation/proof boundaries in the written artifacts. It adds a meaningful orientation layer by separating three reader questions and making source basis, scale, uncertainty, and route limitations visible.

It does not demonstrate OLS conformance, biological correctness, improved learning, or reader preference. Those remain separate review tasks.

## Independent role review

### Secondary-school student perspective

- **Strength:** Reader Mode gives three recognizable questions and avoids beginning with full biochemical detail.
- **Risk:** terms such as NADPH, carbon fixation, and photorespiration still require teaching support.
- **Unknown:** whether the routes reduce confusion in an actual reading session.
- **Verdict:** suitable for a small reader test, not yet demonstrated as clearer.

### Domain expert perspective

- **Strength:** variants, scale shifts, and the separation of light reactions from carbon fixation are not erased.
- **Risk:** linear routes simplify networks; the Living Systems and Human Context paths need broader sources for full scientific coverage.
- **Unknown:** fidelity of all article claims because cited literature was not independently reviewed.
- **Verdict:** requires expert source-fidelity review before broader use.

### OLS reviewer perspective

- **Strength:** operator order, declarations, products, profile activation, provenance, and non-implications are explicit. `SELECT` and validation are not smuggled into the process.
- **Risk:** this is a human-authored interpretation of the contracts and may contain unnoticed completeness issues.
- **Unknown:** full OLS-5 test outcome.
- **Verdict:** correctly labeled informative; independent conformance review remains necessary.

### Skeptical visitor perspective

- **Strength:** the experiment states where its added structure comes from and exposes negative findings.
- **Risk:** much of the gain may be achieved by good educational editing without OLS terminology.
- **Unknown:** whether the formal traceability justifies its cognitive and maintenance cost.
- **Verdict:** compare Reader Mode against the baseline with real users before claiming distinct value.

## Required evaluation questions

1. **Did the result provide orientation rather than only summarization?** Yes. It adds declared reader position, eight separated lenses, comparisons, unresolved regions, and three source-bounded routes. The additional value is still untested with readers.
2. **Can the reader identify their current position?** Yes: basic biology, no specialist biochemistry, facing a cross-scale article and choosing a question rather than receiving a chosen route.
3. **Are perspectives visibly separated?** Yes in the eight-lens model and comparison matrix; three higher-level paths group them without merging them.
4. **Are evidence, provenance and uncertainty inspectable?** Yes through the fixed revision, section bases, evidence-class declaration, local limitations, and traceability table.
5. **Are boundaries and prohibited inferences explicit?** Yes at pilot, stage, route, and claim levels.
6. **Are several meaningful next paths available?** Yes: Mechanism, Living Systems, and Human Context, plus named neighboring representations.
7. **Is the reason for each path understandable?** Yes: each path records purpose, prerequisites, source basis, expected gain, limitations, alternative, and reason offered.
8. **Did Reader Mode remain faithful to Explain Mode?** Internally yes: both use the same orientation and source record. External audit remains pending.
9. **Did the application introduce any unsupported semantics?** It introduces editorial lenses and one under-supported bridge to food systems, both labeled as pilot constructions. No new OLS primitive or semantic authority is claimed.
10. **What failed or remained artificial?** The eight-lens scheme and clean linear paths are editorial simplifications; bookkeeping is heavy; wider routes have uneven source coverage; no reader, domain, or conformance test has run.
11. **Would a real reader know what to do next?** Reader Mode offers clear conditional entrances, but only observation with real students can establish whether they are usable.
12. **What should the second pilot test differently?** Use a source with a very different form, pre-register reader tasks, include one domain expert before mapping, and measure whether orientation questions improve without exposing OLS bookkeeping to the reader.

Supporting controls remain explicit: the source is reproducibly fixed; the complete universal order is used; Representation is dependency-activated while Navigation and Education are explicit; `OP-SELECT` is absent; no learning or OLS conformance is claimed.

## Next bounded test

Run a small comparison in which students answer “where am I, what connects, and where can I continue?” after reading either the baseline or Reader Mode. Separately ask a biology reviewer to audit fidelity and an OLS reviewer to inspect contracts. Do not combine these into one success metric.
