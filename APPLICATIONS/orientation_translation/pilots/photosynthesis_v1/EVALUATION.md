# Evaluation

## Comparison with the baseline

| Criterion | Baseline summary | Orientation translation | Finding |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Concision | Strong | Lower | Baseline is faster for a general overview |
| Mechanism continuity | Good | Strong and explicitly routed | Orientation adds a visible path and prerequisites |
| Scale awareness | Present but compressed | Explicit across three lenses | Orientation makes scale changes easier to inspect |
| Reader position | Implicit general reader | Explicit age/knowledge boundary | Orientation is more situated without psychographic inference |
| Alternatives | Not explicit | Three non-ranked routes | Orientation supports multiple questions |
| Provenance | One final caveat | Source basis attached to routes and stages | Orientation is more auditable |
| Uncertainty | General source caveat | Local limits and unsupported bridge exposed | Orientation better preserves boundaries |
| Cognitive load | Lower | Higher | Full Explain Mode is not an entry-level reading experience |
| Measured learning | None | None | Neither artifact establishes learning |

## Does OLS add a meaningful difference?

Yes, but narrowly. The main gain is not more biological content. It is explicit separation of source, representation, comparison, orientation, explanation, reader position, authority, and uncertainty. The three routes expose why a reader might move through different parts of the same article.

The experiment also reveals costs. OLS bookkeeping is substantial. If exposed too early, it can obscure the subject. Reader Mode therefore needs to remain short while Explain Mode carries audit detail. The pilot’s lens design is editorial judgment, not a mechanical consequence of the source.

## Negative findings

1. The baseline is more efficient when the reader only wants a compact definition.
2. One Wikipedia article cannot fully support the Living Systems and Human Context routes at equal depth.
3. The food-systems endpoint is under-supported by the primary source and must remain a bridge, not a finding.
4. No student has tested whether these paths improve orientation.
5. No biologist has independently checked source fidelity or scientific omissions.
6. No independent OLS reviewer has performed a complete OLS-5 conformance assessment.
7. The paths may give a false appearance of clean linearity to a highly interconnected process.
8. Article maintenance warnings show why tertiary-source status must remain visible.

## Evaluation questions

- **Is this more than a summary?** Yes: it exposes position, lenses, alternatives, transitions, provenance, and limits.
- **Is it already a proven educational application?** No.
- **Does it preserve source status?** By design and in every artifact, yes; independent review remains pending.
- **Can it replace the source article?** No. It is an orientation layer over one fixed revision.
- **Should it proceed?** Only to bounded reader and expert review, without a conformance or learning claim.
