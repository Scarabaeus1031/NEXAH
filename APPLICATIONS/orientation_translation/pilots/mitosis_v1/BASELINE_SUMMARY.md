# Baseline Summary

Mitosis is the part of the eukaryotic cell cycle in which replicated chromosomes are separated into two daughter nuclei while chromosome number is maintained. It follows DNA replication during interphase. The article commonly describes prophase, prometaphase, metaphase, anaphase, and telophase, with a plant-specific preprophase in some accounts.

Chromosomes condense, spindle structures form, microtubules attach to kinetochores, chromosomes align around the metaphase plate, sister chromatids separate and move toward opposite sides, and nuclear envelopes form around the daughter chromosome sets. Cytokinesis is a separate process that may follow or overlap mitotic progression to divide the cell; it does not always occur.

The article also describes open, closed, and semi-open forms of mitosis, different spindle arrangements, checkpoints, errors such as nondisjunction and multipolar mitosis, and wider functions in growth, replacement, regeneration, and asexual reproduction.

## What the baseline foregrounds

- the familiar phase sequence;
- chromosome separation;
- spindle-mediated movement;
- two daughter nuclei and often two daughter cells;
- normal completion.

## What it compresses

- multiple events occurring within each phase;
- changes in chromosome naming and state;
- spatial reorganization across nucleus and cell;
- organismal differences in nuclear-envelope behavior;
- the distinction between condition, cue, cause, and guarantee;
- the boundary between Mitosis and Cytokinesis;
- abnormal and incomplete outcomes.

## Hidden questions

Where exactly does each event occur? Which names refer to phases, structures, states, or outcomes? What permits progression without guaranteeing it? What persists as chromosomes change name and position? When does nuclear division end and cell division begin? How should variants be navigated without treating the standard diagram as universal?

Sequence dominates the conventional summary. It is readable, but it does not make all spatial, conditional, terminological, and outcome boundaries easy to locate.
