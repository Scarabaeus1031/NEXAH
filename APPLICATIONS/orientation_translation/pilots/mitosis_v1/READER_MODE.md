# Reader Mode

## Where are you?

Mitosis is represented here as nuclear division inside a wider cell-cycle context. DNA replication precedes it. Cytokinesis may accompany or follow it, but the source explicitly treats Cytokinesis as a separate process that does not always occur.

## Choose a question

- **What happens next?** Follow the named phase landmarks.
- **What happens to the chromosomes?** Track continuity and changing state labels.
- **Where does it happen?** Follow spindle, attachments, plate, poles, and nuclear boundaries.
- **What permits continuation?** Inspect attachment, alignment, and checkpoint language.
- **What else can happen?** Open the variation and outcome route.
- **Where does Mitosis stop?** Use the process-boundary route.

## Essential distinctions

- Phase ≠ state.
- Temporal succession ≠ causality.
- Alignment ≠ successful final outcome.
- Checkpoint condition ≠ guarantee.
- Daughter nucleus ≠ daughter cell.
- Mitosis ≠ Cytokinesis.
- A standard diagram ≠ every organismal form.
