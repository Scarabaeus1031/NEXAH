# Transition Geometry — Concept-Family Test

**Phase:** X1 · reader/explain evaluation  
**Status:** human review required · non-canonical  
**Scope:** Transition Geometry, JANUS, Inbetween, Boundary, Aperture, Balance  
**Evaluated:** 2026-07-16

## Purpose

This test asks whether the reviewed Concept-family evidence can answer real
reader questions before NEXAH creates Concept identities, graph edges, or new
Kernel behavior.

It evaluates two answer layers:

- **Reader Mode** gives a short, useful orientation without internal IDs;
- **Explain Mode** reveals the editorial synthesis, provenance, uncertainty,
  and identity boundaries behind that orientation.

The test does not implement a graph. Paths below are curated documentary
routes, not canonical relations.

## Evaluation model

Each question is checked against six requirements:

1. **clarity** — a reader can understand the answer without schema knowledge;
2. **evidence** — the answer points to identified Works or repository sources;
3. **identity separation** — Concept, Operator, Work, method, and motif remain
   distinct;
4. **provenance** — authored, architectural, research, and historical claims
   remain distinguishable;
5. **uncertainty** — hypotheses and unsupported universal claims stay visible;
6. **navigation** — the answer offers a meaningful next place to go.

Result states:

| State | Meaning |
|---|---|
| `pass` | direct reviewed evidence supports the answer and route |
| `pass_with_editorial_synthesis` | the answer is useful, but its sequence is a human-curated synthesis rather than a stored relation |
| `pass_with_concept_boundary` | the answer succeeds by explaining that the apparent Concept is not one coherent identity |

## CFQ-01 — What is Transition Geometry?

### Reader Mode

> **Transition Geometry is NEXAH's way of describing the structured
> in-between: the relations, boundaries, apertures, paths, and constraints
> through which one state may become another.**

It is not one universal space. Different Works use different declared
representations—fields, graphs, basins, corridors, thresholds, trajectories,
or visual maps—to make a transition inspectable.

**Begin with:** THE CARTOGRAPHY LABORATORY for the three-part overview, then
GEOMETRIA NOVA for the wider language and OPERATIONAL GEOMETRY OF TRANSITION
for the Atlas treatment.

### Explain Mode

The answer is the accepted working definition from the Transition Geometry
Family Review. It synthesizes evidence from GEOMETRIA NOVA, THE OPERATOR, THE
OPERATOR LIBRARY, THE CARTOGRAPHY LABORATORY, OPERATIONAL GEOMETRY OF
TRANSITION, the Whiteboards, Research, and Architecture.

The governing boundary is essential: geometry is a **declared relational
representation**, not the represented reality. Current evidence supports a
family of representation-specific transition models. It does not support one
representation-independent `TransitionSpace`, a universal cross-domain law,
or equivalence between equations printed in Works and implemented methods.

**Result:** `pass`

## CFQ-02 — How is JANUS related to Transition Geometry?

### Reader Mode

> **Transition Geometry describes a possible passage. JANUS helps inspect
> that passage from complementary sides without collapsing them into one
> view.**

In THE CARTOGRAPHY LABORATORY, JANUS evaluates direction, aperture, mismatch,
and viability; Atlas Reconstruction maps the larger landscape; Geometry of
Transition describes change through that landscape. These are related roles,
not three names for the same thing.

**Continue with:** the JANUS Concept Dossier, then THE CARTOGRAPHY LABORATORY.

### Explain Mode

The relation is a review proposal, not a graph edge. JANUS is itself layered:

- the broader JANUS principle preserves complementary perspectives;
- `NX-OP-0016` is the existing controlled Janus Operator;
- the Janus Bridge is planned and distinct;
- the Directional Coherence Operator is experimental and method-specific.

The answer therefore says that Transition Geometry may be **interpreted
through** the JANUS principle. It does not make JANUS the geometry, identify
the principle with the controlled Operator, or treat the laboratory formulas
as validated universal mechanics.

**Result:** `pass`

## CFQ-04 — Show me the path from Boundary to Transition.

### Reader Mode

A useful NEXAH path is:

```text
Difference
    ↓
Boundary
    ↓
Opening / Aperture
    ↓
Threshold
    ↓
Inbetween / Passage
    ↓
Transition
    ↓
New State, Return, or Block
```

Read THE OPERATOR LIBRARY for the longer evolution of an Operator, then the
Whiteboards THE THRESHOLD and THE JANUS GATE, and finally THE CARTOGRAPHY
LABORATORY for gates, bottlenecks, viability, and transition.

### Explain Mode

The route combines two documented sequences:

- THE OPERATOR LIBRARY moves from difference and boundary through opening,
  threshold, transition, path, bridge, network, and field;
- the Whiteboards move from Inbetween through Threshold and Janus Gate toward
  a new configuration.

The final alternatives—transition, block, or return—come from the provisional
Transition Geometry family model. This is not a deterministic state machine
and not the Whiteboard hypothesis `Region → Boundary → Transition → Closure`.
That four-part recurrence remains explicitly a hypothesis; boundary types also
remain heterogeneous.

Controlled Operators such as Boundary `NX-OP-0006`, Aperture `NX-OP-0002`,
Transition `NX-OP-0005`, Bridge `NX-OP-0008`, and Relation `NX-OP-0011` can
explain parts of the route, but their coexistence does not create a canonical
graph path.

**Result:** `pass_with_editorial_synthesis`

### Reader Mode

Use this five-stop route:

   The clearest visual entrance: opening, thread, passage, orientation.
2. **NEXAH WHITEBOARD SERIES — THE INBETWEEN / THE THRESHOLD / THE JANUS

4. **THE CARTOGRAPHY LABORATORY**  
   Aperture inside the JANUS, Atlas Reconstruction, and Transition Geometry

5. **OPERATIONAL GEOMETRY OF TRANSITION**  
   The extended visual Atlas and Aperture Series.

For the stricter research boundary, continue to
`RESEARCH/CORE_CONCEPTS/aperture_geometry.md`.

### Explain Mode

The route separates Work-level explanation from Research-level definition.
The controlled Aperture Operator `NX-OP-0002` provides a stable existing
identity, while Research distinguishes **access**—where passage can occur—from
JANUS questions about directional organization and transition activation.

GEOMETRIA NOVA and THE OPERATOR LIBRARY provide important surrounding grammar
and form taxonomies, but the five stops above give the clearest progression
from visual introduction to extended Atlas. None of these Works expands the
controlled Operator Registry automatically.

**Result:** `pass`

## CFQ-06 — What does Balance mean in NEXAH?

### Reader Mode

> **Balance is not one fixed NEXAH geometry. Across the Library it usually
> means that different directions, forces, or forms of motion remain in a
> viable relation without one simply erasing the other.**

Some Works show this as a figure-eight, paired JANUS axes, a Relation Tube, or
OLGO regulation. These are related Balance models, not yet one canonical
Concept.

**Explore the differences:** THE DUAL SPINE for relational balance,
LIBRARYBOOK for directional balance, THE OPERATOR LIBRARY for regulatory
balance, and GEOMETRIA NOVA for the figure-eight visual family.

### Explain Mode

The Geometry of Balance Evidence Review found one historical source with the
exact title and at least seven related models: resonant drift, relational,
directional, regulatory, figure-eight, dynamical compensation, and
equilibrium. Their family resemblance is meaningful, but their identity is not
established.

Balance must remain distinct from equilibrium, stability, closure, coherence,
and resonance. OLGO and the figure-eight are possible realizations or motifs,
not definitions. There is no Balance Concept ID, Registry record, graph edge,
or current Concept Census entry.

**Result:** `pass_with_concept_boundary`

## What the test demonstrates

The Concept-family layer adds value before graph implementation. It can:

1. answer what an idea means across several Works;
2. keep related but distinct roles apart;
3. show the development of an idea as a curated path;
4. connect reader language to exact documentary evidence;
5. make uncertainty and non-results useful rather than invisible.

The most important result is not that every question has a node. It is that
each answer can say **what is known, how it is connected, and where the
connection remains editorial rather than canonical**.

## Current limitation

This test is a human-authored review artifact. The current Kernel does not yet
derive these answers from a Concept overlay. Repeatable machine navigation
would require an approved, minimal representation of:

- reviewed Concept-family handles;
- evidence-backed occurrences;
- typed relation proposals;
- curated Concept Paths;
- answer status and claim boundaries.

That representation must preserve the distinction between evidence and
editorial synthesis. It must not infer graph truth from co-occurrence.
