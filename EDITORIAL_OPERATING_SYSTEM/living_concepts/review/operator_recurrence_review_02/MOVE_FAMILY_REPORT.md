# Operator Recurrence Review 02 — Move Family Report

The families below emerged from observable functions before authored labels
were compared. They are descriptive Work-level findings, not Registry terms or
canonical categories.

## F1 — Situated attention

### Evidence

All four independent units establish a view, witness a field, or sense another
participant before or during transformation.

### Assessment

**Strongly supported.** Passive inspection, active sensing, and witnessing are
adjacent but not proven equivalent.

## F2 — Distinction and relation

### Evidence

All units form relations; only two explicitly divide or distinguish first.
Some relations are links, while others are reciprocal, bridged, or interwoven.

### Assessment

Relation formation is **strongly supported**. Prior separation is **locally
supported** and not a necessary first step.

## F3 — Frame, counterpart, and sidedness change

### Evidence

Every unit changes directional reference or participant position and depicts a
counterpart, reflection, reversal, inside/outside exchange, or role exchange.

### Assessment

The broad family is **strongly supported**. The corpus contradicts collapsing
rotation, reflection, reversal, and exchange into one operation.

## F4 — Form and continuity change

### Evidence

Three units explicitly reorganize, fold, twist, or compact form. The Butterfly
Work changes relational trajectories while maintaining the shared process.

### Assessment

**Strongly supported as a broad family**, with uncertainty about whether
relational interweaving is the same kind of form change as folding or twisting.

## F5 — Representation change

### Evidence

Three independent units explicitly map a state into another surface, chart, or
dimensional presentation. The Butterfly Work does not require this function.

### Assessment

**Strongly supported but optional.** Projection, mapping, and translation are
not treated as automatically equivalent.

## F6 — Alignment and coherence

### Evidence

All four units depict patterns becoming aligned, amplified, coordinated, or
coherent.

### Assessment

**Strongly supported as editorial function.** A shared measurable resonance
mechanism is not established.

## F7 — Cyclic continuity

### Evidence

Return, re-entry, homecoming, release, or another cycle appears strongly in
three units and locally in the fourth.

### Assessment

**Strongly supported and optional.** Release and Return retain different roles.

## F8 — Reciprocal adaptation

### Evidence

The Living Library and Butterfly units explicitly change orientation through
mutual or environmental response. The paired synthesis offers opposition and
counteraction but not the same adaptive process.

### Assessment

**Locally supported.** This is a coherent relational development, not a
corpus-wide base operation.

## F9 — Trace and memory

### Evidence

Memory, retention, trails, and field traces occur in the Living Library and
locally in the general cycle. They are absent from the other independent units.

### Assessment

**Locally supported.** Remembering may be a state, capacity, or outcome rather
than a Move.

## F10 — Selection and composition

### Evidence

Every independent unit presents subsets, stacks, cycles, or sequences of
functions. Different selections lead to different outcomes, and no unit
requires the full palette.

### Assessment

**Strongly supported meta-function.** This is the only entirely new recurring
function produced by the independent reconstruction. It describes how the
Works use Moves; it is not thereby another Move.

## Family conclusion

The corpus supports a reusable ecology of functions. It does not support a
closed alphabet or formal grammar. What recurs most strongly is the practice of
selecting and composing transformations of view, relation, form,
representation, coherence, and continuity.
