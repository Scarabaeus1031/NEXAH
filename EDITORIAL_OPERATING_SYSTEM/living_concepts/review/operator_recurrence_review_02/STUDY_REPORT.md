# OPERATOR RECURRENCE REVIEW 02

## Objective

Determine whether recurring orientation functions emerge independently from
the supplied Work corpus when reconstructions begin with observable behavior,
editorial dependence is corrected, and no prior Move vocabulary or Registry
terminology is used as evidence.

## Why this matters

The review tests methodological dependence. A pattern found only after one
taxonomy has already named it may be an artifact of that taxonomy. A pattern
that reappears from neutral reconstructions is more robust as a description of
the authored corpus, even though it remains non-canonical.

This review does not validate mechanisms. It replicates an editorial-corpus
observation.

## Method result

Seven files resolve into four independent editorial units. Each unit was first
reconstructed as observable functions. Authored labels were mapped only after
the function matrix and support decisions were complete.

## Independent finding

The corpus independently yields recurrent functions involving:

- situated attention;
- relation formation;
- directional reference change;
- counterpart reflection;
- sidedness and form transformation;
- representation change;
- alignment or coherence;
- cyclic continuity;
- selection and composition.

The first eight correspond only later to familiar Work-level Move language.
The ninth—selection and composition—is a newly explicit meta-function: every
independent unit selects subsets of transformations and combines them into
different paths or outcomes.

After the function-first analysis, the strongest recurring Work-level actions
can be described as:

- situate or observe;
- connect;
- rotate;
- mirror;
- twist;
- fold;
- project;
- resonate;
- return.

`Flip`, `Separate`, `Adapt`, `Co-orient`, and `Emerge` recur more narrowly or
with less stable roles. `Zoom`, `Layer`, `Counterrotate`, `Remember`, and the
Butterfly cycle vocabulary remain local or family-specific.

## Support classes

### Strongly supported

Observation, relation, frame change, reflection, broad form transformation,
representation change, alignment/coherence, cyclic continuity, and selective
composition.

### Locally supported

Prior separation, reciprocal adaptation, co-orientation, memory/trace, and
scale change.

### Uncertain

Universal counterbalance, a common Layer function, threshold/release as one
operation, and emergence as a selected Move.

### Contradicted

A fixed alphabet, mandatory ordering, universal necessity of individual Moves,
equivalence of adjacent functions, one shared geometry, and one mechanism
across example domains.

## Grammar test

The independent corpus does not establish:

- a fixed alphabet size;
- a universal Move;
- one required ordering;
- shared composition rules;
- invalid composition rules;
- a common formal semantics;
- one mechanism across domains.

It does establish that the Works repeatedly present orientation as selecting
and composing changes of attention, relation, frame, form, representation, and
coherence.

## Comparison with previously known Work-level vocabulary

Only after the independent result was fixed was it compared with Operator
Recurrence Review 01. The central vocabulary reappears at the functional level:
view/sensing, connection, rotation, reflection, twisting, folding, projection,
resonance/alignment, and return/cyclic continuity.

The independent review also reproduces the earlier limitations:

- several files are editorially dependent;
- some apparent Moves are local;
- similar labels carry different functions;
- composition recurs without a fixed grammar;
- Work recurrence does not create canonical authority.

The explicit emergence of selection/composition sharpens the explanation of
how the vocabulary is used but does not expand the Move inventory. It therefore
does not change the comparative disposition.

## Confirmations

- A recurring vocabulary emerges independently.
- Its functional center closely matches the first review.
- Transformations recur more strongly than objects or geometries.
- Composition recurs, while syntax remains unfixed.
- Adaptive and collective Moves remain a bounded subfamily.
- Work recurrence does not create Registry authority.

## Contradictions and qualifications

- Correcting for editorial dependence lowers raw recurrence relative to a
  seven-file count.
- Some borderline Moves change support class.
- `Emerge` cannot be assigned one stable grammatical role.
- Several apparently central Moves have explicit counterexamples and remain
  optional.

These qualifications constrain the strength of the conclusion but do not
overturn the independently reconstructed vocabulary.

## Answer

The recurring vocabulary emerges again without name-first searching. NEXAH's
visual Works recur more strongly through transformations and their selective
composition than through stable objects or a single representation geometry.
The result remains descriptive, Work-level, and non-canonical.

## Authority boundary

No Registry, controlled Operator, Living Concept, OLS, Kernel, Architecture,
Overlay, Are.na state, or canonical terminology was changed or inferred.
