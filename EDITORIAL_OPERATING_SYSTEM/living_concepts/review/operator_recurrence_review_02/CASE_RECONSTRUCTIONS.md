# Operator Recurrence Review 02 — Case Reconstructions

Status: non-canonical independent replication review  
Primary evidence: supplied Work visuals only

Each case begins with observable behavior. Authored Move names are introduced
only in a second, explicitly separated pass.

## Independence correction

The seven supplied artifacts are not counted as seven independent Works.
Repeated layout, palette order, examples, and explanatory structure indicate
four independent editorial units for recurrence counting:

| Independent unit | Supplied artifacts | Reason for grouping |
|---|---|---|
| IU-A — General action grammar | Operator Map v2.1; The Moves; Operations of Orientation | shared numbered palette, stack logic, examples, and reader procedure |
| IU-B — Living operator library | Living Library of Orientation Operators; Operator Library v1.0 | shared natural exemplars, palettes, and stack outcomes |
| IU-C — Paired and cyclic synthesis | Language of Moves | distinct organization around opposition, balance, cyclicity, and cross-domain pairs |
| IU-D — Reciprocal relational case | Butterfly Operator | focused two-participant process and reciprocal adaptation cycle |

Within-unit repetition is recorded as authored persistence, but it counts once
for cross-Work recurrence.

## IU-A — General action grammar

### Pass 1: observable functions

Across its three artifacts, this unit repeatedly depicts:

- holding or establishing a view;
- dividing a field into distinguishable parts;
- creating relations between parts;
- changing a reference direction;
- reflecting a configuration across a plane or line;
- reversing face, side, or polarity;
- shearing or exchanging inside and outside while retaining continuity;
- compacting or reorganizing form;
- mapping a form into another surface, dimension, or chart;
- changing scale or stacking levels in one variant;
- bringing patterns into alignment;
- selecting and composing subsets of these functions;
- in one artifact, returning through a cycle.

The examples omit and reorder functions. No complete sequence is required.

### Pass 2: Work-level vocabulary

The artifacts later name these functions `Observe`, `Separate`, `Connect`,
`Rotate`, `Mirror`, `Flip`, `Twist`, `Fold`, `Project`, `Zoom`, `Layer`,
`Resonate`, and locally `Return`.

### Internal contradictions

- The headline palettes contain different members.
- `Zoom` and `Layer` occur in only one variant.
- `Return` is explicit in Operator Map v2.1 but absent from the other two
  primary palettes.
- The numbered order is not preserved by the example stacks.

## IU-B — Living operator library

### Pass 1: observable functions

This unit depicts:

- sensing a field or another participant;
- distinguishing and linking actors;
- changing direction or viewpoint;
- reflecting or exchanging roles;
- changing sidedness and continuity;
- reorganizing form;
- mapping a state into another representation;
- amplifying or coordinating a pattern;
- returning or re-entering a cycle;
- adapting to another actor or environment;
- coordinating without a central controller;
- locking, releasing, hovering, flowing, and retaining traces in local
  biological metaphors;
- composing different subsets to yield different outcomes.

### Pass 2: Work-level vocabulary

The artifacts name a shared core including `Observe`, `Connect`, `Rotate`,
`Mirror`, `Twist`, `Fold`, `Project`, `Resonate`, and `Return`. One artifact
also names `Separate` and `Flip`. The exemplar layer adds `Pair`, `Adapt`,
`Co-orient`, `Hover`, `Flow`, `Lock`, `Release`, `Map`, `Remember`, and
`Emerge`.

### Internal contradictions

- The two headline palettes are not identical.
- A function may be a general action in one artifact and a metaphor-specific
  behavior in the other.
- Emergence alternates between an action label and an outcome.

## IU-C — Paired and cyclic synthesis

### Pass 1: observable functions

This Work depicts:

- changing direction relative to a reference;
- applying an opposed or counter-direction;
- reversing face or orientation;
- exchanging inside and outside;
- reorganizing or compacting form;
- reflecting a state into a counterpart;
- mapping into another chart or surface;
- returning without treating change as termination;
- aligning patterns;
- balancing action with counteraction;
- constructing larger structures from smaller allowed actions.

### Pass 2: Work-level vocabulary

The headline vocabulary names `Rotate`, `Counterrotate`, `Flip`, `Twist`,
`Fold`, `Mirror`, `Project`, `Return`, and `Resonate`. A composed stack adds
`Observe`; a construction example uses `Bridge` and connection language.

### Internal contradictions

- Observation is structurally central but not part of the headline row.
- Counterrotation is not demonstrated as the inverse of every rotation.
- Cross-domain examples share visual roles, not proven mechanisms.

## IU-D — Reciprocal relational case

### Pass 1: observable functions

This Work depicts two participants that:

- detect one another and a shared field;
- approach and orbit;
- respond to each other's movement;
- mutually adjust;
- exchange roles;
- interweave trajectories;
- form a shared pattern;
- release and begin another cycle;
- retain no single leader or fixed path.

A secondary sequence selects frame change, inside/outside exchange,
reflection, alignment, reciprocal orientation, and return.

### Pass 2: Work-level vocabulary

The central cycle names `Approach`, `Orbit`, `Response`, `Interweave`,
`Exchange`, and `Release`. A process sequence names `Sense`, `Move`, `Adapt`,
`Co-orient`, `Emerge`, `Evolve`, and `Return`. Its secondary stack names
`Observe`, `Rotate`, `Twist`, `Mirror`, `Resonate`, `Co-orient`, and `Return`.

### Internal contradictions

- The Work functions without explicit separation, folding, projection, or
  reversal.
- Its central unit is reciprocal adjustment, not a solitary geometric change.
- Emergence is both a process stage and the result of preceding interaction.

## Pre-comparison synthesis

Before consulting any earlier review, four observations follow from the corpus:

1. No stable object inventory spans the four independent units.
2. No single geometry spans them.
3. Comparable changes of attention, relation, frame, form, representation,
   alignment, and cyclic continuity recur across different geometries.
4. The corpus supports selection and composition, but not one mandatory syntax.
