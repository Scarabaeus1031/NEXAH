## Decision

The review does **not** find one current, stable NEXAH Concept called Geometry
of Balance.

It finds:

1. one explicit historical formulation named **GEOMETRY OF BALANCE**;
2. several current Work-level models of balance;
3. experimental uses that measure or simulate different kinds of balance;
4. a recurring figure-eight and counter-direction visual language;
5. no single definition that makes those uses equivalent.

The appropriate outcome is therefore:

> **Multiple related Balance models organized around a shared motif—not one
> canonical Geometry of Balance.**

This review creates no identity, Registry record, Operator, or graph edge.

## Provisional shared thread

The reviewed uses share a cautious editorial pattern:

> **Balance describes a maintained relation between non-identical tendencies
> in which neither side is simply erased and the combined behavior remains
> within a viable or intelligible pattern.**

This is a synthesis for comparison, not a Concept definition. Individual
sources disagree about what is balanced, what persistence means, and whether
the result is static, oscillatory, resonant, bounded, or navigational.

## Required distinctions

### Balance versus equilibrium

**Equilibrium** is a state or configuration in which net change may vanish or
a system rests at a stable attractor. The Gradient System documentation uses
this conventional scoped meaning.

**Dynamic balance** may contain continuous movement, oscillation, expansion,
contraction, or drift. Net or long-term behavior can remain bounded without
the trajectory becoming stationary.

Therefore:

```text
equilibrium may be one balance condition
but balance does not imply equilibrium
```

### Balance versus stability

Current Architecture represents stability as spatial structure: stable
regions, weak boundaries, and transition corridors. Experimental formulations
also describe coherent motion within a structured field.

Balance may be a **mechanism associated with persistence**, while stability is
the persistence or viable structural condition being assessed. A stable state
need not be produced by counterbalancing tendencies, and a momentarily balanced
system need not be robustly stable.

### Balance versus closure

Closure concerns return, recurrence, containment, or the preservation of a
coherent region or identity. Balance can help maintain closure; closure can
also arise from boundaries, feedback, attraction, or topology without a
declared balance relation.

The Architecture also uses “closure” for completed evidence and work packages.
That governance meaning must never be merged with geometric closure.

### Balance versus coherence

Research defines local coherence as alignment between observed motion and a
declared flow geometry. It is a scoped alignment measure.

Balance concerns the relation among tendencies. Two opposing flows can be
balanced while locally incoherent; a single strongly aligned flow can be
coherent without being balanced against another flow.

### Balance versus resonance

Resonance may describe phase/frequency coupling, a structural recurrence, a
named research program, or an editorial metaphor. The existing Concept review
already finds these meanings heterogeneous.

Resonance can produce or support a balance condition, but frequency coupling
is not synonymous with balance. OLGO's resonance-band formulation is therefore
one possible Balance model, not the definition of Balance.
