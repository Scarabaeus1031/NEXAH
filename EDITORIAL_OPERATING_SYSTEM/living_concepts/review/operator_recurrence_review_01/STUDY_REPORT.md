# OPERATOR RECURRENCE REVIEW 01

## Objective

Determine whether the supplied authored visual corpus contains a recurring
grammar of orientation Moves independent of any single representation
geometry, without extending the Registry or canonical architecture.

## Corpus

Seven supplied visual artifacts were reviewed:

1. Orientation Operator Map v2.1 (embedded in the supplied TextClipping);
2. The Moves: A Grammar of Orientation;
3. The Operations of Orientation;
4. The Living Library of Orientation Operators;
5. NEXAH Operator Library v1.0;
6. Orientation Library: The Language of Moves;
7. The Butterfly Operator.

They were treated as Work-level editorial evidence. Closely related visual
variants were not counted as fully independent Works.

## Main finding

The corpus recurs most strongly through **transformations and orientation
functions**, not through stable objects or one representation geometry.

The following Work-level Moves remain recurrent after correcting for visual
and editorial dependence:

- Observe / Sense;
- Connect;
- Rotate;
- Mirror;
- Flip, with narrower and definition-sensitive recurrence;
- Twist;
- Fold;
- Project;
- Resonate;
- Return.

The corpus also supports bounded local developments:

- `Separate` in the core grammar family;
- `Zoom` and `Layer` in one palette;
- `Counterrotate` in the balance-oriented synthesis;
- `Adapt` and `Co-orient` in living/relational Works;
- `Remember`, `Lock`, `Release`, `Hover`, `Flow`, and related natural metaphors
  in local examples.

## Vocabulary versus grammar

A vocabulary recurs when comparable functions remain recognizable across
different artifacts. A grammar would additionally require stable roles,
composition constraints, ordering, admissibility rules, or transformation
contracts.

The visuals repeatedly encourage Move composition, but their stacks:

- contain different Moves;
- use different orders;
- omit allegedly fundamental Moves;
- produce different outcomes;
- do not define invalid compositions;
- do not establish a shared formal semantics.

Therefore the corpus supports a recurring vocabulary, not yet a recurring
grammar in the stronger sense.

## Move families

Eight bounded editorial families were observed:

1. observation and attention;
2. distinction and relation;
3. frame and sidedness transformation;
4. form and continuity transformation;
5. representation and scale;
6. resonance and alignment;
7. return and cyclic continuity;
8. adaptive and collective orientation.

The first seven recur across multiple editorial families. The eighth is a
later, more local development. These families are descriptive findings, not a
new taxonomy.

## What is strongly supported

- NEXAH's authored visual language repeatedly describes knowledge work as
  selectable transformations of view, relation, form, or representation.
- Several Move labels survive changes of geometry, metaphor, domain, and
  cardinality.
- Moves are commonly composed into stacks or cycles.
- Different stacks may lead to different forms of orientation.
- Observation commonly precedes or conditions transformation.
- Resonance and Return commonly act as outcomes or closing Moves, but neither
  is proven mandatory.

## What is not supported

- a universal Operator language;
- a fixed number of Moves;
- a mandatory sequence;
- one algebra shared across all Moves;
- identical mechanisms across example domains;
- automatic equivalence between Work-level Moves and Registry Operators;
- promotion of Butterfly, natural metaphors, or Move stacks into canonical
  semantics.

## Registry and authority boundary

This review concerns authored Work vocabulary only. It does not alter or
interpret the controlled Operator Registry. Shared names, icons, or functions
do not allocate identities, change contracts, or establish canonical meaning.

## Answer to the research question

NEXAH recurs less through a stable inventory of objects and less through one
geometry than through a reusable editorial vocabulary of transformations.
That vocabulary is real but plural. Its terms can be selected, omitted,
reordered, and combined. The corpus has not yet demonstrated the constraints
needed to call the result a recurring grammar.

## Final disposition

# RECURRING MOVE VOCABULARY

This is the strongest conclusion supported by the supplied corpus. It remains
strictly Work-level, non-canonical, and observational.
