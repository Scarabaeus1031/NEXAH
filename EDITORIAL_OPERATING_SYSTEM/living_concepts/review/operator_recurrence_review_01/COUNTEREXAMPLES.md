# Operator Recurrence Review 01 — Counterexamples

## Purpose

The shared visual system makes recurrence easy to overstate. These cases test
whether the apparent language survives missing terms, incompatible lists,
local meanings, and dependence between artifacts.

## CE1 — The palettes have different cardinalities

The corpus presents nine, ten, and eleven headline Moves, plus focused cycles
of five, six, or seven stages. A stable vocabulary does not imply a stable
alphabet size.

## CE2 — Separate disappears

`Separate` is fundamental in the Operations visual and Operator Map, but is
absent from the Living Library palette, Language of Moves headline row, and
Butterfly process. It is not universally required.

## CE3 — Flip disappears

`Flip` is explicit in the core grammar and Language of Moves but absent from
the Living Library and Butterfly process. Those explanations remain coherent
without it.

## CE4 — Project disappears from the focused relational Work

The Butterfly Operator explains a complete relational cycle without requiring
`Project`. Projection is therefore not necessary for every orientation process.

## CE5 — Zoom and Layer occur only locally

`Zoom` and `Layer` are primary Moves only in The Moves. Other visuals can
describe scale, stacks, or levels without preserving those labels. They are
local editorial candidates, not stable corpus-wide Moves.

## CE6 — Counterrotate is not the general inverse of Rotate

Language of Moves presents `Counterrotate` through balance and conservation.
Other visuals use counteraction or paired motion without naming the same Move.
The corpus does not establish a universal inverse contract.

## CE7 — Resonance is semantically broad

`Resonate` can mean frequency alignment, amplification, coherence, social
coordination, or an emergent pattern. Strong word recurrence masks substantial
variation in function and evidence.

## CE8 — Mirror and Flip conflict if treated as synonyms

Some captions associate Mirror with reflection, duality, or conjugation, while
Flip denotes front/back, parity, charge, or orientation reversal. Merging them
would erase a distinction authored by the visuals themselves.

## CE9 — Emerge alternates between Move and outcome

The Living Library palette depicts `Emerge` as a Move. Elsewhere emergence is
what happens after applying or composing other Moves. Its grammatical role is
not stable.

## CE10 — The base sequence is not preserved

Numbered palettes suggest an order, but example stacks omit Moves, reorder
them, and end differently. The Butterfly sequence introduces approach,
response, exchange, and evolution. No fixed syntax survives across examples.

## CE11 — Several visuals are editorial variants

Operator Map v2.1, The Moves, and Operations of Orientation share structure,
examples, and language. Living Library and Operator Library v1.0 also overlap.
Counting each file as independent confirmation would inflate recurrence.

## CE12 — Domain examples do not establish shared mechanisms

The corpus places Möbius forms, proteins, waves, social coordination, birds,
insects, electronics, and attention beside the same Move labels. This supports
editorial portability, not physical or mathematical identity.

## CE13 — Registry separation

The visuals call many items operators or Moves. That wording does not make
them controlled Registry Operators. In particular, Work-level `Rotate`,
`Mirror`, `Flip`, `Twist`, `Return`, `Counterrotate`, `Adapt`, and `Co-orient`
must not be promoted through recurrence alone.

## Counter-analysis result

The counterexamples reject both a universal operator language and a fixed
recurring grammar. They do not reject recurrence itself. A bounded vocabulary
of transformation and orientation functions remains visible after variant
dependence and negative evidence are accounted for.
