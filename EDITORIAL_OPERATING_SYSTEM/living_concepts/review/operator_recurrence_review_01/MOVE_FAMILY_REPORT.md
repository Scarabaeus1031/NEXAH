# Operator Recurrence Review 01 — Move Family Report

## Admission rule

A family is admitted only when at least two functionally related Moves recur
across more than one editorial family. A family groups comparable orientation
functions; it does not create a canonical taxonomy.

## F1 — Observation and attention

### Admitted members

- `Observe`
- `Sense` (adjacent, not identical)
- local forms: witness, detect, hover, focus

### Recurring function

Establish or update what is available from a situated view before another
transformation is chosen.

### Boundary

Observation is sometimes an explicit Move and sometimes the precondition for a
stack. The corpus does not show that all observation is passive or that sensing
and witnessing are equivalent.

## F2 — Distinction and relation

### Admitted members

- `Separate`
- `Connect`
- local forms: bridge, pair, interweave, exchange

### Recurring function

Create distinctions and then establish relations or passages between distinct
parts.

### Boundary

`Separate` is concentrated in core grammar variants and disappears from
several later visuals. `Connect` is considerably more persistent. The corpus
does not establish a mandatory `Separate → Connect` sequence.

## F3 — Frame and sidedness transformation

### Admitted members

- `Rotate`
- `Mirror`
- `Flip`
- local form: `Counterrotate`

### Recurring function

Change reference, handedness, face, direction, or relation between a view and
its counterpart.

### Boundary

The same words range from literal geometric operations to editorial analogies.
No common algebra, parity rule, angle, or conservation law is demonstrated
across the corpus.

## F4 — Form and continuity transformation

### Admitted members

- `Twist`
- `Fold`
- local forms: compact, contain, belt trick, Demizel/pivot

### Recurring function

Alter form, inside/outside relation, or continuity without necessarily
replacing the represented material.

### Boundary

Twist and Fold are sometimes topological metaphors and sometimes material or
biological analogies. Topological preservation is explicit only in selected
examples.

## F5 — Representation and scale

### Admitted members

- `Project`
- local forms: map, translate, zoom, layer

### Recurring function

Produce another representation, surface, dimension, chart, or scale from a
current view.

### Boundary

`Zoom` and `Layer` occur as primary Moves in only one visual. `Map` and
`Translate` often describe results or examples. They are not silently merged
with Projection.

## F6 — Resonance and alignment

### Admitted members

- `Resonate`
- adjacent language: align, amplify, coherence

### Recurring function

Bring patterns, signals, or participants into a described relation of
alignment, amplification, or coherence.

### Boundary

The corpus uses Resonance broadly across waves, social coordination, attention,
and metaphor. Recurrence of the label is strong; recurrence of one measurable
mechanism is not demonstrated.

## F7 — Return and cyclic continuity

### Admitted members

- `Return`
- adjacent language: cycle, homecoming, re-entry, release

### Recurring function

Re-enter, close, continue, or begin another cycle after transformation.

### Boundary

Return is absent from two primary palettes and appears elsewhere as a local
stage or outcome. It is recurring but optional.

## F8 — Adaptive and collective orientation

### Admitted members

- `Adapt`
- `Co-orient`
- local forms: respond, hover, flow, lock, release, remember, emerge

### Recurring function

Modify orientation through reciprocal, environmental, or collective response.

### Boundary

This family is strong inside the Living Library and Butterfly materials but is
not present in the base geometric palettes. It is a bounded later development,
not a universal Move family.

## Family-level result

The corpus supports several recurring Move families, but the families have
different reach and maturity. Observation, relation, transformation,
representation, resonance, and return recur across editorial families.
Adaptive and memory language remains more local. No visual establishes a
single mandatory grammar that orders all families.
