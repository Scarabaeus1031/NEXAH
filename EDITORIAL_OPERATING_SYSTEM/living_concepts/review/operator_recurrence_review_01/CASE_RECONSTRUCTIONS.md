# Operator Recurrence Review 01 — Case Reconstructions

Status: non-canonical observational review  
Primary evidence: supplied Work-level visuals only

The reconstructions begin with observable functions. Authored labels are added
only after the functional description. A shared icon, color, or layout is not
treated as independent recurrence.

## C1 — Orientation Operator Map v2.1

### Neutral reconstruction

The visual presents a finite palette of actions that alter attention,
distinction, relation, reference, sidedness, form, dimensional presentation,
and coherence. It also shows a five-position cycle, a surface transformation
that preserves continuity, paired counter-actions, and a sequence in which
actions may be composed.

### Explicit Move language

`Observe`, `Separate`, `Connect`, `Rotate`, `Mirror`, `Flip`, `Twist`, `Fold`,
`Project`, and `Resonate` are named as fundamental Moves. `Return` is explicit
in the Belt Trick, torus example, and five-position cycle but is not numbered
inside the ten-Move palette. `Counteraction`, `pivot`, and `drift` are local
supporting language.

### Functional assessment

- strongly present: observation, distinction, relation, frame change,
  reflection, reversal, topological change, projection, coherence;
- compositional: yes;
- fixed syntax: no;
- required sequence: no;
- character: structural and editorial, with domain analogies.

## C2 — The Moves: A Grammar of Orientation

### Neutral reconstruction

Ten actions are arranged as a stack ranging from perspective change through
transformation to emergence. Five examples show selected actions composed in
different orders. A reader procedure begins with looking, chooses an action,
applies it, notices a pattern, and integrates the result.

### Explicit Move language

The named set is `Rotate`, `Mirror`, `Flip`, `Twist`, `Fold`, `Connect`,
`Project`, `Zoom`, `Layer`, and `Resonate`. `Observe` is explicit in the reader
procedure but is not one of the numbered ten. `Align`, `Build`, `Apply`,
`Discover`, and `Integrate` occur as procedural language.

### Functional assessment

This visual adds scale change and stacking while omitting `Separate` and a
named `Return` from its primary palette. It therefore contradicts the idea of
one already-fixed ten-Move alphabet while supporting a reusable action
vocabulary.

## C3 — The Operations of Orientation

### Neutral reconstruction

The visual organizes actions into an apparent progression from holding a view,
through decomposition and relation, to geometric transformation, projection,
and pattern alignment. Examples demonstrate that actions can be composed, but
no single composition is declared mandatory.

### Explicit Move language

`Observe`, `Separate`, `Connect`, `Rotate`, `Mirror`, `Flip`, `Twist`, `Fold`,
`Project`, and `Resonate` form the numbered palette. `Return` is absent as a
named fundamental operation. `Choose`, `Apply`, `Discover`, and `Integrate`
belong to the reader procedure rather than the operation palette.

### Functional assessment

The visual closely matches C1 in palette and ordering. Its similarity is
evidence of an authored variant, not by itself an independent Work recurrence.

## C4 — The Living Library of Orientation Operators

### Neutral reconstruction

A compact action palette is followed by biological and collective metaphors.
The examples emphasize sensing, mutual adjustment, collective coordination,
propagation, memory, emergence, and return. Several short action stacks lead
to different outcomes rather than one universal endpoint.

### Explicit Move language

The palette names `Observe`, `Rotate`, `Mirror`, `Twist`, `Fold`, `Project`,
`Resonate`, `Return`, `Connect`, and `Emerge`. The living examples add
`Adapt`, `Co-orient`, `Hover`, `Flow`, `Lock`, `Release`, `Map`, and `Remember`
as local or derived language.

### Functional assessment

The core geometric vocabulary persists, but `Separate` and `Flip` disappear.
Adaptive and collective Moves become more important. This is a substantive
shift in emphasis, not merely a restyled core list.

## C5 — NEXAH Operator Library v1.0

### Neutral reconstruction

A broad palette combines distinction, relation, transformation, projection,
coherence, and cyclic continuity. Natural metaphors then instantiate selected
stacks. Each stack produces a different outcome: shared pattern, stabilization,
collective order, communication, or field mapping.

### Explicit Move language

The primary row names `Observe`, `Separate`, `Connect`, `Rotate`, `Mirror`,
`Flip`, `Twist`, `Fold`, `Project`, `Resonate`, and `Return`. Living examples
add `Pair`, `Hover`, `Flow`, `Adapt`, `Lock`, `Release`, `Sense`, `Map`,
`Remember`, and collective/emergent language.

### Functional assessment

The visual synthesizes the core grammar and living-library vocabularies. Its
width is evidence that the corpus is still editorially generative. It is not a
Registry record and cannot establish canonical Operator semantics.

## C6 — Orientation Library: The Language of Moves

### Neutral reconstruction

The visual concentrates on paired or reversible transformations, cycles,
opposition, balance, and return. It connects the same action labels to examples
from mathematics, physics, biology, architecture, electronics, art, and
attention. A long stack shows one possible journey through nine actions.

### Explicit Move language

`Rotate`, `Counterrotate`, `Flip`, `Twist`, `Fold`, `Mirror`, `Project`,
`Return`, and `Resonate` form the headline row. `Observe` occurs in the stack;
`Bridge`, `Push`, `Unlock`, `Stack`, and `Connect` occur in the LEGO example.

### Functional assessment

This visual introduces `Counterrotate` as an explicit paired action and gives
balance/conservation a central role. It omits `Separate` from the main action
language. Cross-domain examples remain analogical and do not prove identical
mechanisms.

## C7 — The Butterfly Operator

### Neutral reconstruction

Two participants sense one another, approach, orbit, respond, interweave,
exchange roles, separate, and begin again. A second sequence describes sensing,
movement, adaptation, co-orientation, emergence, evolution, and return. The
shared field and relational pattern are treated as outcomes of reciprocal
adjustment.

### Explicit Move language

The central cycle names `Approach`, `Orbit`, `Response`, `Interweave`,
`Exchange`, and `Release`. The operator map names `Sense`, `Move`, `Adapt`,
`Co-orient`, `Emerge`, `Evolve`, and `Return`. A stack additionally names
`Observe`, `Rotate`, `Twist`, `Mirror`, and `Resonate`.

### Functional assessment

This is the strongest partially independent test of the generic palette. It
reuses several core Moves, but its explanatory work depends on reciprocal
adaptation and co-orientation—functions not reducible to the base geometric
list. `Project`, `Fold`, `Flip`, and `Separate` are not required.

## Independence assessment

The corpus contains at least three editorial families:

1. **Core grammar variants:** C1, C2, and C3;
2. **Living-library syntheses:** C4 and C5;
3. **Focused or cross-domain developments:** C6 and C7.

Recurrence within a family is real authored persistence but is not equivalent
to recurrence across independent Works. Cross-family recurrence carries more
weight in the final assessment.
