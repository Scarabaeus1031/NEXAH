# Fourth-Term Representation Review 01 — Counterexamples

## Why counterexamples matter

The corpus has a strong shared visual lineage. Repetition may result from
authorship, prompt reuse, panel templates, or preferred symbols. A valid
cross-work claim must survive cases that use the same visual language but a
different representational structure.

## CE1 — Two projections, seven bridges, one observer

`Projection — Two Worlds, Many Views` cannot be reduced to three internal
registers without discarding the source's seven bridges and two-sided
architecture. It supports external observation but contradicts the proposed
internal cardinality.

## CE2 — Two loops, one thread, several transition devices

`Transition Gap & Root Thread` contains a gap, switch, thread, drift, and
return route. Choosing exactly three as internal and one as external would be
post-selection. The source's important distinction is continuity versus jump,
not a four-term decomposition.

## CE3 — Six-layer field and observer handle

`Mandelbrot Field Architecture` has an explicit observer handle but declares
six main field layers. This shows that an external-reference relation does not
imply `3+1`.

## CE4 — Move repertoires

The Operator Library visuals contain nine, ten, or more Moves, five-state
wheels, and composed stacks. Their purpose is transformation grammar, not
state cardinality. `Flip` is one move among several and is not privileged as
the universal exit operation.

## CE5 — Controlled Registry

The canonical Library Registry contains seventeen controlled Operators. It
includes Observer, Transition, Boundary, Projection, Fold, JANUS, and Scale,
but not controlled Operators named Flip, Rotate, Counterrotate, Twist, Mirror,
Return, Iota, Breachpoint, `+j`, or `-j`.

This does not invalidate the Work taxonomy. It prevents it from silently
becoming canonical semantics.

## CE6 — Thirteen recurring editorial operations

Editorial Grammar of Orientation 01 admits thirteen recurring editorial
operations and explicitly rejects a fixed grammar. Orientation Operations 01
finds recurring functions but demonstrates none as universally necessary.
This is incompatible with treating one four-term pattern as the general
architecture of orientation.

## CE7 — Existing Q(t) meaning

Current Research assigns `Q(t)` to a four-quadrant phase partition. It is not
an external point. The proposed notation `3 + Q(t)` would create semantic
collision rather than explain recurrence.

## CE8 — Orthogonal suppression in JANUS research

JANUS Building Log 02 reports a zero score for pure 90° alignment under its
current diagnostic and interprets the gates as avoiding orthogonal crossing.
This does not prove that orthogonal models are impossible. It directly
contradicts making a 90° `j` axis universal.

## CE9 — Synthetic flip construction

Historical scripts labeled `escape_vector_control` and `90_stift_flip` insert
orthogonal perturbations or flip terms into simplified trajectories. One
contains a TODO to replace its trajectory with real data. Another triggers a
state change through a coded threshold. These are demonstrations of designed
behavior, not observations that a natural fourth term exists.

## CE10 — Complex mathematics without fourth-term semantics

Parts of Research use complex planes, conjugate eigenvalue pairs, quaternions,
and rotations legitimately within scoped mathematical models. Their presence
does not connect `j` to observer position, three internal registers, Iota, or
the Operator Library. Mathematical availability is not relational evidence.

## Counter-analysis result

The corpus supports a broad family of situated representations. It does not
support a stable cardinal grammar. The counterexamples are not marginal: they
include several of the six proposed positive cases and the current canonical
Operator model.
