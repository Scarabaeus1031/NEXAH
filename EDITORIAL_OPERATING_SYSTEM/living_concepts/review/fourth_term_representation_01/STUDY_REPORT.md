# FOURTH-TERM REPRESENTATION REVIEW 01

## Objective

Determine whether NEXAH repeatedly uses three internal registers plus one
differently typed fourth term, and whether that term can be identified as
`+1`, `Q(t)`, `+j`, `-j`, an observer, or another role.

This is a completed, non-canonical editorial research review. It changes no
Registry, Operator, Living Concept, Overlay, Kernel, Architecture, OLS, or
Are.na state.

## Finding

The review does **not** find one coherent `3+1` representation geometry.

It finds a broader and better-supported recurrence:

> NEXAH repeatedly distinguishes bounded representations from the situated
> observer, reference, projection, interface, or operation through which they
> become orientable.

That relation is real within the corpus, but its cardinality is not fixed.
Reviewed examples contain:

- three nested internal roles with no separate fourth term;
- two opposed projections with an external observer and seven bridges;
- three shells with multiple gates, flows, and an observer layer;
- two loops with a thread, gap, switch, and return;
- six field layers with an observer handle;
- nine-, ten-, thirteen-, and seventeen-part operation systems.

The common structure is therefore **typed asymmetry**, not `3+1`:

```text
represented structure
        ≠
position or operation through which it is represented
```

## Model decisions

### `+1`

Rejected as the general interpretation. Candidate fourth terms are rarely
equivalent to the internal regions.

### `3 + Q(t)`

Not admitted. Current Research already defines `Q(t)` as a recursive phase
partition, not an external moving observer. The attractive notation would
hide a semantic collision.

### `3 + j` or `3 - j`

Not admitted. Historical experiments contain 90° and quadrature language, but
the bounded corpus does not define `j² = -1`, a stable sign convention,
conjugation, or an invariant connecting `±j` to the proposed fourth term.
Current JANUS research also reports suppressed pure orthogonal alignment under
one diagnostic.

### Observer / external reference

Supported as a recurring **role family**, not as one fourth component. The
observer may be central, external, between projections, distributed through a
selection process, or represented as a handle. Current controlled Operators
already preserve these distinctions through Observer, Projection,
Orientation, Relation, Boundary, and Scale.

## Operator Library assessment

The Operator Library materially strengthens the finding that NEXAH is a
language of transformations rather than things. It does not establish a
fixed fourth-term geometry.

`Flip` is relevant because it denotes orientation reversal or inside/outside
exchange. `Mirror`, `Rotate`, `Counterrotate`, `Twist`, `Fold`, `Project`, and
`Return` provide adjacent transformations. None is shown to be the universal
operation that produces an Iota event.

The proposed relation:

```text
Flip → Iota Breachpoint
```

is an intelligible editorial hypothesis, but the source chain is incomplete.
The Transition Gap visual names a structural jump without naming Flip; the
Move visuals name Flip without linking it to Iota; historical scripts create
90° flips by construction. The relation is therefore recorded as:

```text
editorial_bridge
support: insufficient
mechanism: not established
```

## What is genuinely supported

1. Bounded representations recur.
2. Observer, reference, projection, and interface roles recur.
3. Those roles are often differently typed from the represented structure.
4. Move vocabularies repeatedly describe changes of view.
5. Discontinuous transition and gap motifs occur in authored Works.
6. The same visual lineage uses several incompatible cardinal structures.
7. No reviewed source establishes a universal four-term representation law.

## What remains open

- whether a smaller, explicitly selected subset of Works defines a local
  three-register convention;
- whether a future formal model should assign a new symbol to an external
  reference without reusing `Q(t)`;
- whether a scoped complex or quaternionic realization can be defined and
  tested independently of the current visual language;
- whether readers recognize typed asymmetry without being shown the proposed
  `3+1` interpretation;
- whether Flip and structural breach form a reproducible sequence in any
  implemented system.

These are open questions, not recommendations for canonicalization.

## Final disposition

# MULTIPLE DISTINCT REPRESENTATION MODELS

NEXAH has a recurring ecology of bounded views, situated observers,
references, projections, and transformative Moves. The reviewed evidence does
not reduce that ecology to `3+1`, `3+Q(t)`, or `3⊕{+j,-j}` without discarding
source-declared differences.

The conservative result is stronger than a negative finding: it identifies
the recurring relation while preserving the plurality of its geometries.
