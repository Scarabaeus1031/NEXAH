# Orientation Process Review 01 — Neutral Case Reconstructions

The primary descriptions below avoid inherited process models and named Move
taxonomies. They use ordinary functional language and report only what is
visible or structurally implied.

## WF-A — General action grammar

### Neutral description

A field or configuration is initially available to a viewer. Beside it is a
palette of different changes that can be applied to reference, relation,
sidedness, form, scale, or presentation. Several examples use only subsets of
the palette and arrange them in different orders. A separate five-step reader
sequence begins with attending to what is present, asks for one change to be
chosen and applied, then depicts a newly noticed pattern and integration into
a larger whole. One variant includes a wheel and a return to another condition.

### Initial state

An available but not yet transformed field, object, configuration, or question.

### Elements present

- a situated viewer or viewing symbol;
- a finite palette of possible changes;
- example objects and diagrams;
- short compositions;
- outcome symbols or new configurations.

### Differences or tensions

- current versus changed reference;
- whole versus divided parts;
- one face or side versus another;
- current presentation versus another presentation;
- isolated elements versus connected structure.

### Observable changes

The viewer may distinguish, relate, turn, reflect, reverse, deform, compact,
map, rescale, stack, or align what is shown.

### Alternatives

Multiple changes are explicitly available. Examples demonstrate different
subsets and orders. The Works do not show every possible combination.

### Selection evidence

Explicit in the reader procedure: one available change is chosen. Selection
criteria are not provided beyond the question of what might reveal more.

### Continuation or endpoint

A changed view, new pattern, integration, or another point in a cycle.

### Feedback

One variant visually loops the reader procedure; another presents a cyclic
wheel. Feedback is present but not identical across all variants.

### Unresolved questions

- Is the visual order temporal, logical, instructional, or merely editorial?
- What determines the choice among available changes?
- Can any change follow any other?
- Does integration end the process or reopen it?

## WF-B — Living operator library

### Neutral description

A general palette is followed by natural and collective examples. Each example
begins with actors or a field already in some relation. They detect, adjust,
coordinate, move, communicate, retain traces, or reorganize collectively. Short
sequences use different subsets of changes and produce different results such
as a shared pattern, stabilization, communication, collective order, or a map.
A construction panel explicitly permits combining, testing, refining, and
sharing a chosen stack.

### Initial state

One or more actors situated in an environment, field, or relation.

### Elements present

- actors, groups, or distributed traces;
- an environment or shared field;
- a palette of possible changes;
- example-specific sequences;
- outcomes that differ by example.

### Differences or tensions

- individual versus group;
- actor versus environment;
- detection versus commitment;
- local rule versus collective pattern;
- fixed target versus adaptive path.

### Observable changes

Actors may detect, hover, align, exchange signals, follow traces, lock onto a
target, adjust to movement, propagate, or return.

### Alternatives

The general palette and the construction panel explicitly permit different
combinations. Individual natural examples often depict one characteristic
continuation rather than a visible branch.

### Selection evidence

Explicit at the editorial construction level. Within natural examples,
selection ranges from implied adaptation to environmental constraint; an agent
or criterion is usually unspecified.

### Continuation or endpoint

Shared pattern, stabilization, target commitment, collective order,
communication, mapping, return, refinement, or sharing.

### Feedback

Trail feedback, adaptive response, memory, return, and refinement are visible
in different examples. No single feedback mechanism spans the family.

### Unresolved questions

- Which continuations are choices and which are environmental responses?
- Does testing alter the next combination?
- Are example outcomes endpoints or temporary states?
- Is the field an input, a participant, or an effect?

## WF-C — Paired and cyclic synthesis

### Neutral description

The Work places paired directions, opposing changes, exchanges of inside and
outside, reflections, mappings, cycles, and alignment beside one another. A
five-position wheel surrounds a central viewing point. A long example chain
passes through several kinds of change before returning and aligning. Other
panels show counteraction and construction from smaller steps.

### Initial state

A configuration with a reference direction, viewpoint, or paired relation.

### Elements present

- a central viewing point;
- paired directions or counterparts;
- a five-position wheel;
- a palette of possible changes;
- one long example chain;
- cross-domain analogies.

### Differences or tensions

- direction versus counter-direction;
- inside versus outside;
- current form versus reflected or reversed form;
- action versus counteraction;
- current state versus return.

### Observable changes

Reference direction changes, counterparts are produced, sides exchange,
material is reorganized, a representation is remapped, and a cycle returns.

### Alternatives

Several changes are displayed, but the long chain presents one editorially
continued route. Alternative chains are possible only by implication.

### Selection evidence

No explicit chooser or criterion is shown for the long chain. The route may be
an example rather than a decision among alternatives.

### Continuation or endpoint

Return, alignment, balance, or another state on the five-position wheel.

### Feedback

Cyclic return is explicit. Whether the returned state modifies the next cycle
is not shown.

### Unresolved questions

- Is the wheel a temporal cycle or a structural arrangement?
- Does counteraction constrain every change or only selected examples?
- Is the long chain reversible?
- Is balance an endpoint, a condition, or an interpretation?

## WF-D — Reciprocal relational case

### Neutral description

Two participants share a field without a leader or fixed path. Each detects the
other, moves, responds, and changes in relation to the other's movement. Their
paths approach, orbit, interweave, exchange roles, separate, and begin again.
The shared pattern exists in their relation rather than in either participant
alone.

### Initial state

Two distinct participants and a shared field, with no fixed route declared.

### Elements present

- two participants;
- a co-created field;
- intertwined trajectories;
- a six-stage central cycle;
- a seven-stage explanatory sequence;
- another compositional example.

### Differences or tensions

- participant A versus participant B;
- autonomy versus mutual influence;
- approach versus release;
- local movement versus shared pattern;
- continuity versus new cycle.

### Observable changes

The participants approach, orbit, respond, adjust, interweave, exchange roles,
release, evolve, and re-enter another cycle.

### Alternatives

The Work explicitly denies a fixed path and shows many trajectories. It does
not display a finite set of discrete alternatives.

### Selection evidence

No isolated selection event or criterion is visible. Continuation emerges from
ongoing reciprocal adjustment. Any single chosen path would be analyst-added.

### Continuation or endpoint

A shared pattern, changed relation, release, or new cycle. No final closure is
claimed.

### Feedback

Strongly visible: each participant's movement changes the condition to which
the other responds. The cycle returns to a newly conditioned encounter.

### Unresolved questions

- Can any stage be isolated from the reciprocal motion?
- Is the numbered order descriptive or prescriptive?
- Does release count as termination or renewed openness?
- Where, if anywhere, could a discrete choice be located?
