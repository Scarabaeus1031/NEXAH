# Orientation Process Review 01 — Counterexamples

## CE1 — Perception always occurs first

**Contradicted as a universal claim.** Every family has an initial condition,
but WF-D depicts sensing and movement as reciprocal and ongoing. Some natural
examples begin from actors already moving in a field. The corpus does not prove
that a separable perceptual event always precedes change.

## CE2 — Orientation is always distinct

**Contradicted.** WF-D makes relational adjustment inseparable from movement.
WF-B contains examples where sensing, environmental response, and propagation
overlap.

## CE3 — Alternatives are always represented

**Contradicted.** Finite alternatives are explicit in WF-A and editorially in
WF-B. WF-C shows one example chain; WF-D shows openness without enumerated
alternatives.

## CE4 — Selection always occurs

**Contradicted.** No discrete selection event or criterion appears in WF-D.
Several natural examples can be read as environmental constraint or direct
response.

## CE5 — Action always follows orientation

**Contradicted as a universal temporal rule.** Movement and adjustment are
simultaneous in WF-D. Most WF-A and WF-C changes are representational rather
than physical actions. A visual reading order cannot prove causal precedence.

## CE6 — Every path closes

**Contradicted.** WF-D remains open after release and begins another cycle.
Several palettes leave combinations unresolved. Some stacks end, but the
corpus has no universal closure condition.

## CE7 — Return is universal

**Contradicted.** Return is prominent across several families but absent from
some headline palettes and example endpoints.

## CE8 — Every process is linear

**Contradicted.** WF-D is reciprocal and cyclic. WF-B contains feedback,
distributed coordination, and environmental coupling. WF-A branches at the
palette.

## CE9 — See → Think → Do is universal

**Rejected.** The Works do not consistently depict a thinking stage, physical
action, or a three-part temporal sequence. They show representational,
conceptual, relational, and editorial transitions with loops and coupling.

## CE10 — An intermediate layer implies consciousness

**Rejected.** Nothing in the corpus establishes subjective experience,
phenomenal awareness, or a conscious decision-maker. An intermediate visual
process can be editorial, structural, relational, or environmental.

## CE11 — Selection implies free will

**Rejected.** Depicted narrowing may be editorial choice, environmental
constraint, conditional continuation, or an unspecified transition. The
corpus provides no evidence for metaphysical freedom or moral responsibility.

## CE12 — Absence of reflection implies reflex-only behavior

**Rejected.** A Work may omit internal stages for editorial reasons. The
absence of represented comparison does not establish the absence of an
unrepresented process.

## CE13 — Repeated visual order creates a rule

**Contradicted.** Example stacks omit and reorder functions. The corpus states
no condition under which one stage must precede another.

## Counterexample result

The corpus supports recurring process tendencies and several local models. It
does not support a universal linear chain, a mandatory decision stage, or an
inference to consciousness, agency, or free will.
