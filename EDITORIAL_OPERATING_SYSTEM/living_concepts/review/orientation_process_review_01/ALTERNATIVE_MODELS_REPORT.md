# Orientation Process Review 01 — Alternative Models Report

## Model A — Direct reaction

```text
encounter → response
```

### Supporting cases

Local living-library examples depict detection followed by lock, propagation,
or collective response with no visible comparison stage.

### Contradicting cases

WF-A explicitly presents multiple available changes and a choice. WF-C inserts
a chain of transformations. WF-D depicts continuing mutual adjustment rather
than one reaction.

### Assessment

**Locally supported.** It explains selected examples, not an independent Work
family as a whole.

## Model B — Intermediate orientation

```text
encounter → intermediate reorganization → continuation
```

### Supporting cases

WF-A explicitly inserts a palette, choice, and applied change. WF-B inserts
adjustment or a constructed stack. WF-C places a transformation chain between
configuration and return/alignment. WF-D contains ongoing relational
reorganization between encounter and changed relation.

### Contradicting cases

WF-D does not permit clean separation of the middle from encounter and
movement. Local direct-response examples compress or omit it.

### Assessment

**Strongly supported as a recurring process tendency; moderately supported as
a distinct layer.** Boundaries are unstable.

## Model C — Selection process

```text
encounter → represented possibilities → selection → continuation
```

### Supporting cases

Explicit in WF-A and at the editorial construction level of WF-B. A palette in
WF-C implies alternatives.

### Contradicting cases

WF-D shows no discrete selection event, criterion, or finite alternatives.
Natural examples in WF-B may be constrained by environment or editorial
layout.

### Assessment

**Moderately supported.** Selection is real in parts of the corpus but not a
universal stage.

## Model D — Continuous coupling

```text
encounter ↔ reorganization ↔ movement/change
```

### Supporting cases

Strongly explicit in WF-D. Several adaptive, group, propagation, and trail
examples in WF-B also show ongoing coupling.

### Contradicting cases

WF-A and WF-C present more separable editorial stages. Their layout does not
prove continuous coupling.

### Assessment

**Moderately supported and strongly local.** It is the best model for WF-D and
part of WF-B, not the whole corpus.

## Model E — Cyclic process

```text
encounter → reorganization → continuation → renewed encounter
```

### Supporting cases

Explicit in WF-D; present through return/refinement in WF-B; visible in one
WF-A variant; structurally implied by the wheel and return in WF-C.

### Contradicting cases

Some example stacks stop at alignment, pattern, commitment, or integration.
Not every path visibly reopens.

### Assessment

**Moderately to strongly supported as a tendency.** No universal closure rule
exists.

## Model F — Open orientation

```text
encounter → reorganization → unresolved continuation
```

### Supporting cases

WF-D explicitly denies a fixed path and retains an open trajectory field. Some
palettes in WF-A and WF-B leave possible compositions unresolved.

### Contradicting cases

Many examples show a definite outcome or return. Openness may result from the
editorial omission of a next step.

### Assessment

**Locally supported; otherwise uncertain.**

## Coexistence finding

Multiple models coexist. WF-A most clearly supports an intermediate and
selection model; WF-B combines selection, coupling, direct response, and
feedback; WF-C supports a curated intermediate chain and cycle; WF-D supports
continuous coupling, openness, and feedback. No universal process model is
supported.
