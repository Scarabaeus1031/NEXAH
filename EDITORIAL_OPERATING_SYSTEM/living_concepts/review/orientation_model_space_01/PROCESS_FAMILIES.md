# Orientation Model Space 01 — Process Families

## Evidence boundary

This synthesis uses only the completed findings of:

- Orientation Process Review 01;
- Operator Recurrence Review 01;
- Operator Recurrence Review 02.

The names below are descriptive handles for already-supported process models.
They are not canonical terms, Operators, or architectural components.

## PF-1 — Direct response

### Description

An encounter is followed by a response without a separately visible stage of
comparison, selection, or reorganization.

### Defining property

The middle is compressed, omitted, or inseparable from the response.

### Strongest evidence

Selected Living Library examples show detection followed by locking,
propagation, or collective response.

### Weakest evidence

No complete independent Work family is explained by direct response alone.
Omission of a middle stage does not prove that no middle process exists.

### Neighboring families

- Intermediate orientation, when a compressed middle becomes visible;
- Continuous coupling, when encounter and response remain mutually active.

### Boundary

This family does not establish reflex, causality, absence of interpretation, or
a universal encounter-response rule.

## PF-2 — Intermediate orientation

### Description

An initial configuration is reorganized relationally, perspectivally,
formally, or representationally before a changed continuation appears.

### Defining property

A meaningful middle region is visible, although its internal boundaries vary.

### Strongest evidence

All four Work families in Orientation Process Review 01 contain recurrent
reorganization between an initial and a changed condition. The recurrence
reviews independently support composition of changes to view, relation, form,
representation, coherence, and continuity.

### Weakest evidence

The middle is not consistently a separate layer. Continuous coupling and local
direct-response cases dissolve or compress it.

### Neighboring families

- Selection, where alternatives are explicitly narrowed;
- Continuous coupling, where reorganization is ongoing;
- Cyclic orientation, where continuation returns to encounter;
- Open orientation, where continuation remains unresolved.

### Boundary

This family does not establish a decision layer, fixed sequence, causal order,
consciousness, or architecture.

## PF-3 — Selection

### Description

Displayed or implied alternatives are narrowed into one continuation or one
composed path.

### Defining property

Possibility and narrowing are both represented.

### Strongest evidence

Selection is explicit in the general action grammar and in the editorial build
level of the Living Library family. Both recurrence reviews support selective
composition as a Work-level meta-function.

### Weakest evidence

Selection is uncertain in the paired synthesis and absent as a discrete event
in the reciprocal relational case. Criteria are usually unspecified.

### Neighboring families

- Intermediate orientation, which may contain selection;
- Open orientation, which retains possibilities without a visible final
  narrowing;
- Direct response, which provides no explicit alternatives.

### Boundary

Selection does not establish free will, evaluation, agency, optimality, or a
mandatory stage.

## PF-4 — Continuous coupling

### Description

Encounter, sensing, movement, response, and adjustment continuously alter one
another rather than forming cleanly separated stages.

### Defining property

The process is reciprocal and its middle cannot be isolated reliably.

### Strongest evidence

The Butterfly/relational Work family explicitly depicts mutual adjustment,
shared-field change, release, and renewed encounter. Several adaptive and
collective examples provide local support.

### Weakest evidence

The general grammar and paired synthesis are presented as more separable
editorial stages and do not demonstrate continuous coupling.

### Neighboring families

- Intermediate orientation, interpreted as ongoing rather than staged;
- Cyclic orientation through repeated adjustment;
- Open orientation through non-fixed trajectories;
- Direct response at locally compressed moments.

### Boundary

This family does not establish one biological, physical, social, or control
mechanism across examples.

## PF-5 — Cyclic orientation

### Description

A changed condition continues into return, feedback, refinement, release, or
renewed encounter.

### Defining property

Continuation reopens the process rather than serving as a final endpoint.

### Strongest evidence

The reciprocal relational family contains an explicit cycle. Return,
homecoming, refinement, feedback, and renewed encounter recur across the
Living Library, paired synthesis, and one general-grammar variant. Both
recurrence reviews support cyclic continuity as recurring but optional.

### Weakest evidence

Some stacks stop at alignment, integration, commitment, or a new pattern.
Return is not present in every palette or endpoint.

### Neighboring families

- Intermediate orientation, selection, direct response, and continuous
  coupling can each participate in a cycle;
- Open orientation may reopen without returning to a prior state.

### Boundary

Cycle does not imply exact repetition, closure, periodicity, homeostasis, or a
universal return rule.

## PF-6 — Open orientation

### Description

Reorganization leads to an unresolved continuation, open trajectory field, or
remaining set of possible compositions.

### Defining property

The represented process withholds a fixed endpoint or final path.

### Strongest evidence

The reciprocal relational Work explicitly denies a fixed path and depicts an
open field of trajectories. Some palettes leave composition unresolved.

### Weakest evidence

Many examples do present definite outcomes or returns. Openness can also be an
editorial omission rather than a process property.

### Neighboring families

- Continuous coupling, where trajectories remain adaptive;
- Selection, where possibilities may later be narrowed;
- Intermediate orientation, where a changed but unresolved state follows.

### Boundary

Open orientation does not establish infinite possibility, indeterminism,
autonomy, or the absence of constraints.

## Family assessment

Intermediate orientation is the broadest cross-family tendency. Direct
response, selection, continuous coupling, cyclic orientation, and open
orientation describe different organizations or continuations of that
tendency. They are not one hierarchy and are not mutually exclusive. Their
boundaries remain descriptive and provisional.
