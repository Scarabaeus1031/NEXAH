# Orientation Model Space 01 — Model Relationship Map

## Relation vocabulary

- **overlap** — models share supported components without being equivalent;
- **overlay** — one model describes an additional topology or continuation of
  another process;
- **compression** — a supported middle is not separately represented;
- **boundary tension** — two models organize the same region differently;
- **incompatibility** — claims cannot both hold for the same case under the
  same interpretation;
- **isolation** — support is too local to establish a stronger relation.

These relations are descriptive and do not form a canonical hierarchy.

## Relationship matrix

| Model A | Model B | Relationship | Evidence-bounded interpretation |
|---|---|---|---|
| Direct response | Intermediate orientation | compression / boundary tension | A direct-response case omits or compresses the distinct middle that intermediate orientation foregrounds. Absence cannot prove nonexistence. |
| Direct response | Continuous coupling | overlap | A coupled process can contain locally immediate responses, but continuous mutual adjustment is more than one encounter-response pair. |
| Direct response | Selection | incompatibility in a single discrete event | A transition cannot simultaneously show no explicit narrowing and an explicit selection event under the same reconstruction. Different moments may still use both models. |
| Intermediate orientation | Selection | overlap; selection may be a local submodel | Selection is one possible organization of the middle, not its universal content. |
| Intermediate orientation | Continuous coupling | boundary tension and complementarity | Both support reorganization; one stages it, while the other dissolves the boundary between encounter, adjustment, and movement. |
| Intermediate orientation | Cyclic orientation | overlay | A staged or continuous middle may lead to return or renewed encounter. Cycle describes continuation topology, not the entire middle. |
| Intermediate orientation | Open orientation | overlay | A reorganized condition may remain unresolved rather than close. |
| Selection | Open orientation | tension and possible transition | Selection narrows possibilities; open orientation retains or reopens them. A process may move between these conditions, but no general transition rule is established. |
| Selection | Cyclic orientation | overlap | Different cycles may include selected paths, but selection is not required for return. |
| Continuous coupling | Cyclic orientation | strong overlap | Reciprocal adjustment can recur and renew encounter; exact periodic return is not implied. |
| Continuous coupling | Open orientation | strong local overlap | Non-fixed relational trajectories support both coupling and openness in the reciprocal Work family. Cross-corpus support is limited. |
| Cyclic orientation | Open orientation | complementary, not opposite | A cycle may reopen into changed possibilities rather than close identically. Some paths remain open without returning. |

## Nested relations

Only one bounded nesting claim is supported:

- explicit selection can occur **within** an intermediate orientation process.

The evidence does not support claiming that all direct responses, cycles,
couplings, or open processes are nested inside one master orientation model.

## Isolated or weakly connected models

Direct response and open orientation have the weakest independent reach.
Direct response is supported by selected examples rather than a full Work
family. Open orientation is strongest in one relational family and otherwise
may be an editorially unresolved endpoint.

## Incompatibilities

The map preserves four genuine incompatibilities at the claim level:

1. a universally discrete middle is incompatible with continuous coupling;
2. universal selection is incompatible with cases lacking a discrete choice;
3. universal closure is incompatible with open continuation;
4. a universally linear sequence is incompatible with reciprocal and cyclic
   processes.

These incompatibilities reject universalization. They do not require the
families themselves to be mutually exclusive across different cases or
moments.

## Relationship finding

The families form an overlapping field rather than a derivation tree.
Intermediate orientation occupies the broadest shared area, but it is neither
the origin nor the authority of the other models. Cyclic and open orientation
primarily describe continuation; selection describes one kind of narrowing;
direct response describes compression; continuous coupling describes a
different boundary regime.
