# Orientation Model Space 01 — Descriptive Model Space

## Chosen representation: topological landscape

A topological landscape best fits the evidence because the supported models:

- overlap without sharing one required sequence;
- differ partly by how they draw process boundaries;
- can coexist within one Work family or even one path;
- include both staged and continuously coupled processes;
- add return or openness as continuation topologies;
- do not form a hierarchy of derivation or authority.

A tree would overstate nesting. A linear flow would erase reciprocity and
cycles. A single-centered radial map would imply one privileged process. The
landscape therefore uses proximity and overlap, not rank.

```text
      '.         |            ↕              |        .'
        '.       ;   changed continuation    ;      .'
          '-.     \                          /   .-'
             '----\ INTERMEDIATE ORIENTATION/---'
                   '._ staged or ongoing _.'
                         \            /
                          \          /
                           '--------'
                                ↕
                       CYCLIC CONTINUATION
                  feedback / return / renewed

```

This textual plate is a proximity map, not a process diagram. Horizontal
placement contrasts compressed response, broad reorganization, and explicit
narrowing. The upper region marks processes whose boundaries dissolve or whose
continuation remains open. The lower region marks feedback, return, and renewed
encounter. A model may occupy more than one region.

## Stable shared area

The best-supported common area is not one named process but a recurring
transformational field:

- an initial or situated configuration;
- relation or encounter;
- some change of reference, counterpart, form, representation, coherence, or
  relation;
- a changed continuation;
- frequent composition of changes.

This is a stable descriptive center only in the weak cartographic sense of
maximum overlap. It is not a universal sequence, mechanism, or architectural
core.

## Model locations

### Direct response

At the compressed edge of the map. It minimizes or omits a separately visible
middle.

### Intermediate orientation

Across the broad central region. It names the recurrence of reorganization,
not a fixed decision layer.

### Selection

At the narrowing edge. It requires represented or implied alternatives and a
visible editorial or process choice.

### Continuous coupling

Across the porous upper center. It makes encounter, response, movement, and
adjustment mutually active and destabilizes stage boundaries.

### Cyclic orientation

Along the lower continuation zone. It can overlay direct, intermediate,
selection, or coupled processes when a changed condition returns, feeds back,
or renews encounter.

### Open orientation

At the unresolved upper boundary. It marks non-fixed trajectories or retained
possibility, not unlimited freedom.

## Multiple centers

The landscape has three observational centers rather than one governing
center:

1. **transformation** — the strongest cross-family recurrence;
2. **relation** — the situated context in which change occurs;
3. **continuation** — the variable result: response, selection, return,
   feedback, or openness.

Composition links these centers editorially, but it is not proven as a
universal process stage.

## Atlas status

The landscape is sufficient as a preliminary descriptive atlas of the observed
corpus. Its model boundaries and external generality remain uncertain. It
cannot yet serve as a canonical model space, formal taxonomy, or predictive
framework.
