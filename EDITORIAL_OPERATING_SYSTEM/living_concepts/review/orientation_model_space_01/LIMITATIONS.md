# Orientation Model Space 01 — Limitations

## Unsupported claims

This synthesis does not support:

- one universal orientation process;
- a mandatory `perception → orientation → selection → action` chain;
- a fixed intermediate or decision layer;
- one required beginning, endpoint, or closure condition;
- universal selection, feedback, return, or openness;
- a shared causal, physical, biological, cognitive, or computational
  mechanism;
- consciousness, agency, free will, autonomy, or optimal decision-making;
- an executable control architecture;
- a formal grammar, algebra, ontology, taxonomy, or state machine;
- equivalence between Work-level functions and Registry Operators;
- canonical status for any family or relationship in this atlas.

## Missing evidence

- independent corpora beyond the reviewed, editorially related visual Works;
- reader studies showing that the reconstructed families correspond to actual
  reading or orientation behavior;
- temporal observations distinguishing reading order from process order;
- explicit criteria for selection, evaluation, success, failure, and stopping;
- negative cases designed independently of the NEXAH visual language;
- reproducible coding by independent reviewers;
- external-domain validation of model boundaries;
- evidence that the same family remains stable when represented without the
  shared visual and editorial style.

## Alternative explanations

### Editorial layout

Apparent processes may arise from the need to arrange a visual explanation
rather than from a represented process in the subject matter.

### Shared authorship and style

Recurring functions and cycles may reflect a common editorial vocabulary.
Dependence correction reduces but cannot remove this source of recurrence.

### Omitted stages

Direct response may be a compressed explanation. Open orientation may be an
unfinished explanation. Neither absence nor openness should be overread.

### Broad analytical categories

Terms such as transformation, relation, continuation, and coherence are broad
enough to group distinct functions. Their usefulness as map coordinates does
not prove common mechanisms.

### Diagram order

Arrows and numbered stages may indicate reading order, instructional order,
logical relation, conditional relation, or metaphor. They do not automatically
establish temporal or causal sequence.

## Unstable distinctions

- staged middle versus ongoing coupling;
- response versus adjustment;
- selection versus editorial narrowing;
- feedback versus return;
- return versus release;
- open continuation versus omitted endpoint;
- action versus representational transition;
- emergence as process versus outcome.

## Future review questions

These are questions, not a roadmap or authorization:

1. Do independent, differently authored Works reproduce the same model
   landscape?
2. Can reviewers identify the families without seeing the proposed map?
3. Which observations distinguish a compressed direct response from an
   unrepresented middle?
4. When does selection become distinguishable from constraint or editorial
   choice?
5. Can continuous coupling and staged intermediate orientation be compared
   without treating one as a deficient version of the other?
6. What evidence distinguishes return, feedback, renewed encounter, and exact
   cycle?
7. Does open orientation remain identifiable when an artifact has an explicit
   stopping boundary?
8. Are relation, transformation, and continuation sufficiently precise to
   remain useful across new corpora?

## Authority boundary

These limitations preserve the atlas as a non-canonical descriptive synthesis.
They authorize no Registry entry, Operator, Meta-function, Living Concept, OLS
change, Kernel behavior, Architecture, implementation guidance, scientific
claim, or Are.na modification.
