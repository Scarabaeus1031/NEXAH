# ORIENTATION MODEL SPACE 01
## Mapping the Landscape of Orientation Processes

## Objective

Construct the smallest descriptive map capable of containing the process
models already supported by previous Orientation Process and Operator
Recurrence Reviews, without searching for one universal process.

## Method

The study synthesized completed findings only. It did not reopen Work visuals,
collect evidence, extend recurrence claims, or reinterpret the source corpus.
Model names were retained as descriptive handles. Relationships were admitted
only where the prior reviews already supported overlap, tension, recurrence,
or counterexample.

## Evidence

- Orientation Process Review 01;
- Operator Recurrence Review 01;
- Operator Recurrence Review 02.

The two recurrence reviews concern the same underlying visual corpus and are
not counted as independent empirical datasets. Review 02 provides a
function-first replication and qualification of Review 01. The process review
supplies the admitted alternative models and their boundaries.

## Process families

Six descriptive families are retained:

1. **Direct response** — locally compressed encounter-response transitions;
2. **Intermediate orientation** — recurrent reorganization between initial and
   changed conditions;
3. **Selection** — represented possibilities narrowed into a continuation;
4. **Continuous coupling** — reciprocal adjustment without stable stage
   boundaries;
5. **Cyclic orientation** — feedback, return, refinement, or renewed encounter;
6. **Open orientation** — unresolved continuation or non-fixed trajectories.

Intermediate orientation has the broadest reach. It is supported strongly as a
tendency and only moderately as a distinct layer. Direct response and open
orientation remain the most local families.

## Shared structures

The strongest recurring components are:

- an identifiable initial configuration;
- situated encounter or attention;
- relation;
- transformation or reorganization;
- a changed continuation;
- composition of transformations.

Possibility, comparison, selection, feedback, return, and renewed encounter
are moderately supported. Direct response, reciprocal adaptation, openness,
and trace/memory are local. Evaluation, shared action, closure, and emergence
as a process remain uncertain.

## Relationship map

The families form an overlapping topological landscape rather than a tree:

- direct response compresses or omits a visible middle;
- selection occupies one bounded part of intermediate orientation;
- continuous coupling supports reorganization while dissolving its boundaries;
- cyclic orientation overlays processes that return, feed back, or renew
  encounter;
- open orientation overlays processes whose continuation remains unresolved;
- selection and openness create a genuine tension but can occur at different
  moments;
- cycle and openness are compatible when return reopens changed possibilities.

## Stable findings

- multiple process families are supported within the observed corpus;
- transformation recurs more strongly than stable objects or one geometry;
- relation and situated attention recur across independent Work families;
- composition is a strong editorial meta-function;
- intermediate reorganization is a recurring tendency;
- cyclic continuity and feedback recur without being mandatory;
- no family requires one fixed alphabet or sequence.

## Unstable findings

- the boundary of an intermediate process;
- whether an omitted middle indicates direct response or editorial compression;
- selection criteria and the distinction between selection and constraint;
- the boundary between feedback, release, return, and renewed encounter;
- whether openness is a process property or an omitted endpoint;
- whether action is representational, relational, or physical;
- external generality beyond the authored corpus.

## Counterexamples

The atlas preserves the counterexamples that prevent universalization:

- sensing and movement can be reciprocal rather than sequential;
- alternatives are not always enumerated;
- selection is absent as a discrete event in one complete family;
- several examples remain coherent without Project, Return, Separate, or Flip;
- some paths stop without visible return;
- processes can branch, overlap, loop, or remain open;
- recurring labels carry different functions and mechanisms;
- repeated visual order does not create a rule.

## Limitations

The evidence comes from a small, editorially related visual corpus. No new
corpus, independent reader behavior, temporal process data, external validation,
or executable implementation was examined. The atlas maps supported
descriptions; it does not validate mechanisms.

## Boundary conditions

Every family remains Work-level, non-canonical, optional, and bounded by its
support. The map must not be read as:

- hierarchy;
- causal flowchart;
- universal sequence;
- cognitive architecture;
- control model;
- ontology;
- scientific theory;
- canonical Operator system.

## Atlas interpretation

The smallest fitting representation is a topological landscape with a broad
transformational overlap and variable boundary regimes. Relation,
transformation, and continuation form three observational centers. Direct
response, selection, coupling, cycle, and openness describe different
organizations of that landscape rather than descendants of one master model.

This is sufficient for a preliminary atlas: the current findings can be
located relative to one another without collapsing their differences. It is
not sufficient for a stable or general model space because the boundaries are
still corpus-dependent and independently untested.

## Future reviews

Open questions concern independent replication, reviewer agreement, temporal
versus editorial order, selection criteria, stopping conditions, and the
distinction between feedback, return, and openness. They are recorded as
uncertainties, not proposed work or architectural commitments.

## Authority boundary

The atlas is descriptive only. It creates no Registry entries, Operators,
Meta-functions, Living Concepts, Architecture, canonical hierarchy,
implementation guidance, scientific validation, OLS changes, Kernel changes,
Overlay changes, or Are.na changes.

## Answer

The smallest currently supportable model space is an overlapping landscape of
six recurring process families organized around relation, transformation, and
changed continuation. No universal process is supported. The families are
sufficiently recurrent to map, but the stability and external validity of the
model space remain uncertain.

## Disposition

**preliminary atlas established**

Universal process status: **universal process not supported**

Recurring family status: **recurring process families supported**

Model-space status: **model space remains uncertain**
