# Plate — Relation Map

**Phase:** X3 · review model  
**Status:** explanatory synthesis · no graph relations created

## Working relation

The evidence supports a possible editorial pattern:

```text
Larger field or landscape
        │
        ├── situated by Observer + Context + Reference Frame
        ├── selected through Aperture
        ├── delimited by Boundary
        ├── transformed through Projection
        └── scoped by Scale
                    │
                    ▼
                 PLATE
        bounded human-facing representation
                    │
        ├── comparison
        ├── reflection
        ├── explanation
        └── navigation
                    │
                    ▼
          another view or Transition
```

This is a review diagram, not a formula, ontology, or Registry relation. The
five controlled Operators retain their existing definitions and identities.

## Distinctions that must remain visible

| Related term | Existing role | Possible relation to Plate | Why they are not synonyms |
|---|---|---|---|
| Field | larger relational or representational domain | a Plate may show part of a field | the field is not exhausted by one presentation |
| Observer | situated act of selection | helps determine a Plate's viewpoint | the Observer is an operation/role, not the resulting representation |
| Aperture | selective opening or frame | determines what can enter the view | the opening is not the presented unit |
| Boundary | distinguishes regions or states | gives a Plate its declared or implied limit | a boundary can exist without a Plate |
| Projection | maps between frames or representations | can produce the content of a Plate | projection is a transformation with possible loss |
| Scale | observational or structural level | scopes what a Plate can show | scale is a condition, not a document form |
| Reference Frame | coordinates or situates observation | makes a Plate interpretable | it is typed context, not the visual surface |
| Representation | any declared form used by the system | Plate may be one human-facing kind | not every representation is editorial or visual |
| Map | organized relational representation for orientation | a map may occupy a Plate | a Plate may also contain a diagram, comparison, narrative, or question |
| Page | documentary carrier or sequence unit | a page may contain one or more Plates | page is pagination; Plate is a possible explanatory function |
| Atlas | ordered collection of maps or visual studies | an Atlas may organize Plates | the whole collection is not one Plate |
| Concept | evolving reviewed idea with occurrences and evidence | a Plate may present a Concept | the presentation is not the idea |
| Answer Contract | governed mapping from question to reviewed answer and evidence | can specify what an explanation must preserve | the contract is not its reader-facing rendition |
| Transition | relation or passage between represented states | Plates may make transitions comparable or lead to a next view | transition is not a presentation unit |

## Three levels

The strongest synthesis connects three levels without merging them:

```text
Geometry
declares a view, relation, projection, or cross-section
        ↓
Plate pattern
bounds and presents that view for a human reader
        ↓
Editorial explanation
places the view in a sequence with evidence, limits, and a next step
```

Plate could therefore name a bridge between formal representation and human
presentation. It would not replace either layer.

## Recurring sequence hypothesis

The proposed rhythm

```text
Wonder → Story → Plate → Reflection → Transition → Next Plate
```

is plausible in NEXAH's visual practice but is not yet a repository-wide
contract. The current evidence establishes ordered and interconnected Plates,
chapter compression, layers of transformation, and multiple perspectives. It
does not establish one mandatory narrative sequence across all Works.

## Relation finding

Plate is most coherent when treated as the **resulting bounded editorial
view**, not as the field, the observer, the selection, the projection, or the
transition. This placement avoids conflict with the existing vocabulary while
explaining why the same visual rhythm appears across Books, Atlases,
Whiteboards, Geometry reviews, and explanation artifacts.
