## Answer

`Plate` is not merely a new metaphor. It already exists in NEXAH's Library
Architecture and has a recurring editorial function across several Works.

The strongest evidence describes a Plate as:

- a layer within a transformation;
- a compressed and navigable chapter view;
- an alternative projection of a larger landscape;
- one perspective on a shared question;
- a connected unit within an Atlas.

This supports **Plate as an editorial pattern and visual grammar**. It also
supports Plate as an explanatory metaphor for partial, situated
representation.

It does not support Plate as a canonical Concept, Operator, Orientation
Primitive, or universal form of every representation.

## Architectural meaning

The useful distinction is:

```text
Observer + Aperture + Boundary + Projection + Scale
                         ↓
              bounded editorial view
                         ↓
               reflection / next step
```

Plate may name the middle presentation without replacing any of the existing
Operators or technical representations.

## Decision

Recommend a small, bounded continuation of Phase X3:

1. select a limited sample of explicitly named Plates from three or four
   Works;
2. test whether editors can distinguish Plate from page, image, map, and
   panel;
3. record larger field, viewpoint, boundary, projection or selection, scale,
   purpose, and next relation;
4. test whether this makes partiality and navigation clearer for readers.

Only if that test succeeds should a later review consider a non-canonical
Plate grammar. Concept acceptance remains out of scope.
