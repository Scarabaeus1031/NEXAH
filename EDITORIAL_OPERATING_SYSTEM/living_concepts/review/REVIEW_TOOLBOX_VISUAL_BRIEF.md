# NEXAH Review Toolbox — Visual Brief

## Working title

**NEXAH REVIEW TOOLBOX**

*A Growing Instrument for Orientation Research*

## Purpose

Show how the repository's review capability has expanded from recurrence
detection to representation review while preserving the independence and
authority boundary of every instrument.

The image is an overview of review functions. It is not a Territory map, an
ontology, a causal law, a maturity ladder, a canonical architecture, or a
claim that every review must follow one sequence.

## Primary composition

Use a calm, horizontal research workbench or opened instrument case. Present
five equally weighted instruments. Avoid rising steps, pyramids, nested layers,
or a central throne-like object.

```text
OBSERVE THE WORK

[ RECURRENCE REVIEW ]
What repeats?
        ⋯
[ INDEPENDENCE REVIEW ]
Does it reappear neutrally?
        ⋯
[ PROCESS REVIEW ]
How does change unfold?
        ⋯
[ MODEL-SPACE SYNTHESIS ]
Which models coexist?
        ⋯
[ REPRESENTATION REVIEW ]
Which map fits the evidence?
```

Use small, non-directional separators or a fine review thread between the
instruments. If arrows are used, label them **question development**, not
progress, authority, or causality.

## Instrument cards

### 1. Recurrence Review

- Function: recurrence detection
- Question: What repeats across independent Works?
- Current result: recurring vocabulary; no grammar

### 2. Independence Review

- Function: function-first replication
- Question: Does the result reappear without prior names?
- Current result: confirms Review 01 with qualifications

### 3. Process Review

- Function: process reconstruction
- Question: How does change unfold across the Works?
- Current result: multiple models coexist

### 4. Model-Space Synthesis

- Function: bounded synthesis
- Question: Which models overlap, diverge, or remain open?
- Current result: preliminary atlas; universal process not supported

### 5. Representation Review

- Function: representation comparison
- Question: Which map preserves the reviewed relations and limits?
- Current result: territory moderately supported; multiple styles remain

## Shared safeguard rail

Place one continuous, visually secondary rail below all five instruments:

```text
NEUTRAL RECONSTRUCTION · DEPENDENCE CORRECTION · SUPPORT CLASSES
COUNTEREXAMPLES · DELAYED COMPARISON · AUTHORITY BOUNDARIES
```

The rail supports every instrument but does not imply that the instruments
share evidence or conclusions.

## Territory-review inset

If the latest review result is shown, use a small three-case evidence inset:

- two independent territorial reconstruction families;
- one non-territorial layered/matrix hybrid family;
- four boards in total, with two Solar–Lunar–Carbon boards explicitly marked
  as two publication layers of one reviewed space.

Do not turn every case into an island. Do not count the Museum Edition as an
independent replication. Caption the inset:

> Territory is moderately supported. Multiple representation styles remain.

## Visual language

- quiet research-publication aesthetic;
- white or warm neutral background;
- black typography with restrained blue, green, violet, and ochre accents;
- precise, simple instrument icons;
- equal visual weight across all five cards;
- ample breathing space;
- no glowing science-fiction effects;
- no database, neural-network, or universal-tree imagery;
- no implication that the final card supersedes the earlier cards.

## Required authority statement

Place this sentence in the lower field:

> NEXAH does not add authority when it adds an instrument.

Optional secondary line:

> The toolbox grows by testing its own tools.

## Exclusions

The visual must not depict:

- a canonical Operator sequence;
- one universal orientation process;
- a hierarchy of evidence authority;
- review stages as increasing truth;
- Territory as the required shape of future atlases;
- Registry, Kernel, OLS, or Architecture changes;
- completed visual validation or independent reader validation.
