## Hard boundaries

Hard boundaries dominate Planetary Orientation because the comparison objects
must remain distinct: element inventory is not molecular species, atmosphere,
process, or interpretation. Here separation prevents category collapse.

The territorial boards also use visible boundaries, but their regions are not
sealed containers. A boundary identifies a change in support, function, or
question rather than an absolute division.

## Gradients and overlap

Gradients are strongest in the Museum Edition and function as a translation of
uncertainty and distance. Explicit overlap is strongest in Orientation Model
Space. The devices are related only at the descriptive level: both resist
binary membership, but neither establishes a metric or topology.

## Frontiers

The Solar–Lunar–Carbon maps make frontier visible by placing open questions at
the edge of admitted relations. Orientation Model Space preserves unsettled
boundaries between selection and constraint, feedback and return, and process
versus editorial compression. Planetary Orientation instead records limits in
tables and prose.

## Evidence-dependent boundaries

The clearest evidence-dependent boundary is the separation of the lunar region
from biological and carbon shortcuts. Missing lines are meaningful: Moon to
Photosynthesis, Moon to Calvin Cycle, Moon to Carbon Cycle, and Tides to Carbon
Cycle are not admitted.

In Orientation Model Space, boundary confidence follows recurrence and
counterexamples from prior reviews. This remains corpus-dependent and is not a
formal confidence surface.

## Unknown space

Unknown space is actively mapped in two independent territorial
reconstructions, but not in every atlas form. It appears as:

- an explicit Open Questions or Unknown region;
- a sparse, weakly connected branch;
- unresolved overlap between process families;
- stated instability of region boundaries.

Unknown space is therefore a recurring territorial practice, not yet a
universal atlas requirement.

## Boundary condition

All boundary descriptions are visual and editorial. None proves a scientific
boundary, geometric manifold, basin, field discontinuity, or natural region.
