# Visual Language Report

## Question

Do the completed boards exhibit a recurring cartographic language, rather than
merely sharing an editorial style?

## Recurring candidates

### Proximity over hierarchy

Solar–Lunar–Carbon and Orientation Model Space locate related regions near one
another without assigning one universal root. Planetary Orientation is more
ordered and therefore limits the recurrence of this feature.

### Regions plus pathways

The territorial boards combine areas with routes or relations. Neither is a
pure geographic picture. Planetary Orientation also uses pathways, but across
layers rather than territories.

### Boundaries as information

All three independent reconstruction families preserve distinctions. The
visual form varies: columns, regional edges, sparse bridges, overlap, or
frontier. This supports a recurring concern with boundaries more strongly than
one recurring boundary graphic.

### Unequal evidence

The Solar–Lunar–Carbon maps visibly weaken the lunar branch and distinguish
supported, indirect, weak, and open relations. Orientation Model Space varies
the stability of its families. Planetary Orientation relies more heavily on
textual qualification. Evidence weighting recurs, but not in one uniform
visual syntax.

### Preserved unknowns

Unknown or unstable areas are prominent in the two territorial reconstruction
families. They are recorded but not territorialized in Planetary Orientation.

## Stylistic features

The following are stylistic or locally editorial and must not be treated as a
method:

- continent and island metaphors;
- museum-atlas typography;
- dark versus light palette;
- atmospheric gradients;
- astronomical imagery;
- one particular color assigned to one region;
- cartographic texture or decorative geography.

## Methodological candidates

The following may be methodological tendencies because they recur across
different forms:

- preserve distinctions between comparison objects;
- show admitted routes and meaningful non-routes;
- keep uncertainty and unresolved material visible;
- avoid forcing one hierarchy or one causal sequence;
- use spatial organization to expose asymmetry;
- pair visual translation with textual evidence and boundary records.

These tendencies are supported descriptively. They are not independently
validated as a visual method.

## Dependence correction

The Evidence Map and Museum Edition cannot be counted twice. Their difference
shows that one reviewed space can support both an analytical and a public
cartographic rendering. It does not demonstrate recurrence across independent
research.

## Cartographic-language disposition

**recurring cartographic language uncertain**

Two independent territorial reconstructions share region, proximity,
boundary, asymmetry, and unknown-space behavior. A third completed atlas uses
a different layered/matrix language, and all work remains editorially related.
The evidence is enough to identify a candidate language, but not a standard or
an obligatory future form.
