# Territory Characteristics

## Scope

Territorial characteristics are reported only for the Solar–Lunar–Carbon and
Orientation Model Space reconstruction families. The Museum Edition is a
dependent rendering of the former. Planetary Orientation supplies the main
counterexample.

## Strongly supported

### Identifiable cores

Both territorial reconstruction families contain recognizable centers of
local coherence. Solar–biological transformation forms the strongest route in
the Solar–Lunar–Carbon space. Relation, transformation, and continuation form
the observational center of Orientation Model Space.

### Neighboring regions

Regions gain meaning partly through adjacency. Solar, biological, carbon, and
planetary regions are placed as neighbors without being made identical.
Process families in Orientation Model Space overlap or sit in tension rather
than descend from one parent.

### Preserved unresolved area

Unknown or unstable material is represented rather than erased. The
Solar–Lunar–Carbon maps expose open questions and an intentionally sparse lunar
branch. Orientation Model Space records uncertainty about boundaries,
selection, feedback, return, and openness.

## Moderately supported

### Bounded regions

Both families distinguish regions, but their boundaries are editorial and
evidence-dependent. They are not formal partitions.

### Overlap and transition zones

Orientation Model Space depicts explicit overlap. Solar–Lunar–Carbon uses
bridges and boundary crossings between regions. These are comparable
functions, but not identical visual devices.

### Internal coherence

Regions contain relations that fit locally: a supported biological-energy
route, a planetary comparison context, or a process family. Internal coherence
does not imply one mechanism, closure, or complete coverage.

### Asymmetry

The lunar region has fewer admitted routes than the solar–biological region.
Direct response and open orientation are more local than the broad
intermediate-orientation region. Unequal density is treated as information.

## Local

- Continents, islands, coast-like edges, and unexplored terrain occur most
  clearly in the Solar–Lunar–Carbon Museum Edition.
- A shared central overlap occurs explicitly in Orientation Model Space.
- Evidence-weighted line types are most developed in the
  Solar–Lunar–Carbon Evidence Map.
- Gradual geographic fading is a publication choice of the Museum Edition,
  not a corpus-wide property.

## Uncertain

- Whether the represented regions remain stable under independent
  reconstruction;
- whether spatial proximity is interpreted consistently by readers;
- whether boundaries follow evidence alone or also editorial composition;
- whether the same corpus would yield the same territories under another
  reviewer;
- whether territorial organization generalizes beyond the authored atlas
  corpus;
- whether local coherence deserves the term *stability region*.

## Stability-area caution

The regions can be described as locally coherent, transitional, overlapping,
weakly connected, or unresolved. They cannot be treated as basins of
attraction, dynamical equilibria, or mathematically stable sets. No such
properties were established by these boards.

## Counterexample

Planetary Orientation preserves distinctions more effectively through layers,
matrices, and qualified cross-band links. Converting it into islands would
hide the ordered comparison objects and could reintroduce the linear causal
reading that its review rejects. This shows that territorial form is
conditional, not mandatory.
