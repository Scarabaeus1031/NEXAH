# ORIENTATION TERRITORIES REVIEW 01
## Do Orientation Reconstructions Naturally Form Territories?

## Objective

Determine whether completed independent NEXAH atlas reconstructions repeatedly
organize relational knowledge into bounded regions rather than exclusively
into trees, isolated nodes, hierarchies, or linear sequences.

## Method

The study inspected completed descriptive atlas boards only. It classified
their dominant representations, compared boundary behavior and unknown space,
tested alternative representations, and corrected for editorial dependence.
It performed no new reconstruction and collected no new corpus evidence.

## Evidence

Four boards were reviewed:

1. Planetary Orientation, the source board reviewed by Planetary Chemistry
   Atlas 01;
2. Solar–Lunar–Carbon Evidence Map;
3. Solar–Lunar–Carbon Museum Edition;
4. Orientation Model Space 01.

The two Solar–Lunar–Carbon boards are publication layers of one reviewed space,
not independent replications. The effective evidence base is therefore three
independent reconstruction families. Unfinished atlas proposals and the
Photosynthesis Neighborhood were excluded.

## Representation findings

Two independent families are territory-dominant. Solar–Lunar–Carbon uses
regions, a sparse lunar branch, pathways, and explicit unknown territory.
Orientation Model Space uses overlapping process regions with unstable and
local boundaries. Planetary Orientation is instead dominated by layered
architecture, matrices, and directed comparison. All boards remain hybrids.

## Territory findings

The recurring territorial features are identifiable local cores, neighboring
regions, unequal connectivity, bounded but non-absolute areas, and preserved
unresolved material. Overlap and transition recur, but use different visual
devices. Continents and islands are local publication metaphors rather than
stable methodological terms.

## Boundary findings

Boundaries range from hard categorical panels to porous overlap, weak bridges,
gradients, and frontiers. They are evidence-dependent or editorially assigned.
No board establishes mathematical topology, a natural scientific boundary, or
a basin of attraction.

## Unknown-region findings

Unknown space is part of both territorial reconstruction families. It appears
as an explicit Unknown or Open Questions region, a sparse branch, or unstable
process-family boundaries. Planetary Orientation records uncertainty in prose
and tables rather than spatial territory. Unknown-region preservation is thus
recurring but not universal.

## Alternative representations

Graphs could preserve explicit relations; tables could preserve evidence
classes; sequences could preserve the strongest directional route; trees and
hierarchies could preserve nesting. None alone preserves all overlap,
asymmetry, frontier, and meaningful silence found in the territorial boards.
Conversely, territory would weaken the staged category discipline of Planetary
Orientation. No universal style is warranted.

## Stable observations

- More than one independent atlas reconstruction uses bounded relational
  regions.
- Multiple representation styles coexist.
- Boundaries and non-connections carry information across all reviewed
  families, though not through one graphic language.
- Territorial boards preserve uncertainty and asymmetry spatially.
- The Museum Edition is derivative evidence, not a replication.

## Unstable observations

- exact territory boundaries;
- objective meaning of spatial distance;
- independent reviewer agreement;
- persistence of regions under another reconstruction;
- external generality beyond the authored corpus;
- whether readers interpret unknown territory as intended;
- whether territory improves orientation relative to a graph or table.

## Limitations

The corpus is small and editorially related. Only four boards and three
independent reconstruction families were available. One territorial family has
two publication layers, which may make the pattern appear visually stronger
than its independent evidence warrants. No reader study, independent
reconstruction, quantitative spatial model, or external validation was used.

## Comparison with previous atlas works

Planetary Chemistry established that layered comparison is preferable to a
false linear chemistry-to-property theory. Solar–Lunar–Carbon established a
multi-system orientation space with a strong biological spine, incomplete
planetary extensions, and an intentionally weak lunar branch. Orientation
Model Space established a preliminary overlapping landscape of process
families while rejecting a universal process. Together they show recurrence
of territory and a real non-territorial counterexample.

## Cartographic-language test

Bounded regions, proximity, pathways, unequal support, boundaries, and
preserved unknowns recur in two independent territorial reconstructions.
Planetary Orientation uses a different spatial grammar, and all artifacts
share editorial ancestry. Therefore the candidate cartographic language is
visible but not independently stable.

**Cartographic-language disposition: recurring cartographic language uncertain**

## Territory hypothesis

Hypothesis: independent NEXAH atlas reconstructions tend to organize
relational knowledge into bounded territories rather than exclusively into
trees, hierarchies, or isolated node networks.

Two of three independent reconstruction families support the tendency. The
third is a counterexample that uses layers and matrices for principled reasons.
The hypothesis is therefore **moderately supported**.

## Boundary conditions

Territory remains a descriptive representation. It creates no named Concept,
formal method, mathematical region, design standard, Architecture component,
or requirement for future boards. Territory may represent a broader
methodological tendency, but this review does not establish one. Future atlas
boards remain free to use trees, graphs, layers, matrices, sequences, hybrids,
or other fitting forms.

## Answer

Yes, bounded relational territories recur naturally in the completed atlas
corpus, especially where overlap, asymmetry, frontier, and unknown space must
remain visible. They do not replace other representations. The current
evidence supports a moderate tendency, not a universal cartographic law.

## Final disposition

**territorial representation moderately supported**

Cartographic language: **recurring cartographic language uncertain**

Territory status: **territory may represent a broader methodological tendency**

Territory-hypothesis result: **moderately supported**
