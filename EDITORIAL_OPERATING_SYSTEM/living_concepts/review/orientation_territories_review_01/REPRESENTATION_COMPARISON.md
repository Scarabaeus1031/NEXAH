# Representation Comparison

## Comparison rule

No representation is treated as intrinsically superior. The question is which
form preserves the relations and limits already reconstructed.

| Representation | What it preserves well | What it can obscure | Fit to reviewed boards |
|---|---|---|---|
| Territory | proximity, region, frontier, asymmetry, unknown space, porous boundaries | exact relation semantics, order, formal distance | strong fit for Solar–Lunar–Carbon and Orientation Model Space |
| Tree | inheritance, nesting, one-to-many branching | overlap, reciprocity, multiple membership, unresolved adjacency | poor fit to the two territorial reconstructions |
| Graph / node network | explicit nodes and edges, direction, relation type | broad regions, silence, frontier, qualitative context | important secondary form in the Evidence Map; insufficient alone |
| Hierarchy | level, authority, containment | lateral movement, overlap, alternative paths | useful for some repository structures, not these atlas findings |
| Matrix | systematic pairwise or cross-category comparison | spatial neighborhood, open territory, movement | strong fit to Planetary Chemistry comparisons |
| Sequence / flow | order, prerequisite, directional continuation | alternatives, feedback, weak branches, co-presence | useful for the strong solar-biological spine and planetary layers, but not the whole spaces |
| Layered architecture | distinct comparison objects and controlled crossings | porous regions and geographic exploration | dominant fit for Planetary Orientation |
| Hybrid | simultaneous use of regions, lines, matrices, and paths | can increase cognitive load and blur visual conventions | all reviewed boards are hybrid to some degree |

## Why territory fits two reconstructions

The two territorial reconstruction families contain overlapping membership,
unequal support, neighboring questions, unresolved edges, and meaningful
non-connections. A tree would force parentage; a sequence would force order; a
pure graph would make regions and unknown space secondary. Territory keeps
these qualitative differences visible.

## Why territory does not dominate Planetary Orientation

Planetary Chemistry depends on disciplined separation of element, molecule,
atmospheric mixture, process, and interpretation. Its main problem is category
control across a staged comparison. Layers and matrices expose that structure
more directly than a landscape. A territorial rewrite could make proximity
look like causal or chemical equivalence.

## Could the territorial boards be graphs?

Yes, partially. Their admitted relations can be encoded as nodes and edges.
But the resulting graph would not automatically preserve:

- intentionally sparse regions;
- qualitative frontier;
- unknown territory;
- broad overlap;
- the visual difference between an isolated branch and a dense local region.

The graph would be an alternative representation, not an equivalent reading.

## Could they be tables?

Tables could preserve evidence class, boundary statements, and source status
more precisely. They would weaken exploratory adjacency and make open space
look like missing data rather than a represented result. The most disciplined
publication may therefore require both a cartographic view and an inspectable
register, as the Solar–Lunar–Carbon package already demonstrates.

## Conclusion

Multiple representation styles coexist because the reconstructed relations
differ. Territory is a recurring fit for relational spaces with overlap,
frontier, and unknowns. It is not a universal replacement for matrices,
graphs, layers, or sequences.
